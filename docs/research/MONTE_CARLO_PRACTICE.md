# What the real world does in Monte Carlo and wind-uncertainty systems, and what we should copy (research of 2026-11-08)

Question from the user: look at what is done in real Monte Carlo systems (launch vehicles, rocketry codes, weather ensembles, wind energy) and find ideas to make our system more accurate. Method: web research (sources below) compared with what the tool does now, then a ranked list of items. Some items were tested immediately; their results are marked. Source summaries come from search results; where a source was only seen as a summary it is said so.

## 2. Test run immediately: M6, measure-correlate-predict with a short on-site record
`scripts/mcp_test.py`: the "site" is the USCRN basin station (1.5 m wind), the reference is ERA5 10 m. A campaign of 1, 2, 3 or 6 months (started in each calendar month of 2021 or 2022) calibrates a correction that is applied to the rest of the same year.
- No correction: MAE 0.97-1.02 m/s, bias +0.63..+0.72.
- One scale factor (ratio of means), 1-month campaign: MAE 0.77 (**-21 %**), bias about 0, but the 90th-percentile speed is 0.37-0.41 m/s too low.
- **Hour-of-day scale factors: MAE 0.73 (1 month), 0.71 (3 months), 0.70 (6 months), i.e. -25..-28 %.**
- Quantile (Weibull-type) mapping: MAE 0.76-0.80 (no better than the scale on the error) but the 90th-percentile error is only -0.03 (against -0.40 for the scale): best for the tail.
- The season of the campaign matters less here than the method (best/worst start month differ by 0.05-0.2 m/s).
Reading: **a single month of pad anemometer data, used with the hour-of-day correction, removes about a quarter of the ground-wind error of the reanalysis**; more months help little. Built as `fit_site` / `apply_site` (methods ratio, hourly, quantile; 2 tests). Caveats: one station, 1.5 m, speed only; it needs the user's pad record.
