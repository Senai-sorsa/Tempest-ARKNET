Sixty-second executive summary

Tempest-ARKNET is a hybrid physics-and-AI atmospheric profile generator engineered for high-power rocket flight simulation and launch-day dispersion analysis. Rather than feeding a 6-DOF trajectory simulator a single idealized atmosphere or one deterministic weather forecast, it generates calibrated ensembles of physically coherent vertical air columns from the launch rail to 86 km—and serves them at the pad with pure NumPy and zero GPU dependencies.

1 · In Plain English & Analogy Start Here

400 Realistic Vertical Skies Instead of One Textbook Guess

Planning a rocket flight with a single textbook atmosphere is like planning a cross-country road trip using only the national average speed limit—it ignores headwinds, mountain passes, afternoon heat, and sudden gusts. Tempest-ARKNET replaces that single static guess with an ensemble of 400 physically coherent vertical sky columns tailored to your exact launch pad, altitude, time of day, and forecast lead time.

Every generated sky moves together smoothly from the launch rail to 18 km (and blends cleanly into the upper atmosphere up to 86 km): wind speed and direction shift realistically across layers, temperature cools or forms morning inversions naturally, and air density matches the exact weight of the air column overhead. On launch morning, you can nudge those 400 skies to agree with live airport weather reports, pad anemometers, and nearby Doppler radar—without needing a GPU or PyTorch installed.

2 · Technical Summary Commit d081e1c

Hybrid Engression + Hydrostatic Physics + Zero-PyTorch Serving

Offline, Tempest-ARKNET ingests ERA5 reanalysis, IGRA2 weather-balloon soundings, ASOS surface gusts, and climate indices onto a canonical 44-level AGL (10 m to 18 km) grid. A 4-member conditional Engression neural network (~2.8M parameters per member) trains directly on proper scoring rules (almost-fair CRPS + Energy Score + tail shear + low-altitude vector wind) in vertical-difference and log dewpoint-depression coordinates, then calibrates against balloons with quantile spread maps, a 2-degree-block Schaake shuffle, shear/lapse maps, a vertical-wind w-head, and a heteroscedastic Gumbel gust law.

For production, tempest/export_tool.py packs all weights and tables into a standalone pure-NumPy bundle guarded by a SHA-256 cryptographic fingerprint in manifest.json (fail-closed verification). At query time, tempest_weather_v2.Launch routes across past (Mode P), live forecast (Mode F), and climate (Mode C) regimes, enforces 2-pass hydrostatic thermodynamics, assimilates live observations via soft copulas, and compiles each sky into a 10 m float32 LUT (~0.7 μs scalar lookups) with an 86 km ISA upper tail for 6-DOF Monte Carlo flight simulators.

0.2

Why standard forecasts fail rockets

A rocket climbing to 30,000 ft AGL experiences the atmosphere differently from an airliner or a backyard weather station. In roughly thirty seconds of powered flight, it slices through the surface friction layer, morning thermal inversions, mid-tropospheric shear layers, and high-altitude jet streaks—while its stability, weathercocking, and recovery drift integrate every layer along the way. Standard weather forecasts break down for five concrete physical reasons:

  • Single deterministic forecasts hide variance and collapse wind shear. An average of two opposite wind regimes predicts calm air, even when both regimes are windy. Training a neural network with mean-squared error (MSE) produces the exact same pathology: blurry, overly smooth vertical profiles that erase sharp upper-air wind-shear cliffs and bimodal jet streaks. Rockets need full joint ensembles trained on proper scoring rules (afCRPS and Energy Score) rather than point averages.
  • 87% of wind-driven apogee spread originates in the lowest 300 m AGL. As a rocket leaves the launch rail at low airspeed, lateral wind exerts maximum weathercocking torque. Coarse global weather models space their lowest pressure levels hundreds of meters apart and report hourly mean winds—completely hiding the directional terrain roughness ($z_0$), low-level shear, and sudden 10 m gusts that dominate rail-exit dispersion.
  • Marginal calibration without vertical coherence creates impossible skies. If you independently adjust the temperature or wind distribution at each altitude to match weather balloons, you scramble the pairing between layers. A profile that grabs a 95th-percentile wind at 1,500 m and a 10th-percentile wind at 1,750 m invents physically impossible shear jumps and violates hydrostatic balance between pressure, temperature, and density.
  • Bounded relative humidity slams into 0% and 100% walls in desert air. At desert launch sites like Spaceport America, dry air routinely pushes relative humidity near 0%. Models trained in raw RH% bounce off the boundary and clip flat, distorting lower-tail moisture and virtual temperature $T_v$. Expressing moisture as log dewpoint depression $ ext{lnd} = \ln(1 + \max(T - T_d, 0))$ turns a walled box into a smooth, unbounded coordinate space.
  • Forecasts age between T-24 h and launch morning, and pads lack GPUs. A global NWP run from yesterday evening cannot see this morning’s valley wind shift or local airport METAR report. Meanwhile, trajectory teams running 1,000-shot Monte Carlo sweeps on field laptops cannot afford a 3 GB PyTorch runtime or slow per-step neural evaluations inside an ODE solver.

Design Principle: Separate Physics Coordinates, Proper Scoring, and Runtime Serving

Tempest-ARKNET solves these five failure modes by attacking each at its root: smooth physics coordinates (44 AGL + vertical differences + dpd), single-step Engression trained on proper scoring rules, balloon-calibrated vertical rank templates (Schaake shuffle + 2-pass hydrostatic integration), launch-morning soft copula assimilation, and a frozen pure-NumPy 10 m float32 LUT engine.

0.3

Core pipeline vs. modular add-ons

Every query—whether reconstructing a historical 2022 launch, forecasting next Saturday’s window, or sampling July climatology—travels through four permanent load-bearing pillars. Optional plug-ins and experimental modules attach cleanly to documented hooks around this backbone without altering its core data contracts or breaking serving reproducibility.

Pillar 1 · Physics Coordinates Chapters 1.1–1.2 →

44-Level AGL Grid & Smooth Target Space

Plain English: Rather than asking a neural network to predict raw altitude temperatures and humidity percentages that slam into 0% and 100% walls, Pillar 1 stores each column as a base value plus step-by-step vertical changes and expresses dryness on a smooth, unbounded log ruler.

Technical: Projects ERA5 pressure levels via geopotential-to-geometric height onto 44 canonical AGL levels (10–18,000 m), converts mixed-phase RH to liquid-water RH, encodes D = 179 targets via upward differences (diff.py) and log dewpoint depression lnd = ln(1 + max(T - Td, 0)), and packs C = 13/16 climate conditioning features.

tempest/region.py · tempest/profiles/{to_heights,thermo,diff}.py · tempest/generator/features.py
Pillar 2 · Probabilistic Core Chapters 2.1–2.3 →

4-Member Engression Generator & Scoring Losses

Plain English: Instead of guessing one “most likely” sky and sprinkling fake noise on top, four independent neural artists each draw plausible whole-column weather scenarios directly from random noise—graded by how well their cloud of scenarios matches real skies and extreme wind-shear cliffs.

Technical: Four width=512, depth=4 AdaLN/FiLM residual MLPs (~2.8M params each) with a linear slope bypass for slow climate drivers (year/gmst, oni, wwv, qbo30, qbo50), trained with EMA (0.999) on afCRPS ($lpha=0.95$) + Energy Score + step CRPS + 90th-percentile shear-tail loss + 0–300 m vector wind loss.

tempest/generator/{model,train,sample}.py · configs/generator_c4w.toml · tempest/gust/model.py
Pillar 3 · Balloon Calibration & Physics Chapters 3.1–3.4 →

Post-Generation Calibration & Hydrostatic Column

Plain English: Raw AI ensembles can be slightly too narrow or slightly jagged between adjacent altitudes. Pillar 3 calibrates ensemble widths against real weather balloons, re-links vertical layers using historical rank templates, shifts air for exact pad elevation, and weighs the column from the ground up so pressure, density, and speed of sound obey physics exactly.

Technical: Applies 109-knot quantile spread mapping (M_CAL = 199), 2-degree-block Schaake shuffle (uint8 199×179 templates), shear & lapse-rate quantile maps, to_site elevation adjustment ($e^{-z/1500}$ decay), and 2-pass hypsometric integration with altitude-varying $g(z)$ and virtual temperature $T_v$.

tempest/calibrate/{spread,shuffle,shear_map,lapse_map}.py · tempest_weather/_core.py
Pillar 4 · Production Serving & Sim Bridge Chapters 4.0–6.0 →

Pure-NumPy Runtime, Launch API & Fast LUT Readers

Plain English: Rocket teams should never have to install a 3 GB GPU deep-learning stack just to run a trajectory sweep. Pillar 4 freezes the entire trained system into compact, tamper-checked NumPy files, routes requests across past/forecast/climate automatically, and serves instant altitude lookups directly inside 6-DOF flight simulators.

Technical: export_tool.py writes SHA-256-verified .npz assets consumed by tempest_weather/engine.py and the immutable tempest_weather_v2.Launch API, compiling uniform 10 m float32 LUTs (~0.7 μs scalar lookup, 86 km ISA tail, optional hybrid air="standard") for joint Vehicle + Atmosphere Monte Carlo.
