Chapter 03 · Balloon Calibration & Spatial Routing

Calibration & Vertical Coherence

How Tempest-ARKNET shifts raw ERA5-trained neural ensembles onto IGRAv2 weather balloon truth, restores physical wind shear via the Schaake Shuffle, and crossfades smoothly across launch-day and climate horizons.

3.1

Weather balloon station calibration

Neural ensembles trained on ERA5 inherit reanalysis grid-box smoothing. Before any profile enters a rocket flight simulation, Tempest-ARKNET calibrates both its center and its spread against real IGRAv2 radiosonde weather balloons.

  • Why ERA5 differs from IGRAv2 balloons: ERA5 averages across a ~25 km grid cell, smoothing out sharp inversions, terrain channeling, and jet streaks that 00Z/12Z weather balloons measure directly.
  • The M_CAL = 199 quantile grid: Calibrated ensembles use 199 members spaced on exact 0.5% quantile steps ($0.5\%, 1.0\%, \dots, 99.5\%$), keeping tail extraction and rank reordering exact without rounding jitter.
  • Additive bias + spread correction: Station calibration applies a linear bias shift $(a_g + b_g E)$ with moisture-tercile residuals, then maps standardized anomalies across 109 tail-dense quantiles with linear outer-tail extrapolation.
  • Site elevation shifting: Because pads rarely match the nearest ERA5 grid elevation, Tempest-ARKNET adjusts surface pressure hydrostatically, decays the temperature lapse offset aloft, and rescales humidity within saturation limits.
Site Elevation Shift (Δz = z_site − z_grid, Γ = 0.0065 K/m) $$p_s' = p_s \exp\!\left(\frac{-g_0\,\Delta z}{R_d\,(t_{2\text{m}} - \Gamma\,\Delta z / 2)}\right), \qquad T'(z) = T(z) - \Gamma\,\Delta z\,e^{-z/1500}, \qquad \mathrm{RH}'(z) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\mathrm{RH}(z)\,\frac{e_{s,l}(T)}{e_{s,l}(T')},\,0,\,100\right)$$

Surface pressure adjusts across the full elevation offset $\Delta z$, while the temperature offset decays with a 1.5 km e-folding scale so upper-tropospheric synoptic layers remain anchored to the free atmosphere.

3.2

Restoring wind shear with the Schaake Shuffle

Marginal calibration fixes each altitude level on its own, which creates a hidden hazard for rocket dynamics: it breaks vertical wind coherence. The Schaake Shuffle repairs vertical shear without changing a single calibrated marginal value.

  • Why independent calibration destroys wind shear: Quantile mapping stretches each height independently. Member 12 might land at the 90th percentile at 1 km AGL and the 10th percentile at 3 km AGL, inventing unphysical zig-zag kinks across layers.
  • Historical ERA5 (< 2021) rank templates: Tempest-ARKNET stores compact 199 × 179 rank templates drawn strictly from pre-2021 ERA5 training profiles so zero validation or test data leaks into calibration.
  • Rank-matching without altering marginals: At each height, the 199 calibrated values are sorted and reassigned to match a historical template's rank order, preserving every marginal histogram while restoring vertical shear.

3.3

Spatial maps & lead-time routing

Launch sites rarely sit next to a radiosonde station, and mission planning spans from six months out to ten minutes before ignition. Tempest-ARKNET combines spatial interpolation with smooth cosine lead-time routing to keep ensembles continuous across both space and time.

  • Spatial interpolation to new launch sites: Station bias and spread maps interpolate across the region while the serving engine bilinearly allocates the 199 ensemble members across the four surrounding 0.25° grid cells.
  • Climate vs. Forecast vs. Launch modes: The lead-time router automatically picks the strongest data source for the requested horizon:
    • Launch mode (0 to 18 h): Anchors on live pad/METAR observations, NEXRAD VAD radar, and 3 km CONUS HRRR surface winds.
    • Forecast mode (0.75 to 15 d): Blends medium-range global NWP (ECMWF IFS / GFS) with SIGMA lead-dependent noise scaling.
    • Climate mode (> 46 d or fallback): Samples the pure-NumPy Engression generator conditioned on seasonal cycle and climate indices (ONI, QBO, GMST).
  • Smooth HRRR → GFS/IFS → Climate crossfade: Rather than hard step switches that cause sudden dispersion jumps as launch day approaches, Tempest-ARKNET blends adjacent regimes with a $C^1$-smooth cosine ramp.
  • UR5 wind sequence post-processor: Enabled by default for climate horizons, it applies a 10 m speed quantile map, spread rescaling, and a 2-regime (calm/windy) Markov chain so multi-hour wind trajectories capture realistic persistence.
Cosine Lead-Time Crossfade $$r(x;\,a, b) = \tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos\!\left(\pi\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{x - a}{b - a},\, 0,\, 1\right)\right)$$

Transitions smoothly from weight $0$ at lead time $a$ to weight $1$ at lead time $b$ with zero derivative at both endpoints, preventing jarring dispersion jumps between HRRR, global GFS/IFS, and climate Engression.
