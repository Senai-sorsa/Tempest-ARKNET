6.1

Bridging Atmospheric Profiles to 6-DOF Flight

Generating calibrated 44-level vertical ensembles is only half of the engineering problem. Inside a 6-DOF rocket trajectory integrator (such as flight_sim, RocketPy, OpenRocket, or RASAero) stepping at 100–500 Hz, the solver queries local wind velocity $(u, v, w)$, air density $\rho$, temperature $T$, pressure $p$, and speed of sound $a$ tens of thousands of times per flight—and across hundreds or thousands of trajectories in a dispersion campaign. Calling np.interp() over a 44-point non-uniform altitude grid on every Runge–Kutta substep quickly turns atmospheric lookups into the dominant runtime bottleneck.

Subsystem 6 bridges Tempest-ARKNET’s 44-level profiles into flight dynamics without losing physical fidelity or interactive speed. Four tightly coupled modules carry the load: tempest_weather_v2/readers.py compiles each profile into a sub-microsecond 10 m float32 lookup table with a continuous 86 km stratospheric tail; tempest_weather/flight/dryden.py adds fine-scale 3-axis turbulence and coherent 1-cos discrete gusts; tempest_weather/flight/acoustics.py and avionics.py derive 2D acoustic ray refraction, barometric altimeter bias, and ITU-R P.453 radio horizons; and tempest_weather_v2/montecarlo.py pairs atmospheric ensembles with vehicle parameter priors for honest dispersion bounds, variance attribution, and rare-event importance sampling.

The ~0.7 µs Scalar Fast Path & Exact 10 m Grid Equivalence

tempest_weather_v2/readers.py::Atmosphere eliminates non-uniform search overhead once at construction time. Because every single one of Tempest-ARKNET’s 44 canonical AGL heights (10, 50, 100, …, 18000 m) plus the optional 0 m launch-pad node is an exact integer multiple of the module constant GRID_M = 10.0 m, expanding the profile onto a uniform 10 m float32 table reproduces piecewise-linear interpolation over the original 44 levels bit-for-bit with zero approximation error. Pressure is interpolated linearly in $\ln p$ to honor hydrostatic exponential decay, while moist air density $\rho = p / (R_d T_v)$ and speed of sound $a = \sqrt{\gamma R_d T_v}$ are precomputed at every 10 m node from the interpolated $(T, \ln p, \mathrm{RH})$.

On the first scalar query (wind(alt), air(alt), conditions(alt), or velocity(alt)), _Table.lists() lazily materializes plain Python list copies of (u, v, w, T, lnp, rho, a). Inside _fast(alt), table lookup collapses to a single multiplication pos = (z - z0) * 0.1, integer floor i = int(pos), and scalar linear blend arr[i] + (arr[i + 1] - arr[i]) * f using standard-library math.exp. Completely bypassing NumPy array-dispatch overhead cuts scalar query latency to ~0.7 µs. When a caller evaluates an entire trajectory or altitude sweep at once, vectorized methods (wind_many, air_many) operate directly on the contiguous float32 arrays with no Python loop.

Continuous 86 km ISA Stratospheric Tail & Third-Party Exporters

Switching abruptly from Tempest’s 18 km model top to the raw 1976 US Standard Atmosphere would introduce an unphysical jump in pressure and density—causing sudden aerodynamic drag spikes in high-altitude rockets. Instead, _tail(z_top, t_top, p_top, z_max=86000.0, step=100.0) maps each 100 m geometric step into geopotential height $h_p = R_\oplus z / (R_\oplus + z)$ and integrates hydrostatically along the 7 standard ISA lapse layers (11, 20, 32, 47, 51, 71, 86 km) starting continuously from the profile’s own top $(T_{\text{top}}, p_{\text{top}})$.

For teams running external flight simulators, rocketpy_inputs(step_m=100.0, reference="asl") exports a coherent dictionary dict(pressure, temperature, wind_u, wind_v) of (alt_m, value) pairs ready for RocketPy’s Environment.set_atmospheric_model(type="custom_atmosphere", ...), while to_openrocket_csv(path, step_m=100.0) writes altitude_m, temp_K, pressure_Pa, wind_speed_ms, wind_from_deg. The legacy module tempest_weather/flight_sim_readers.py exposes standalone duck-typed TempestWind and TempestAtmosphere wrappers sharing the exact same 86 km geopotential _Tail and anchor() validation check.

Validation Paradox Resolved — Why Hybrid air="standard" Beat Full Tempest on 3-Flight Apogee MAE

Against 149 real radiosonde balloons at Spaceport America, Tempest-ARKNET’s moist air density is accurate to within 0.5%, whereas the 1976 International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) is 6.4% off because summer desert heating makes real New Mexico air markedly thinner than sea-level-referenced ISA. Yet when those same atmosphere models were flown through a 6-DOF simulator across 3 instrumented flights, Hybrid (air="standard" + Tempest wind) achieved the lowest apogee MAE at 4.89%, beating both Full Tempest (5.80%) and Pure 1976 ISA (6.15%).

The physical cause is error cancellation in ISA-tuned rocket models: collegiate and amateur rocketry teams almost universally calibrate aerodynamic drag coefficients ($C_D$), rail friction, and motor impulse efficiencies by matching prior flights inside ISA-based simulators (OpenRocket, RASAero, standard-air RocketPy). Because those parameters were fitted against an atmosphere ~6.4% too dense, the fitted $C_D$ silently absorbed a compensating bias of the opposite sign. Dropping physically truthful Tempest density into an ISA-tuned vehicle removes the atmosphere’s error and unmasks the vehicle’s drag bias. Exposing air="standard" as a first-class option preserves that historical density-vs-drag cancellation while still injecting Tempest’s calibrated wind profile—until the team re-tunes $C_D$ against true-density flights.

6.3

MIL-HDBK-1797 Dryden Turbulence Filters

ERA5 reanalysis and Tempest-ARKNET’s 44-level ensembles represent grid-cell hourly means: they capture synoptic winds and mesoscale vertical shear, but average out the sub-second, meter-scale eddies that rattle fins during Max-Q and kick the nose immediately off the launch rail. tempest_weather/flight/dryden.py synthesizes fine-grid (dz = 2.0 m) 3-axis turbulent velocity fields directly from each sampled profile’s own wind and thermal structure.

The Two-Engine Mental Model of Atmospheric Turbulence

Before reading the equations, picture atmospheric turbulence as two distinct physical engines operating at different altitudes:

  • Engine 1 — Mechanical Ground Churn ($\sigma_{\text{BL}}$): Picture a fast river scraping over a rocky riverbed. Friction against desert scrub and terrain roughness ($z_0$) rips smooth 10 m wind into rolling eddies scaled by friction velocity $u_*$. Those eddies are fiercest near the ground and decay smoothly as $(1 - z / h_{\text{PBL}})^{0.75}$ up to the planetary boundary-layer inversion lid $h_{\text{PBL}}$.
  • Engine 2 — Upper-Air Shear Instability ($\sigma_{\text{CAT}}$): High above the boundary layer, the ground is far away, so turbulence emerges from a tug-of-war between two forces. Thermal stratification acts like vertical buoyancy springs ($N^2$, the Brunt–Väisälä frequency squared) trying to snap displaced air parcels back to their home altitude, while sharp vertical wind shear ($|d\mathbf{V}/dz|^2$) tries to roll one layer over the next. Their ratio is the gradient Richardson number $\mathrm{Ri} = N^2 / |d\mathbf{V}/dz|^2$: the moment $\mathrm{Ri}$ drops below the classic Miles–Howard threshold of 0.25, shear wins and layers curl into Kelvin–Helmholtz breaking waves.

Vertical-Ascent Coordinate Flip & Profile-Derived Envelopes

In horizontal aircraft textbooks (MIL-HDBK-1797), the longitudinal axis is horizontal along the wing-fuselage line. Because a rocket ascends vertically, the along-path (“longitudinal”) turbulence component is the vertical velocity $w$, while the two horizontal components $(u, v)$ are transverse to the flight path. dryden.envelopes() computes both engines, combines them into vertical and horizontal standard deviations $\sigma_w(z)$ and $\sigma_u(z) = \sigma_v(z)$, and assigns MIL-HDBK-1797 integral length scales $L_w(z)$ and $L_u(z)$:

Equation 6.3.1 — Friction Velocity, Brunt–Väisälä Frequency & Richardson Number $$u_* = \frac{\kappa\,\max(V_{10},\, 0.5)}{\ln(10 / z_0)}, \qquad N^2 = \frac{g}{T}\!\left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \frac{g}{c_p}\right), \qquad \mathrm{Ri} = \frac{N^2}{\max(|d\mathbf{V}/dz|^2,\, 10^{-6})}$$

Here $\kappa = 0.40$ is the von Kármán constant, $V_{10}$ is the 10 m wind speed, $z_0$ is surface roughness length, and $g/c_p \approx 9.8\text{ K/km}$ is the dry adiabatic lapse rate.

Equation 6.3.2 — Two-Engine Turbulence Envelopes & MIL-HDBK-1797 Length Scales $$\sigma_{\text{CAT}} = \left|\frac{d\mathbf{V}}{dz}\right|\ell_{\text{CAT}}\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{0.25 - \mathrm{Ri}}{0.25},\, 0,\, 1\right), \qquad \sigma_w(z) = 1.3\,u_*\!\left(1 - \frac{z}{h_{\text{PBL}}}\right)^{0.75} + 0.05 + \sigma_{\text{CAT}}$$ $$\sigma_u(z) = \sigma_v(z) = \frac{\sigma_w(z)}{(0.177 + 0.000823\,z)^{0.4}} \;\; (z < 305\text{ m}), \qquad L_w = \min\!\left(\tfrac{z}{2},\, 152\right), \qquad L_u = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{z}{(0.177 + 0.000823\,z)^{1.2}},\, 2.5,\, 533\right)\text{ m}$$

For $z \ge 305\text{ m}$ (1,000 ft AGL), turbulence transitions to isotropic free-atmosphere scaling $\sigma_u = \sigma_v = \sigma_w$.

Opt-In Physics Upgrades (IL-130, IL-135 & Reynolds Stress)

  • Exact buoyancy frequency & clean free-atmosphere floor (IL-130): Enabled by default inside TempestFlightSimEngine.sample_sobol_batch(), exact_n2=True replaces an older linearized potential-temperature approximation that biased $N^2$ 31% low at 10 km and 45% low at 18 km. Pairing it with bl_floor0=True lets the boundary-layer factor decay cleanly to zero above $h_{\text{PBL}}$ with a flat 0.05 m/s free-atmosphere floor rather than leaking surface $u_*$ all the way to 18 km.
  • Profile-derived boundary-layer depth (pbl_height(), IL-135): When h_pbl_from_profile=True, pbl_height() scans upward to find the lowest altitude where dry potential temperature $\theta = T + (g / c_p) z$ rises by at least 1.0 K above the surface value, clamped to [200, 3000] m (fallback 1200.0 m).
  • Boundary-layer Reynolds stress (uw_corr): Passing opt-in uw_corr=0.35 anti-correlates vertical gusts $w'$ with the along-mean-wind horizontal fluctuation $u'_\parallel$ ($\overline{u'_\parallel w'} = -\rho \sigma_u \sigma_w$, decaying as $(1 - z / h_{\text{PBL}})^{1.5}$) while preserving the exact marginal variance of $w'$.
6.4

Euler Sub-Stepping vs. Exact AR(2) Stepping

Once envelopes() supplies altitude-varying standard deviations $(\sigma_u, \sigma_v, \sigma_w)$ and length scales $(L_u, L_w)$ on the fine vertical grid (dz = 2.0 m), dryden.synthesize() turns unit white Gaussian noise into spatially correlated 3-axis turbulence fields. The longitudinal vertical component $w$ follows a 1st-order Markov process (exact AR(1) with spatial lag coefficient $\phi = e^{-\Delta z / L_w}$), while the two transverse horizontal components $(u, v)$ obey a 2nd-order critically damped shaping filter whose discretization can be run in two distinct modes—or swapped for an FFT von Kármán cascade.

Coherent one_minus_cos() Discrete Gusts

Continuous turbulence models stationary background chatter, but structural certification and range-safety checks also test discrete coherent shear pulses. Passing 4 uniform columns (u_alt, u_len, u_dir, u_mag)—driven automatically by Sobol columns k_atmo..k_atmo+3 inside sample_sobol_batch()—superimposes a smooth horizontal 1-cos gust over $[z_{\text{start}},\, z_{\text{start}} + 2 L_{\text{half}}]$:

Equation 6.4.1 — Coherent 1-Cosine Discrete Gust Pulse $$g(z) = \frac{A}{2}\!\left(1 - \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi (z - z_{\text{start}})}{L_{\text{half}}}\right)\right), \qquad z \in [z_{\text{start}},\, z_{\text{start}} + 2 L_{\text{half}}]$$

Default parameter ranges span $z_{\text{start}} \in [0, 12]\text{ km}$, half-wavelength $L_{\text{half}} \in [15, 150]\text{ m}$, azimuth $\in [0, 2\pi)$, and peak amplitude $A \in [3, 15]\text{ m/s}$. When gust_params=(gust_mu, gust_beta) is supplied (dryden_modelling=True), $A$ is drawn as the $u_{\text{mag}}$-quantile of the profile’s own Gumbel gust law minus the 10 m mean wind, coupling gust severity directly to launch-day wind conditions.

SeedSequence Block Prefix Stability (block = 256)

A naive single RNG stream destroys reproducibility the moment you scale a Monte Carlo sweep from 200 to 1,000 flights: changing $N$ reshapes every random draw and scrambles earlier trajectories. Both dryden.synthesize() and TempestFlightSimEngine._blocked_normals() solve this by partitioning trajectories into fixed blocks of block = 256, each seeded independently via np.random.SeedSequence([seed, run_id, b0 // block]) (plus a stream ID in engine.py). As a result, the first 200 flights of a 1,000-flight campaign match a 200-flight pilot run bit-for-bit, while distinct run_id values remain statistically independent.

6.5

Acoustics, Speed of Sound & Avionics Altimetry

Beyond aerodynamic forces on the airframe, vertical gradients in temperature, moisture, and wind bend sound waves, shift barometric parachute deployment triggers, and refract radio telemetry links. Tempest-ARKNET models both domains directly from the 44-level profiles via tempest_weather/flight/acoustics.py and tempest_weather/flight/avionics.py.

2D Stratified Acoustic Ray Tracing (acoustics.py)

Predicting where solid-motor roar or supersonic N-waves strike the ground—and where observers downwind or upwind experience an eerie acoustic shadow—requires combining the local speed of sound $c(z)$ with the wind component projected along propagation azimuth $\psi$ (e_h = [sin(psi), cos(psi)]). AcousticRayTracer.trace_ray_fan() integrates a 2D fan of azimuths and depression angles $\beta$ using the stratified Snell invariant $a$:

Equation 6.5.1 — Effective Sound Speed & Stratified Ray Integrals $$c_{\text{eff}}(z, \psi) = c(z) + \mathbf{V}(z)\cdot \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\psi, \qquad a = \frac{\cos\beta}{c_{\text{eff}}(z_{\text{src}})}$$ $$\cos\theta(z) = a\,c_{\text{eff}}(z), \qquad x_\parallel = \int_0^{z_{\text{src}}} \cot\theta\,dz, \qquad t = \int_0^{z_{\text{src}}} \frac{dz}{c_{\text{eff}}\sin\theta}$$

Acoustic shadow zones: As a downward ray enters warmer low-altitude air or a strong headwind, $c_{\text{eff}}(z)$ increases. Whenever $a\,c_{\text{eff}}(z) \ge 1 - 10^{-9}$, $\cos\theta$ reaches 1, the ray turns horizontal at turning_height_m and refracts back skyward—never reaching the ground (reaches_ground = False). Cross-wind advects the ground impact by $V_\perp t$ to first order. Opt-in flags include earth_flattening=True ($c_{\text{eff}} \leftarrow c_{\text{eff}}(1 + z / R_\oplus)$, ~+0.3% at 20 km for grazing rays), moist sound_speed=batch["a"], and midpoint=True so integrands stay finite near turning points.

Pad-Zeroed Barometric Altimeter Bias & ITU-R P.453 Radio Horizon (avionics.py)

Dual-deploy flight computers invert static pressure using the 1976 ISA pressure-altitude table and zero their readout on the pad (z_baro_agl = zb - zb[:, :1]). Because real desert atmospheres are warmer or colder than ISA, barometric deployment triggers systematically disagree with true GPS geometric altitude. avionics.compute() quantifies both barometric altimeter bias and tropospheric radio refraction:

Equation 6.5.2 — Piecewise ISA Barometric Inversion & ITU-R P.453 Radio Horizon $$z_{\text{baro}}(p) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{T_0}{L}\!\left(1 - \left(\dfrac{p}{P_0}\right)^{R_d L / g_0}\right), & p \ge 22{,}632.06\text{ Pa} \\[8pt] 11{,}000 - \dfrac{R_d T_{11}}{g_0}\ln\!\left(\dfrac{p}{P_{11}}\right), & p < 22{,}632.06\text{ Pa} \end{cases}$$ $$N = 77.6\,\frac{p_{\text{hPa}}}{T} + 3.73\times 10^5\,\frac{e_{\text{hPa}}}{T^2}, \qquad k = \frac{1}{1 + R_\oplus\,\frac{dN}{dz}\cdot 10^{-6}}, \qquad d_{\text{hor}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 k R_\oplus z_{\text{AGL}}}}{1000}\text{ km}$$

Altimeter bias sign (altimeter_bias_m = z_baro_agl - z_true_agl): Negative on a warm day (warm air expands the atmospheric column so pressure stays higher at a given true height, causing the barometric altimeter to under-read) and positive on a cold day. Passing geometric=True (the default in compute()) converts ISA geopotential height to geometric height $z = R_\oplus H / (R_\oplus - H)$, removing a 51 m systematic bias at 18 km. For telemetry links, compute() outputs refractivity $N$, vertical gradient dN_dz_per_km, effective Earth-radius factor $k$ clipped to [0.2, 20.0] for ducting stability, and line-of-sight radio_horizon_km. ITU-R P.676 gaseous attenuation is intentionally omitted because it is negligible below 10 GHz.

6.6

Joint Monte Carlo & Variance Attribution

Even a perfectly calibrated atmospheric ensemble will produce dangerously narrow rocket dispersion bounds if it is run with a single deterministic vehicle model. In the 2026-11-08 flight-sim validation, measured flight apogees fell outside the atmosphere-only Monte Carlo pool in 18 of 20 cases because vehicle uncertainty (drag coefficient $C_D$, total impulse, and dry mass) is 2–4× larger than atmospheric spread. Understanding both where atmospheric wind matters and how to combine it with vehicle priors is the central job of tempest_weather_v2/montecarlo.py.

The 0–300 m Weathercocking Lever Arm

When a high-power rocket clears the launch rail at 50–150 m AGL, its upward velocity $V_{\text{rocket}}$ is still low (typically 25–60 m/s). Even a modest 6 m/s crosswind creates a large aerodynamic angle of attack $\alpha \approx \arctan(V_{\text{wind}} / V_{\text{rocket}})$ right off the rail. Because a statically stable rocket has its center of pressure aft of its center of gravity, the fins immediately generate a restoring torque that swings the nose into the relative wind—weathercocking. Once tilted off-vertical during the first two seconds, the rocket’s own motor thrust locks in that tilted flight path for the entire burn. By contrast, a 25 m/s jet-stream wind at 8 km strikes a vehicle already coasting at Mach 0.8–1.5, producing a tiny $\alpha$ and minimal apogee loss. Sensitivity sweeps confirm that 87% of all wind-driven apogee spread originates in 0–300 m AGL, with 53% coming from the 50–150 m rail-exit band alone.

Joint VehiclePriors Sampling, Rank-$R^2$ Attribution & Sobol Indices

tempest_weather_v2/montecarlo.py pairs atmospheric ensembles with named multiplicative vehicle scale factors centered at 1.0:

  • VehiclePriors & run(): Supports normal ($1 + \sigma z$), lognormal ($\exp(\sigma z - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)$, exact mean 1), and uniform ($1 + \sigma(2u - 1)$), sampled via Latin Hypercube (method="lhs", default) or scrambled Sobol QMC (method="sobol"). Optional wind_age injects frozen persistence increments $\sigma_{\text{persist}} = \sqrt{2(1-\rho)}\,\sigma_{\text{pool}}$ and requires conditioned=True to prevent double-counting forecast uncertainty.
  • Physical Rank-$R^2$ Attribution (MCResult.contributions() & explained()): Computes single-input rank-$R^2$ for every vehicle prior alongside three physical atmospheric summaries extracted by atmosphere_metrics(): pad_air_density, mean_wind_0_200m, and mean_wind_0_3km (replacing the meaningless raw sample index). explained() fits joint OLS rank regression to report r2_vehicle, r2_all, and incremental atmosphere_gain = max(r2_all - r2_vehicle, 0).
  • Saltelli $S_i$ & Jansen $S_{Ti}$ (sobol_indices()): Executes an A / B / AB_i scrambled Sobol design over $d$ inputs (vehicle priors plus uniform atmosphere index) in $n(d + 2)$ simulator evaluations, returning first-order variance share $S_i$ and total-effect share $S_{Ti}$ (capturing nonlinear wind×drag interactions) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.

Rare-Event Importance Sampling, Deconvolution & Online Conformal Calibration

Estimating a 1-in-1,000 range-safety exceedance probability $p$ via crude Monte Carlo demands $\sim 100 / p$ flights. run_importance() avoids that cost by ranking a large, cheap pool of $m$ atmospheres ($m \ge 20 / p$) by metric="mean_wind_0_200m", mapping ranks to normal scores $z_j = \Phi^{-1}((r_j + 0.5) / m)$, tilting sampling probabilities $q_j \propto \exp(\text{tilt}\cdot z_j)$ (default tilt = 2.0 shifts the sampling mean into the windiest 2–3%), and reweighting each simulated flight by $w_k = (1/m) / q_{\text{idx}_k}$ for unbiased probability(), Kish effective_n(), and weighted quantile().

Empirical Caution on Importance Sampling (2026-11-09 Benchmark)

When vehicle priors also drive the rare failure tail and Kish effective_n() < 20, reported importance-sampling standard errors can appear deceptively tight: in the 2026-11-09 benchmark, 150 runs reported 0.0017 ± 0.0003 against a 3,000-run crude MC truth of 0.0080, whereas 300 runs converged cleanly to 0.0080 ± 0.0023. Always use n ≥ 300, tilt the dominant physical metric, and spot-check against crude MC when effective_n() drops below 20.

To close the loop across multi-flight launch campaigns, calibrate_vehicle_sigma() deconvolves systematic vehicle bias and random vehicle scatter from historical signed apogee errors $e_i$ (%) and atmosphere-only pool spreads $\sigma_{\text{atm},i}$ (%) (flagged reliable = (n >= 5)), while AdaptiveConformal(alpha=0.10, gamma=0.05) (Gibbs & Candès 2021) multiplies the ensemble’s central $(1 - \alpha)$ interval symmetrically about its midpoint by an online stretch factor $s_t$:

Equation 6.6.1 — Vehicle Error Deconvolution & Adaptive Conformal Interval Stretch $$\sigma_{\text{veh,rms}} = \sqrt{\max\!\left(\overline{e^2} - \overline{\sigma_{\text{atm}}^2},\, 0\right)}, \qquad \sigma_{\text{veh,scatter}} = \sqrt{\max\!\left(\mathrm{Var}(e) - \overline{\sigma_{\text{atm}}^2},\, 0\right)}$$ $$s_{t+1} = \mathrm{clip}\!\big(s_t + \gamma(\text{miss}_t - \alpha),\; 0.05,\; 20.0\big)$$

Unlike quantile re-leveling—which saturates at the ensemble min/max when a simulator is under-dispersed—multiplicative stretching guarantees long-run marginal coverage $1 - \alpha$ regardless of underlying miscalibration.
