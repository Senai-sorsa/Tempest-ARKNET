Proper Scoring Rules & Physics-Informed Losses

How Tempest-ARKNET avoids MSE ensemble collapse using a five-term strictly proper composite objective: Almost-Fair CRPS (alpha = 0.95), dimension-normalized 179-D Energy Score, vertical step CRPS, 90th-percentile vector shear tails, and 0-300 m rail-exit wind loss.

2.2.1

Why MSE collapses atmospheric ensembles

A probabilistic atmospheric generator lives or dies by its training objective. Suppose you train a neural network with noise input $\varepsilon$ using ordinary Mean Squared Error (MSE): $\mathbb{E}\big[\|g_\theta(c, \varepsilon) - y\|_2^2\big]$. By elementary calculus, the unique minimizer of expected squared error is the deterministic conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}[y \mid c]$—completely independent of $\varepsilon$. The optimizer actively learns to zero out all noise weights, collapsing every ensemble member onto one smooth, unrealistically calm mean profile that misses wind shear, jet streaks, and turbulence.

To force the network to output an honest probability distribution rather than a blurred average, Tempest-ARKNET trains its 4-member Engression ensemble (configs/generator_c4w.toml, m = 4 parallel draws per row, batch = 4096, forced fp32 inside tempest/generator/train.py::score) by minimizing a five-term composite of strictly proper scoring rules—objectives whose unique global optimum is achieved if and only if the generated ensemble distribution equals the true conditional atmospheric distribution:

Equation 2.2.1 · Complete Shipped c4w Composite Objective $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{c4w}}(s, y) = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{afCRPS}}(s, y)}_{\alpha = 0.95,\;\text{179-D margins}} + \underbrace{\frac{0.1}{\sqrt{179}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ES}}(s, y)}_{\text{joint 179-D coherence}} + \underbrace{0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{afCRPS}}\!\left(\hat{d}_{0:176},\, d^{\text{true}}_{0:176}\right)}_{\text{layer-to-layer jumps}} + \underbrace{1.0\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{shear-tail}}}_{q = 0.9\text{ shear spikes}} + \underbrace{0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{low-wind}}}_{0\text{–}300\text{ m vector + speed}}$$
Why five terms instead of one? Every term targets a distinct physical failure mode of rocket atmospheres: (1) marginal calibration at every height, (2) cross-altitude and cross-variable coherence, (3) local layer-to-layer wind roughness, (4) extreme 90th-percentile fin-breaking shear spikes, and (5) near-ground 0–300 m rail-exit weathercocking vector accuracy.

Why height-to-height step CRPS (step_weight = 0.5) is mandatory

Even though Generator outputs raw targets in telescoping vertical-difference space, the first two loss terms are evaluated after cumsum reconstructs physical levels (phys_std, all 179 coordinates). Cumulative summation acts as a low-pass filter: small errors of opposite sign in adjacent layers cancel out in level space, hiding missing roughness. Empirically, when Tempest-ARKNET was trained on reconstructed physical levels alone, generated profiles exhibited only 1/2 to 1/3 of real atmospheric wind shear (configs/generator_c4w.toml:8), yielding deceptively smooth columns that under-predicted structural loads.

Adding step_weight = 0.5 evaluates afCRPS directly on the raw normalized vertical-difference slice [0:176] ($4\text{ variables} \times 44\text{ heights} = 176$, excluding the 3 surface scalars at [176:179]), penalizing over-smoothed layer-to-layer jumps in $u, v, T$, and log dewpoint depression lnd directly before integration can hide them.

2.2.2

Unbiased finite-sample Fair CRPS

For any single scalar coordinate—say, East-West wind $u$ at 3 km AGL—the population Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) compares a predictive distribution $F$ against an observed scalar $y$ via two competing expectations, where $X, X' \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} F$:

Equation 2.2.2 · Energy-Form Representation of Population CRPS $$\mathrm{CRPS}(F, y) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}|X - y|}_{\text{accuracy (fit penalty)}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}|X - X'|}_{\text{dispersion (spread credit)}}$$
Plain-English intuition: The first term pulls ensemble members close to the true sounding $y$. The second term pushes distinct ensemble members $X$ and $X'$ apart from each other. Because the spread credit carries a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$, an ensemble cannot lower its score by exploding to infinity (fit penalty grows twice as fast as spread credit once members leave the data cloud), nor can it collapse to a single point (losing all spread credit). Equilibrium occurs precisely when the ensemble spread matches nature’s true uncertainty.

During GPU training, however, we cannot draw infinite samples $X, X'$; we draw a tiny finite ensemble of $m = 4$ members per training row. If you naively estimate $\mathbb{E}|X - X'|$ by averaging over all $m^2$ pairs $(i, j)$—the plain CRPS estimator—you include the $m$ diagonal pairs where $i = j$ and $|s_i - s_i| = 0$:

Equation 2.2.3 · Plain vs. Unbiased Fair CRPS at Finite Ensemble Size $m$ $$\widehat{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{plain}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|s_i - y| - \frac{1}{2m^2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|s_i - s_j|, \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{CRPS}}_{\text{fair}} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|s_i - y| - \frac{1}{2m(m-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|s_i - s_j|$$
Why plain CRPS causes chronic overconfidence at $m = 4$: There are only $m(m-1) = 12$ distinct off-diagonal pairs out of $m^2 = 16$ total pairs. Dividing by $2m^2$ instead of $2m(m-1)$ silently discounts the spread reward by $1 - \frac{m-1}{m} = \frac{1}{m} = 25\%$! Facing a 25% tax on dispersion, the optimizer systematically shrinks ensemble spread below reality, producing overconfident launch envelopes. Replacing $m^2$ with $m(m-1)$ yields the exact unbiased U-statistic (Fair CRPS, Ferro 2014) for any $m \ge 2$.
2.2.3

The crps_alpha = 0.95 stability blend

If Fair CRPS ($\alpha = 1$) is mathematically unbiased for every $m \ge 2$, why doesn’t Tempest-ARKNET simply train with $\alpha = 1$? Because at small training ensembles ($m = 4$), exact Fair CRPS exhibits a subtle runaway outlier degeneracy during stochastic gradient descent.

Consider what happens to a single member $s_1$ that drifts far above the observation $y$ and the other three members $s_2, s_3, s_4$. Moving $s_1$ upward by $+\Delta$ increases the fit term $\frac{1}{m}\sum |s_i - y|$ by $+\frac{1}{m}\Delta = +\frac{1}{4}\Delta$. Simultaneously, $s_1$ moves $+\Delta$ farther from each of the $(m-1) = 3$ other members (counted twice in the double sum, once as $(1, j)$ and once as $(j, 1)$), so the Fair CRPS spread credit $-\frac{1}{2m(m-1)}\sum_{i,j}|s_i - s_j|$ decreases the loss by $-\frac{2(m-1)}{2m(m-1)}\Delta = -\frac{1}{m}\Delta = -\frac{1}{4}\Delta$.

The fit penalty and spread credit cancel out to exact zero! Once a stray sample escapes past the cloud, pure Fair CRPS exerts zero restoring force to pull it back—opening the door to explosive gradients and outlier blowup. Following ECMWF AIFS-CRPS, Tempest-ARKNET uses the Almost-Fair CRPS (afcrps) in tempest/generator/model.py:400–407 with crps_alpha = 0.95:

Equation 2.2.4 · Almost-Fair CRPS (afcrps, $\alpha = 0.95$, weight = 1.0) $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{afCRPS}} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m} |s_{b,i,d} - y_{b,d}| - \frac{1 - \varepsilon}{2m(m-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m} |s_{b,i,d} - s_{b,j,d}|\right], \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{1 - \alpha}{m}$$
Exact arithmetic at $m = 4, \alpha = 0.95$: $\varepsilon = (1 - 0.95)/4 = 0.0125$, so the spread multiplier is $1 - \varepsilon = 0.9875$. An escaping outlier now pays $+\frac{1}{4}\Delta$ in fit penalty while earning only $-\frac{0.9875}{4}\Delta$ in spread credit—leaving a gentle, strictly positive net restoring gradient of $+\frac{0.0125}{4}\Delta$ that reels runaway tails back in without re-introducing overconfidence.
CRPS Regime $\alpha$ Spread Factor $(1 - \varepsilon)$ at $m = 4$ Optimization & Calibration Behavior
Plain CRPS 0.00 0.7500 Discounts pairwise spread by 25% due to $m$ zero diagonal terms; produces chronic under-dispersion (overconfident ensembles).
Fair CRPS 1.00 1.0000 Unbiased U-statistic, but has zero net restoring slope for an escaping single outlier at finite $m = 4$; risks runaway tail gradients.
Almost-Fair CRPS (Shipped) 0.95 0.9875 Retains 95% of the unbiased finite-$m$ spread correction while preserving a 5% restoring leash on escaping tails; stable across all 6,000 steps in c4w.
2.2.4

Multivariate 179-D Energy Score

Marginal CRPS averages independently across all $D = 179$ coordinates. A generator could match the exact marginal bell curve of wind at 10 m and wind at 10 km while completely scrambling their vertical correlation—or pairing a frigid winter temperature column with a humid tropical dewpoint—and incur zero penalty from afcrps. To enforce physical coherence across all 44 altitudes and all 4 atmospheric variables simultaneously, train.py::score adds the multivariate unbiased Energy Score (tempest/generator/model.py:392–397) over the full standardized 179-D column vector:

Equation 2.2.5 · Dimension-Normalized Unbiased 179-D Energy Score (es_weight = 0.1) $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{ES}} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\left[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m} \|s_{b,i} - y_b\|_2 - \frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i < j}\|s_{b,i} - s_{b,j}\|_2\right], \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{ES,weighted}} = \frac{w_{\text{ES}}}{\sqrt{D}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ES}}$$
Why divide by $\sqrt{D} = \sqrt{179} \approx 13.38$? Because each standardized coordinate has unit variance ($\mathrm{std} \approx 1$), the Euclidean norm $\|s_{b,i} - y_b\|_2$ of a $D$-dimensional atmospheric vector grows as $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{D})$—roughly $13.4\times$ larger than a 1-D absolute error. Dividing by $\sqrt{D}$ restores $\mathcal{O}(1)$ scale parity with 1-D afcrps, so es_weight = 0.1 acts as a true 10% relative weight invariant to the number of vertical grid levels. Note also that the pairwise sum runs over unordered pairs $i < j$ (so the denominator is $m(m-1)$ rather than $2m(m-1)$).

Confirmed Code Bug B12 · Missing epsilon floor in model.py::energy_score

In tempest/generator/model.py:395–396, energy_score() computes (samples - y.unsqueeze(1)).norm(dim=-1) and (samples[:, i] - samples[:, j]).norm(dim=-1) with no smoothing epsilon. Because the gradient of $\|x\|_2$ is $x / \|x\|_2$, any coinciding ensemble pair ($s_{b,i} = s_{b,j}$) or exact target match produces $0/0 = \mathrm{NaN}$ in backpropagation. By contrast, the newer Section 25 helper s25_losses.py::_norm (lines 19–20) explicitly guards against this via torch.sqrt(x.pow(2).sum(-1) + 1e-12). Updating model.py::energy_score to reuse _norm eliminates this latent gradient singularity.

Alternative multivariate proper scores benchmarked in s25_losses.py

Before locking in the shipped c4w objective, Tempest-ARKNET evaluated four heavier multivariate scoring rules in tempest/generator/s25_losses.py (all off by default with zero import overhead in production):

Alternative Loss Mathematical Mechanism Why Shipped c4w Kept Simpler Terms
variogram_score Order-$p$ vertical variogram ($p = 0.5$) across all $\binom{44}{2} = 946$ height pairs per variable, weighted by $1 / (j - i)$, matching $\mathbb{E}|X_i - X_j|^p$ to truth. Adds $\mathcal{O}(H^2)$ pairwise compute overhead and overlaps heavily with step_weight + post-hoc Schaake Shuffle (shuffle.py).
shear_vec_es 2-D Energy Score on every layer’s normalized $(\Delta u_l, \Delta v_l)$ shear vector (43 layers averaged) to jointly couple zonal and meridional turning. Gating an entire 88-D vector norm on the single column-max shear dilutes local per-layer gradients compared to per-layer shear_tail_loss.

Quadratic level-2 cross-terms add gradient variance without beating the simpler physical step + tail-shear + low-wind trio on radiosonde verification.

2.2.5

Wind-shear tail loss & excess-std floor

Component-wise step CRPS still averages uniformly across all weather days: the calm 90% of hours dominate the minibatch gradient, leaving rare, razor-sharp wind-shear sheets under-resolved. Yet during rocket ascent through Max-Q, it is precisely those 90th-percentile shear spikes—sudden jumps in wind speed or direction across a thin vertical layer—that trigger violent angle-of-attack transients and snap fins.

Introduced in Phase 2 (p2e/p2f) and shipped in c4w with weight shear_tail_weight = 1.0 (tempest/generator/train.py:268–274, 309–314), shear_tail_loss computes the physical vector wind-shear magnitude $S_l$ ($\text{m s}^{-1}\,\text{km}^{-1}$) on each of the 43 vertical layers and applies threshold-weighted afCRPS exclusively to the excess above that layer’s 90th-percentile training threshold $q_{0.9,l}$:

Equation 2.2.6 · Threshold-Weighted Vector Shear Tail Loss (q = 0.9, weight = 1.0) $$S_l = \frac{\sqrt{\Delta u_l^2 + \Delta v_l^2}}{\Delta z_{\text{km},l}}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{shear-tail}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{afCRPS}}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{ReLU}(S_l - q_{0.9,l})}{\sigma_{\text{excess},l}}\right)$$
Why normalize by the excess-std floor $\sigma_{\text{excess},l}$? Raw wind shear in the lowest 50 m above the ground is vastly larger in $\text{m s}^{-1}\,\text{km}^{-1}$ than shear at 14 km in the lower stratosphere. Precomputing $\sigma_{\text{excess},l} = \mathrm{std}(\max(S_l^{\text{train}} - q_{0.9,l}, 0))$ once from training rows (with a safe positive floor) puts every altitude layer on equal $\mathcal{O}(1)$ footing. And because $g_l(S_l) = \mathrm{ReLU}(S_l - q_{0.9,l}) / \sigma_{\text{excess},l}$ is a fixed, deterministic chaining map applied identically to both generated samples and ground truth, the score remains strictly proper—forcing exact tail frequency and intensity without artificially inflating calm-day shear.

Optional multi-scale & thermodynamic physics guards in train.py

Three additional physical regularizers exist in train.py (all toggled off in shipped c4w because the five core terms plus post-generation calibration already satisfied radiosonde verification):

  • stride_loss (step_strides, lines 294–301): Replaces single-layer step CRPS with averaged afcrps over sliding window sums of $s$ consecutive layers (e.g., [1, 2, 4, 8, 16]), each normalized by its training standard deviation.
  • superadiabatic (superadiabatic_weight, lines 303–307): Penalizes mean-squared excess lapse rate $\mathrm{ReLU}(-\Delta T / \Delta z_{\text{km}} - 9.8)^2$ above 2 km AGL to discourage convectively unstable dry-adiabatic violations.
  • rh_pct (rh_loss_weight, lines 319–325): Reconstructs liquid-water RH (%) from $T$ and lnd for extra CRPS supervision, clamping $T \in [150, 350]\text{ K}$ and $\text{lnd} \le 5.0$ so early training noise cannot hit the Tetens denominator pole at $32.19\text{ K}$.
2.2.6

Low-wind rail-exit vector loss

In 6-DOF rocket dispersion studies, the lowest 300 m AGL accounts for roughly 87% of wind-driven apogee spread. Immediately after the rocket clears the launch rail, its forward airspeed is still low; any crosswind therefore induces a large aerodynamic angle of attack, causing the fins to weathercock the vehicle into the wind and permanently tilt the rest of the ascent trajectory.

On the canonical 44-height grid, HEIGHTS <= 300.0 selects the 6 lowest levels (10, 50, 100, 150, 200, 300 m AGL). Inside tempest/generator/train.py:327–338, 351–352, low_wind_loss rebuilds physical $(u, v)$ on those 6 levels, scales velocities by $3.0\text{ m/s}$ to match $\mathcal{O}(1)$ standardized units, and combines a 12-dimensional stacked vector Energy Score with a 6-level scalar wind-speed afCRPS (shipped weight wind_vec_weight = 0.5):

Equation 2.2.7 · Near-Ground Rail-Exit Vector + Speed Loss (low_top_m = 300 m) $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{low-wind}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{12}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{ES}}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{V}_{0\text{–}300}}{3.0},\, \frac{\mathbf{V}_{0\text{–}300}^{\text{true}}}{3.0}\right) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{afCRPS}}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_k^2 + v_k^2}}{3.0},\, \frac{\sqrt{(u_k^{\text{true}})^2 + (v_k^{\text{true}})^2}}{3.0}\right)$$
Why include scalar wind speed alongside the stacked $(u, v)$ vector? Even when marginal $u$ and $v$ are individually calibrated, subtle cross-component covariance errors systematically bias the nonlinear magnitude $\sqrt{u^2 + v^2}$—the exact quantity governing dynamic pressure and rail-exit weathercocking. In the c4w ablation (configs/generator_c4w.toml:27–30), turning on only wind_vec_weight = 0.5 while holding low_weight = 1.0 locked in the 0–1 km wind gains from the earlier C4b run without incurring the surface-humidity (RH/rh2m) degradation caused by setting low_weight = 3.0.

Why one-step Engression + c4w beat eight alternative architectures (s25_models.py)

Every experimental generative family in tempest/generator/s25_models.py implemented the identical forward(cond, m) → (B, m, 179) contract so downstream calibration and radiosonde verification ran unchanged. None displaced the single-pass 4-block AdaLN MLP:

Experimental Architecture Core Mechanism Trade-Off vs. Shipped One-Step Engression
FlowGenerator Conditional rectified flow predicting velocity $v = x_1 - x_0$ with sinusoidal tau_embed and 8-step Euler sampling. 8× slower inference, breaks simple 1-pass NumPy serving, and MSE velocity matching under-optimizes tail shear.
RefineGenerator Frozen 1-step Engression draft blended with noise at $\tau_{\min}$, refined by a FlowGenerator over $[\tau_{\min}, 1]$. Doubles checkpoint size and requires two-stage composition (s25_compose_refine.py) for marginal gains already achieved by fast post-calibration.
RMLGenerator Reverse Markov Learning ($K = 3$ cosine noise levels) denoising $x_k \to x_{k-1}$ with full score() on $k=1$. 3× serving cost and multi-step exposure bias without beating single-step AdaLN Engression.
HARGenerator Height-Autoregressive column synthesis bottom-to-top in 7 height blocks (har_blocks). 7× sequential passes, surface-to-stratosphere error compounding, and loss of top-down jet conditioning at lower levels.
MoEGenerator ExpertHead ($E = 3$ experts) gated by straight-through softmax on 0–3 km lapse rate, 5.5 km wind, and season + aux_loss. Adds discrete routing variance; the continuous 512-wide AdaLN trunk already separates convective, stable, and jet regimes smoothly.
EOFGenerator Top-$k$ SVD Empirical Orthogonal Functions (95% variance) + parametric AR(1)-in-height residual across 4 altitude bands. Gaussian AR(1) small-scale noise is too smooth for sharp, non-Gaussian shear sheets and temperature inversions.
PriorResidual Monthly box-wide AR(1)-in-height statistical prior (GRAM-style) plus learned residual correction. Ties the generator to a coarse monthly prior that blurs local terrain and diurnal structure.
AnalogGenerator Brute-force GPU kNN (torch.cdist) over up to 200 MB of thinned training rows ($k = 8$ analogs) + learned band warps. ~200 MB checkpoint table, poor serving scaling, and hits Bug B11 (names.index("year") crashes on --features p2).
