Serving & Launch-Day Obs

Zero-PyTorch .npz serving in ~20 ms, Bayesian assimilation of pad/METAR/radar observations, and v2 Student-t copulas that keep extreme gusts coupled.

5.1

Pure-NumPy serving engine

Training requires PyTorch, CUDA, Zarr, and xarray—gigabytes of compiled binaries that do not belong on a desert launch laptop. After training and calibration, every weight, quantile table, and terrain grid exports into a SHA-256-verified .npz bundle that runs in ~20 ms on a standard CPU using vectorized NumPy BLAS calls.

Training GPU Stack vs. Pure-NumPy Launch-Pad Serving

Sealing weights and lookup tables into a verified .npz bundle decouples training hardware from field laptops and 6-DOF worker pools.

5.2

Live pad, METAR & radar assimilation

Between the last global NWP cycle and ignition, local desert winds and thermal inversions evolve faster than coarse grids can track. Live pad anemometer, METAR, and NEXRAD radar readings pull the forecast ensemble toward reality in proportion to how fresh and close each measurement is.

  • Soft Bayesian pull instead of hard pinning: Every observation receives a fitted gain $r \in (0, 1]$ mapped to an effective observation error. A gain of $r = 1$ pins the ensemble; $r = 0.5$ pulls the median halfway toward the live reading while preserving residual uncertainty.
  • Pad-station time decay (6.3 h) and local radius (5.0 km): Measurements within 5.0 km of the launch rail follow own-station persistence decay with a 6.3 h exponential timescale (calibrated on benchmark GW-1: gain ~0.80–0.88 at 1 h, ~0.63–0.66 at 3 h).
  • Cross-station METAR attenuation: Beyond 5.0 km, representativeness drops with distance and stability—from 0.62 (day) / 0.48 (night) at 50 km down to 0.32 (day) / 0.18 (night) at 500 km, with age decay scales of 27 h by day and 12 h in decoupled nighttime layers.
  • NEXRAD VAD radar opt-in (3.0 m/s error floor): Vertical Azimuth Display (VAD) wind profiles from nearby NEXRAD WSR-88D radars assimilate upper-air shear above the pad anemometer, staying strictly opt-in so offline field workflows never stall on network fetches.
Observation Error Conversion $$\sigma_{\text{obs}} = s_{\text{prior}} \sqrt{\frac{1}{r(\Delta t, d, \Delta z)} - 1}, \qquad r_{\text{own}}(\Delta t) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{6.3\text{ h}}\right) \quad (d \le 5.0\text{ km})$$

As observation age $\Delta t$ grows or distance $d$ exceeds 5.0 km, gain $r$ falls, $\sigma_{\text{obs}}$ grows, and the Bayesian update relaxes smoothly back to the calibrated forecast prior.

Observation Assimilation Weight Decay: Time ($\Delta t$) & Distance ($d$)

Fitted ASOS leave-one-station-out (LOSO) gains keep distant or stale airport reports from overriding the forecast while letting fresh pad readings inside 5.0 km use full Bayesian weight.

5.3

v2 toolkit & Student-t copulas

Built on top of the pure-NumPy runtime, tempest_weather_v2 provides an immutable launch-day workflow for comparing windows, computing aviation safety metrics, adjusting surface roughness, and preserving extreme tail coupling across altitude.

  • Immutable Launch & Window ergonomics: A Launch freezes its coordinates, elevation, and timestamp on creation. Branching a 09:00 MDT baseline into a 13:00 MDT slip or adding a pad reading returns a fresh scenario without mutating the baseline, while Window sweeps solar-hour-gated windows cleanly.
  • Vectorized aviation calculators: Computes density altitude, rail headwind and crosswind, local speed of sound and Mach number, altimeter setting, and drift-equivalent uniform wind across the full ensemble in pure NumPy.
  • Directional EPA AERSURFACE roughness: Maps directional terrain land-cover sectors around the pad to aerodynamic roughness length $z_0$ for log-law shear adjustments between the pad anemometer and the 10 m model node.
  • Why Student-$t$ copulas preserve simultaneous extreme gusts:
    • Gaussian tail decoupling: A Gaussian copula has zero asymptotic upper tail dependence ($\lambda_U = 0$ for any $\rho < 1$), so extreme upper-air jet streaks and strong rail-exit gusts artificially separate in the 1-in-100 tail.
    • Student-$t$ upper-tail coupling: With finite degrees of freedom $\nu < \infty$, a Student-$t$ copula retains strictly positive $\lambda_U > 0$, keeping joint extreme wind loads intact for range-safety dispersion.
Asymptotic Upper Tail Dependence $$\text{Gaussian Copula } (\rho < 1): \qquad \lambda_U = \lim_{q \to 1^-} P(U_2 > q \mid U_1 > q) = 0$$ $$\text{Student-}t\text{ Copula } (\nu < \infty): \qquad \lambda_U = 2\,t_{\nu+1}\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{(\nu + 1)(1 - \rho)}{1 + \rho}}\right) > 0$$

Even when two altitude layers share the same rank correlation $\rho$, only the Student-$t$ copula preserves co-occurring extreme gusts in the tail; as $\nu \to \infty$, $\lambda_U$ collapses back to the Gaussian zero limit.
