3.2.1

How Marginal Calibration Destroys Wind Shear

Marginal calibration—spread mapping, PIT recalibration, and seasonal width scaling from Chapter 3.1—guarantees that every single altitude column has the right probability distribution when inspected in isolation. A 6-DOF rocket, however, does not fly through 44 independent lottery draws; it ascends through a continuous vertical column where wind speed at 1 km is tightly coupled to wind speed at 1.25 km and 2 km. If marginal post-processing re-sorts columns independently or pairs member #17's high-wind draw at 1 km with a calm draw at 2 km, it invents a fictitious wind-shear cliff ($\Delta\mathbf{u}/\Delta z$) that spikes aerodynamic angle-of-attack, bends the airframe, and corrupts Monte Carlo apogee and landing dispersion.

Tempest-ARKNET restores physical column structure through a strictly ordered sequence of rank-preserving and high-pass post-processing maps, followed by optional rare-shear tail patching, simultaneous 44-height safety envelopes, and an unwired tropopause-relative quantile map:

Equation 3.2.1 — Ordered Vertical Coherence Restoration Pipeline $$\underbrace{\text{Marginal Calib}}_{\text{spread / PIT / seasonal}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{Schaake Shuffle}}_{\texttt{shuffle.py}\text{ (Shipped ON)}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{Shear Map}}_{\texttt{shear\_map.py}\text{ (Shipped ON)}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{Lapse Map}}_{\texttt{lapse\_map.py}\text{ (Shipped ON)}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\text{Joint 99\% Band}}_{\texttt{joint.py}\text{ (Output-Only)}}$$

3.2.2

Schaake Shuffle Rank-Template Restoration

The Schaake shuffle—also known as Ensemble Copula Coupling (ECC), implemented in tempest/calibrate/shuffle.py (IL-82 / IL-83 / IL-95 / IL-161)—solves a deceptively clean problem: keep every single column's calibrated numbers bit-for-bit identical, and change only which ensemble member at altitude $z_1$ gets paired with which member at altitude $z_2$ (and across $u, v, T, \text{RH}$ and surface variables).

Operating on Tempest-ARKNET's full (N, 199, 179) calibrated ensemble tensor, shuffle.apply(x, template, w=1.0) extracts the column-wise rank order $r_t$ of $m = 199$ real historical training profiles and assigns the sorted calibrated values of x into those exact rank slots via np.take_along_axis. When a fractional blend weight $w < 1.0$ is supplied (IL-161), apply() linearly blends the historical template ranks with the neural network's own native rank order $r_x$ and re-ranks stably (kind="stable"):

Equation 3.2.2 — Pure & Blended Schaake Shuffle Operator $$r_t = \operatorname{rank}_m(\text{template}), \qquad r_x = \operatorname{rank}_m(x), \qquad r_{\text{blend}} = \operatorname{rank}_m^{\text{stable}}\!\big(w \cdot r_t + (1 - w) \cdot r_x\big)$$ $$x^{\text{shuf}}_{n, i, d} = x^{\text{sorted}}_{n,\, r_{\text{blend}}(n, i, d),\, d} \qquad (w = 1 \implies \text{pure Schaake shuffle};\; w = 0 \implies \text{network copula})$$

Exact Marginal Invariance: Because $r_{\text{blend}}(n, \cdot, d)$ is a bijection (permutation) of $\{0, \dots, m - 1\}$ along the ensemble-member axis $m$ for every row $n$ and coordinate $d \in \{0, \dots, 178\}$, the unordered multiset of values in column $d$ is strictly unchanged. Every per-height quantile, median, coverage interval, PIT rank, and per-column CRPS changes by exactly 0.000—while cross-altitude correlation error drops from 0.08 → 0.03.

3.2.3

Worked 3×2 Schaake Shuffle Example

Before running the shuffle on a 199-member × 179-coordinate tensor, walk through a concrete 3-member × 2-altitude example by hand. Suppose marginal calibration has already produced exact calibrated wind speeds at 1 km ({2, 8, 14} m/s) and 2 km ({5, 11, 18} m/s). Three historical profiles from the same month, 2-hour solar bin, and $2^\circ$ geographic cell show positively correlated winds across 1 km and 2 km, giving the rank template (low, low), (mid, mid), (high, high) = rank indices (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2).

Member Unshuffled Pairing (1 km, 2 km) Implied Shear $\Delta u / \Delta z$ Template Ranks $(r_{1\text{km}}, r_{2\text{km}})$ Schaake-Shuffled (1 km, 2 km) Restored Shear $\Delta u / \Delta z$
Member 1 (14 m/s, 5 m/s) −9.0 m/s/km (fake cliff) (low, low) = (0, 0) (2 m/s, 5 m/s) +3.0 m/s/km
Member 2 (8 m/s, 11 m/s) +3.0 m/s/km (mid, mid) = (1, 1) (8 m/s, 11 m/s) +3.0 m/s/km
Member 3 (2 m/s, 18 m/s) +16.0 m/s/km (fake cliff) (high, high) = (2, 2) (14 m/s, 18 m/s) +4.0 m/s/km
Column Multiset 1 km: {2, 8, 14} · 2 km: {5, 11, 18} Range: −9.0 to +16.0 Exact bijection 1 km: {2, 8, 14} · 2 km: {5, 11, 18} Range: +3.0 to +4.0

Verifying the Exact NumPy Indexing Step by Step

  1. Sort each column independently: x_sorted = np.sort(x, axis=1) gives column 1 km = [2, 8, 14] and column 2 km = [5, 11, 18].
  2. Extract template rank slots: r_t = np.argsort(np.argsort(template, axis=1), axis=1) yields rank index 0 for Member 1 at both heights, 1 for Member 2, and 2 for Member 3.
  3. Scatter sorted values into rank slots: np.take_along_axis(x_sorted, r_t, axis=1) places x_sorted[0] = (2, 5) into Member 1, (8, 11) into Member 2, and (14, 18) into Member 3.

Why Marginal CRPS Changes by Exactly Zero

Inspect the bottom row of the worked table. At 1 km, both ensembles contain the exact multiset {2, 8, 14}; at 2 km, both contain {5, 11, 18}. Every univariate score—CRPS, median, 5–95% half-width, 99% coverage, and PIT rank—is symmetric with respect to member permutation, so univariate error moves by 0.000 while the fake −9 and +16 m/s/km shear cliffs collapse immediately into realistic +3 to +4 m/s/km gradients.

3.2.4

ERA5 < 2021 Template Library & Fallback Hierarchy

A Schaake shuffle is only as honest as its dependence templates. To guarantee zero test-set or validation-set leakage, group_template() in tempest/calibrate/shuffle.py:39–55 draws its $m = 199$ dependence templates exclusively from historical ERA5 training-year profiles strictly before the holdout boundary (year < before_year, i.e., ERA5 < 2021 for the 1991–2020 training archive). Neither the 2021–2023 calibration years nor the 2024–2025 test years ever enter the template library.

Compact uint8[199 × 179] Export Packing & Multi-Set Cycling (G3)

Because the calibration block size is fixed at M_CAL = 199, every rank index lies in 0..198—fitting cleanly inside a single unsigned byte (uint8, max 255). During production export (tempest/export_tool.py:143), templates are pre-ranked and stored as (n_cells, 144, 199, 179) uint8 arrays spanning 12 calendar months × 12 two-hour solar bins = 144 temporal bins, slashing disk and memory footprint by 4× compared to float32. When a user requests large Monte Carlo draws ($N \gg 199$), exporting with --post-template-sets K (G3) stores $K$ independent ERA5 < 2021 template realizations per cell and cycles each 199-member block $g$ through template set $g \bmod K$ so large launch campaigns never repeat a single copula.

Spatial Cell Blocking & Graceful Fallback Hierarchy

Storing a separate template for every $0.25^\circ$ grid point bloats exported assets, whereas pooling the entire domain erases terrain-specific wind-turning regimes (such as mountain-valley vs. high-plains boundary layers). shuffle.py therefore implements a clean three-tier spatial resolution hierarchy:

  1. $2^\circ$ Geographic Cell Blocks (Shipped Default, --post-cell 8, IL-95): Groups $8 \times 8$ adjacent $0.25^\circ$ grid points into $2^\circ \times 2^\circ$ blocks. grid_n_lon(cond) (shuffle.py:31–36) dynamically counts longitudes per latitude row—fixing bug L3-35, where a hard-wired n_lon = 29 from the $13 \times 29$ West Texas pilot box sliced the $9 \times 9$ Denver evaluation grid into mixed latitude strips.
  2. Per-Point Fallback on Irregular Grids (grid_n_lon == 0): If the dataset coordinates do not form a complete regular lat–lon rectangle, grid_n_lon() returns 0, --template-cell is ignored with a diagnostic message, and group_template() falls back automatically to per-grid-point templates.
  3. Domain-Pooled Temporal Fallback (--pooled, IL-94): Collapses keys to keys % 144 (month × 2-hour solar bin only) when ultra-compact assets or sparse regional archives are required.

Documented Edge Case: cell_keys() Stride * 100 Overflow (shuffle.py:28)

cell_keys() packs 2-D block coordinates into a single integer key using a stride of 100: 

((pid // n_lon // cell) * 100 + (pid % n_lon) // cell) * 144 + k_ % 144

Because export_tool.py:141 unpacks block coordinates via lat_i, lon_i = cells // 100, cells % 100, any continental or global $0.25^\circ$ grid containing 100 or more longitude cells (n_lon // cell >= 100, e.g., global $0.25^\circ$ with cell ≤ 14) overflows lon_i into lat_i, silently colliding templates across adjacent latitude rows.

3.2.5

Spatial Interpolation & Terrain-Aware Launch-Pad Maps

Rocket launch pads almost never sit exactly on a $0.25^\circ$ ERA5 grid vertex. A tempting shortcut is to apply spatial kriging or bilinear linear averaging directly to the generated atmospheric state vectors across the four surrounding grid points. For probabilistic ensembles, however, linearly averaging two atmospheric profiles destroys physical realism: averaging two independent wind profiles shrinks ensemble variance by up to 50%, damps fine-scale wind shear, and blurs sharp boundary-layer inversions.

Unbiased Bilinear Mixture Allocation in M_CAL = 199 Blocks

Rather than averaging profiles across space, the serving engine (tempest_weather/api.py:378–391, 511–534) constructs a spatial probability mixture of whole, physically intact columns:

  1. 4-Corner Bilinear Weights: _core.bilinear(g["lat"], g["lon"], lat, lon) identifies the four grid vertices enclosing the launch pad $(\phi, \lambda)$ and computes their exact bilinear weights $(w_1, w_2, w_3, w_4)$ summing to 1.
  2. Whole-Block Systematic Allocation (_blend_rows): Because spread.py and shuffle.py require exact M_CAL = 199 calibration blocks, each block of 199 atmospheres is assigned to one of the four surrounding grid points. To eliminate the old largest-remainder bias—which served 0.97 / 0.03 instead of 0.845 / 0.115 at Spaceport America and consistently shortchanged the northeast corner when slicing the final partial block—_blend_rows() (Audit 2026-11-14) allocates $\lfloor w_j \cdot G \rfloor$ guaranteed blocks per vertex, distributes remaining blocks via unbiased systematic sampling proportional to fractional remainders, and shuffles block order so any sliced partial block is unbiased in expectation.
  3. Terrain-Aware Elevation Alignment: Each block runs through the neural generator, spread.py, $2^\circ$-cell Schaake shuffle, and shear/lapse maps at its native grid-point elevation g["elev"][row_pt]. Only after vertical coherence is complete does _core.site_shift() (to_site from Section 3.1.6) shift temperature, hypsometric surface pressure, and dewpoint-conserving relative humidity onto the target pad elevation (user-supplied elevation, or bilinear terrain elevation $\sum_j w_j\,z_{\text{grid},j}$ when omitted), followed by exact hydrostatic density and speed-of-sound reconstruction via _core.thermo_column().

Spatial Kriging vs. Whole-Column Bilinear Mixtures

Where continuous deterministic fields are needed—such as interpolating mean terrain elevation or computing smooth station-correction weights $w_s$ via inverse-squared cosine-latitude distance in station.py (F-20)—spatial weighting works smoothly. For stochastic vertical profiles, however, allocating whole M_CAL = 199 blocks by bilinear weights guarantees that every single Monte Carlo draw retains authentic single-column shear, inversions, and calibrated spread while the ensemble's spatial expectation tracks the exact pad coordinates.

3.2.6

Shear, Lapse-Rate, Tail & Joint Envelope Maps

Even after the Schaake shuffle restores cross-height rank correlation, fine-scale vertical derivatives—vector wind shear between adjacent grid levels and environmental temperature lapse rates—can remain subtly mis-calibrated in the upper tails. Tempest-ARKNET applies two shipped high-pass derivative maps (shear_map.py and lapse_map.py) plus three specialized companion modules (shear_tail.py, joint.py, and tropo.py).

1. Shear-Space Quantile Map (tempest/calibrate/shear_map.py — Shipped ON)

shear_map.py (IL-58 / IL-89 / IL-91) operates directly in vector wind-shear space across the 43 layers separating the 44 AGL grid heights. Layers are grouped into 4 altitude bands by upper-layer height MID = HEIGHTS[1:] against EDGES = [0, 1000, 5000, 12000, 18001] m (0–1, 1–5, 5–12, and 12–18 km; or 7 bands EDGES7 via --groups 7). On a 400-point shear grid per band (GRID = 400), fit() solves for a multiplicative gain curve $g_b(s) \in [0.5, 3.0]$ across 55 upper-tail quantiles (0.50..0.99 plus 0.995, 0.997, 0.998, 0.999, 0.9995; or 103 levels from 0.02 with --full) using 3 damped iterative passes (passes = 3, damp = 0.8) to compensate for downstream high-pass attenuation:

Equation 3.2.3 — Vector Shear Quantile Gain, Monotonicity & Level-0 High-Pass Filter $$\Delta \mathbf{u}_k = \begin{pmatrix} u_{k+1} - u_k \\ v_{k+1} - v_k \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Delta z_k = \frac{z_{k+1} - z_k}{1000}\;[\text{km}], \qquad s_k = \frac{\|\Delta \mathbf{u}_k\|_2}{\Delta z_k}\;\left[\frac{\text{m/s}}{\text{km}}\right]$$ $$g_b^{(t+1)}(q_{\text{ref},j}) = g_b^{(t)}(q_{\text{ref},j}) \cdot \left(\frac{q_{\text{truth},j}}{\max(q_{\text{cur},j}^{(t)},\, 10^{-9})}\right)^{0.8}, \qquad \tilde{g}_b(s_i) = \frac{\max_{r \le i}\!\big(\operatorname{clip}(g_b(s_r),\, 0.5,\, 3.0)\cdot s_r\big)}{\max(s_i,\, 10^{-9})}$$ $$\mathbf{r} = \boldsymbol{\delta} - \operatorname{MA}_9(\boldsymbol{\delta}), \qquad \mathbf{r}^{\text{pin}} = \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}_{:,0}, \qquad \mathbf{u}^{\text{new}} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{r}^{\text{pin}}$$

Simply integrating scaled jumps $\delta_k = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \Delta\mathbf{u}_j(\tilde{g}_b(s_j) - 1)$ up the column would drift upper-level mean winds. Subtracting a 9-level moving average (uniform_filter1d(delta, SMOOTH=9, mode="nearest"), or opt-in physical-metre Gaussian smooth_m from IL-137) keeps only fine-scale roughness, while subtracting $\mathbf{r}_{:,0}$ ("Gemini bug 5" fix at shear_map.py:62) pins the 10 m pad wind strictly to its pre-map value. On radiosonde verification, this slashes vector-shear Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance from 0.044 → 0.010.

Confirmed Bug B14 — Band-Edge Inequality Mismatch (sonde.py:265 vs. shear_map.py:27)

All three band-based calibrators (shear_map.py:27, lapse_map.py:23, and shear_tail.py:58) assign layers using np.digitize(MID, [1000, 5000, 12000])—left-closed, right-open intervals [lo, hi)—so MID = 1000 enters band 1 (1–5 km). By contrast, tempest/scoring/sonde.py:265 evaluates --shear-table using (mid > lo) & (mid <= hi) (left-open, right-closed (lo, hi]), placing MID = 1000 in band 0 (0–1 km). Three exact grid boundaries—the layers ending at 1,000 m, 5,000 m, and 12,000 m—are therefore calibrated in the upper band but scored in the lower band. Note also the climate-tail caveat in shear_map.py:10: calibration years 2021–2023 exhibit a heavier 1–5 km 99.9th-percentile shear than 2024–2025 (29.2 vs. 25.7 m/s/km).

2. Lapse-Rate Quantile Map (tempest/calibrate/lapse_map.py — Shipped ON, L21b)

lapse_map.py (IL-162) is the exact thermal twin of shear_map.py, fitted immediately after the Schaake shuffle and shear map. Because environmental lapse rates $L_k = -(T_{k+1} - T_k)/\Delta z_k$ can be negative inside temperature inversions, fit() updates its 55 upper-tail quantile knots (xk, yk) additively over 3 damped passes (damp = 0.8) while keeping $f_b(L) = L$ below the band median $x_{k,0}$ and continuing with slope 1 above $x_{k,-1}$:

Equation 3.2.4 — Additive Lapse-Rate Quantile Map (Preserving Real Desert Superadiabatic Layers) $$L_k = -\frac{T_{k+1} - T_k}{\Delta z_k}\;\left[\frac{\text{K}}{\text{km}}\right], \qquad y_k^{(t+1)} = \max\!\Big(x_{k,0},\; \operatorname{cummax}\!\big(y_k^{(t)} + 0.8\,(q_{\text{truth}} - q_{\text{cur}}^{(t)})\big)\Big)$$ $$f_b(L) = \begin{cases} L, & L \le x_{k,0}\;\text{(inversions & stable layers untouched)} \\ \operatorname{interp}(L;\, x_k, y_k), & x_{k,0} < L \le x_{k,-1} \\ y_{k,-1} + (L - x_{k,-1}), & L > x_{k,-1}\;\text{(slope-1 tail continuation)} \end{cases}$$

A hard dry-adiabatic clamp ($L \le 9.8\text{ K/km}$) would erase real physics: hot desert afternoons at Spaceport America and Midland routinely sustain genuine superadiabatic surface layers below 1 km. Matching the empirical quantile curve with slope-1 continuation and applying the same SMOOTH = 9 high-pass filter + level-0 pin cuts steep layers (>9.8 K/km) from 33.8% → 23.6% (closely tracking ERA5's 21.9%) without altering pad temperature or hydrostatic density.

3. Companion Extensions: Rare Shear Patches, Joint 99% Band & Tropopause Q-Map

  • Rare Shear Patches (tempest/calibrate/shear_tail.py — Opt-In, IL-5): Inspired by Earth-GRAM 2026 §2.12.2 ("patchy severe turbulence"), targets a 6–20% residual deficit at the 99.9th-percentile neighbor shear. A Bernoulli mask selects only $p = 0.05$ of ensemble members—leaving 95% completely untouched—and adds a 2-component unit-variance AR(1)-in-height perturbation ($\rho = 0.6$) scaled by piecewise-linear $\sigma(z)$ across knots [500, 3000, 8500, 15000.5] m, with each band amplitude $\sigma_b \in [0, 8.0]\text{ m/s}$ fitted via 14-iteration bisection (--only-bands pins unwanted bands to 0).
  • Joint Simultaneous 99% Band (tempest/calibrate/joint.py — Output-Only): While $m = 199$ calibrated members provide 99% pointwise coverage at any single height, simultaneous containment across all 44 altitudes together drops to 85–93%. joint.py solves for one scalar multiplier $s_v \in [1.0, 8.0]$ per variable $v \in \{u, v, T, \text{RH}\}$ (for both known and unknown driver modes) via 25-iteration bisection (precision $7 / 2^{25} \approx 2.1 \times 10^{-7}$) and outputs band99_joint_lo/hi without ever stretching the underlying ensemble samples, fail-closed verified against both model_sha and calib_sha.
  • Tropopause-Relative Quantile Map (tempest/calibrate/tropo.py — Unwired, UR6): Fixed-AGL calibration blurs the migrating tropopause kink and co-located jet maximum (which move 4–6 km seasonally). tropo.py implements the exact WMO (1957) lapse-rate tropopause detector ($L \le 2.0\text{ K/km}$ sustained over 2 km inside $[5, 20]$ km, with coldest-level fallback) and fits nq = 99 quantile knots inside 16 relative-height bins of 500 m spanning $\Delta z_{\text{rel}} = z - z_t \in [-4000, +4000]$ m (requiring min_n = 200 pairs per bin, constant end-offset continuation beyond outer knots).
Equation 3.2.5 — AR(1) Rare Shear Patch, Joint 44-Height Multiplier & WMO 1957 Tropopause $$z_{c,j} = 0.6\,z_{c,j-1} + \sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}\,\varepsilon_{c,j}, \qquad \Delta u_j = \sigma(z_j)\,z_{u,j}, \qquad \Delta v_j = \sigma(z_j)\,z_{v,j}$$ $$s_v^\star = \inf\!\left\{s \in [1, 8] \;:\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\forall k \in \{0, \dots, 43\},\;\; \text{lo}_{n,k}(s) \le y_{n,k} \le \text{hi}_{n,k}(s)\right] \ge 0.99\right\}$$ $$z_t = \min\!\left\{z_i \in [5, 20]\text{ km} \;:\; -\frac{T_{i+1} - T_i}{\Delta z_i} \le 2.0\;\frac{\text{K}}{\text{km}} \;\;\land\;\; \forall z_j \in (z_i,\, z_i + 2\text{ km}],\;\; \frac{T_i - T_j}{(z_j - z_i)/1000} \le 2.0\;\frac{\text{K}}{\text{km}}\right\}$$
