3.4.1

Strict Temporal Holdout (1991–2026 Timeline) & Anti-Leakage Hygiene

A probabilistic atmospheric generator is only as trustworthy as the verification discipline that audits it. In rocket 6-DOF Monte Carlo dispersion, a leaky train/test split, an unfair finite-ensemble baseline, or a silently stale forecast archive can turn a benign landing-dispersion ellipse into a range-safety failure. Because atmospheric columns are strongly autocorrelated across synoptic waves (3–7 days), seasonal cycles, and multi-year ENSO/QBO regimes, random row splits leak neighboring hours of the exact same storm straight into the test set and artificially inflate apparent skill.

Tempest-ARKNET therefore enforces a strict four-era chronological partition spanning 36 years of ERA5 reanalysis and IGRA2 balloon history (1991–2026), backed by hard runtime assertions in code so that neither the 2024–2025 development test set nor the single-use 2026 holdout can ever contaminate model weights, calibration tables, or spatial domain indices.

Hard-Coded Anti-Leakage Firewalls & Driver Realism

  • 2026 Ingest Lock (tempest/ingest/igra_to_heights.py:174): Executes assert a.last_year <= 2025, "2026 is never used" and strips all stations listed in points.npz::held_igra before converting IGRA2 zip archives.
  • Debias Pre-Test Lock (tempest/scoring/debias.py:105–106): Raises SystemExit("debias must be fitted before the test years (2024-25)") if a.year >= 2024, forcing point × month × 4-hour bias tables to fit strictly on 2021–2023.
  • DI 2026 Guard (tempest/scoring/di.py:24–27): Filters d["year"] <= last_year (default 2025) so 2026 observations never enter spatial standardization.
  • Balloon Calibration & Offset Guards (tempest/scoring/sonde.py:119, 222): Enforces --station-calib-year < held_from and assert a.offset_to_year < held_from, plus --first-test-month to isolate the untouched July–October 2026 window once January–June 2026 is spent.
  • Known vs. Unknown Drivers & Warming-Aware clim15 (score.py:268–284): Every evaluation reports both known (observed ONI, WWV, QBO30, QBO50—an idealized seasonal-forecast upper bound) and unknown (analog training draws or the real-time --driver-mode ridge 6-month forecast served in production). Because 1991–2020 climatology is cooler than 2024–2025, skill15 scores against only the 15 years immediately preceding first_test_year so baseline warming is already accounted for.

21-Day Block Bootstrap (BLOCK_DAYS = 21, AU-4)

Resampling individual hours or individual days produces falsely narrow 95% confidence intervals because synoptic weather systems and stratospheric temperature anomalies persist for weeks. Empirically, the lag autocorrelation of daily model - clim15 CRPS drops below $0.1$ at lag 19 in the slowest band (T 12–18 km; all other bands drop by lags 1–7). Rounding up to whole weeks gives BLOCK_DAYS = 21 in tempest/scoring/diag.py:85–118, 131–197:

Equation 3.4.1 — Continuous 21-Day Block Indexing & Multinomial Resampling $$b(d) = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{days\_since\_1970}(d)}{21} \right\rfloor, \qquad c^{(r)} \sim \operatorname{Multinomial}\!\left(N_b,\; \frac{1}{N_b}\mathbf{1}_{N_b}\right), \quad r = 1, \dots, 1000$$

Converting year * 1000 + doy into continuous days since 1970 via day_number() ensures 21-day blocks wrap seamlessly across December 31 / January 1. Every headline metric (skill, skill15, margin_gauss, cov99) is reported as value [2.5%, 97.5%] over 1,000 block-resampled replicates.

3.4.2

CRPS, Energy Score & Variogram Score

All verification executes in physical units over the $D = 179$ output vector (44 AGL heights of $u, v, T, \mathrm{RH}$ plus surface t2m, sp, rh2m), aggregated across four altitude bands (0–1 km, 1–5 km, 5–12 km, 12–18 km) and surface. To guard against models that game marginal quantiles while destroying vertical structure—or vice versa—Tempest-ARKNET combines three complementary strictly proper scoring rules alongside an aerospace Gaussian proxy.

1. Fair Ensemble CRPS, Whole-Pool Climatology & Upper-Tail twCRPS

Plain ensemble CRPS (crps_ensemble) divides pairwise member differences by $m^2$, implicitly penalizing any finite ensemble of size $m$ by roughly $1/m$ of its spread. Comparing a 75-row empirical climatology pool against 199 model draws under plain CRPS creates an artificial handicap for the smaller pool. Since AU-1 (--fair-baseline, default True), tempest/scoring/score.py:48–78 scores every model and baseline with the unbiased $O(m \log m)$ sorted Fair CRPS (Ferro 2014), evaluates climatology over the entire sorted group pool $P$ of size $R$ without replacement (pool_scores, falling back to p_all when clim15 has $R < 20$ rows), and isolates rocket-scrubbing extremes via the 99th-percentile Threshold-Weighted CRPS (--tail, IL-107):

Equation 3.4.2 — Fair Ensemble CRPS, Whole-Pool Skill & Upper-Tail twCRPS $$\operatorname{CRPS}_{\text{fair}}(x, y) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m} |x_{(i)} - y| \;-\; \frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m} (2i - m - 1)\,x_{(i)}$$ $$\operatorname{Skill} = 1 - \frac{\overline{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\text{model}}}{\overline{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\text{pool}}}, \qquad \operatorname{twCRPS}(x, y;\, t) = \operatorname{CRPS}\!\big(\max(x, t),\; \max(y, t)\big), \quad t = Q_{0.99}(\text{clim15})$$

Sorting $x_{(1)} \le \dots \le x_{(m)}$ evaluates all $\binom{m}{2}$ pairwise absolute differences in linear time via $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}|x_i - x_j| = \sum_{i=1}^{m}(2i - m - 1)x_{(i)}$. Clamping both forecast and truth at $t = Q_{0.99}(\text{clim15})$ zeroes out calm-bulk differences while remaining strictly proper on the upper 1% tail (reported in <stem>_tail.csv for 10 m, 100 m, 300 m, 1 km, 3 km wind speeds and 10 m–1 km vector shear).

2. Multivariate Whole-Column Energy Score (mc.py)

Univariate CRPS scores each of the 179 output dimensions marginally and is completely blind to joint cross-variable and cross-altitude correlation. The Energy Score generalizes Fair CRPS to $\mathbb{R}^D$ using Euclidean norms over standardized whole-column vectors $\tilde{x}^{(i)}, \tilde{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ (computed in tempest/scoring/mc.py stratified by season × day/night):

Equation 3.4.3 — Unbiased Multivariate Energy Score (Gneiting & Raftery 2007) $$\operatorname{ES}_{\text{fair}}(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big\|\tilde{x}^{(i)} - \tilde{y}\big\|_{2} \;-\; \frac{1}{2 m(m - 1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j \neq i}\big\|\tilde{x}^{(i)} - \tilde{x}^{(j)}\big\|_{2}$$

Lower is better. Notice that for $D = 1$, $\operatorname{ES}_{\text{fair}}$ reduces algebraically to $\operatorname{CRPS}_{\text{fair}}$. Standardizing each column by training standard deviation prevents high-variance variables (like surface pressure in Pascals) from drowning out boundary-layer wind components in the Euclidean norm.

3. Variogram Score ($p = 0.5$, Scheuerer & Hamill 2015)

While the Energy Score is strictly proper in $\mathbb{R}^D$, its Euclidean norm is famously insensitive to subtle mis-specifications in vertical correlation between adjacent heights. variogram_score() (score.py:91–99) directly audits vertical profile geometry across all $\binom{44}{2}$ altitude pairs per variable ($u, v, T, \mathrm{RH}$) in 200-row chunks:

Equation 3.4.4 — Power-0.5 Vertical Variogram Score & Skill $$\operatorname{VS}_{p}(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{h_1=1}^{H}\sum_{h_2=1}^{H}\left(|y_{h_1} - y_{h_2}|^{p} - \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x_{i,h_1} - x_{i,h_2}|^{p}\right)^{2}, \qquad p = 0.5, \qquad \operatorname{VS\_Skill} = 1 - \frac{\overline{\operatorname{VS}}_{\text{model}}}{\overline{\operatorname{VS}}_{\text{clim}}}$$

Using $p = 0.5$ down-weights isolated outliers while remaining sharply sensitive to scrambled vertical covariance and artificial inter-layer jumps. vs_skill directly quantifies the structural lift provided by Schaake shuffle, shear_map, and lapse_map.

4. Earth-GRAM Cholesky Gaussian Proxy (gaussian_samples & gauss15_samples, AU-7)

Legacy aerospace dispersion suites such as NASA Earth-GRAM and Range Reference Atmospheres model variability as a monthly mean plus a correlated multivariate Gaussian. To benchmark Tempest-ARKNET directly against that industry baseline, score.py:102–126, 252–262 fits the full $179 \times 179$ empirical correlation matrix per grid point × month × 2-hour bin (falling back to month-only if a bin has $< 600$ rows, or $< 300$ for gauss15, capped at 3,000 rows) and samples via Cholesky factorization:

Equation 3.4.5 — Correlated Gaussian Proxy & Non-Gaussian Margin $$L L^{\top} = \operatorname{Corr}\!\left(\frac{X - \mu}{\sigma + 10^{-9}}\right) + 10^{-6}\,I_D, \qquad x = \operatorname{clip\_ranges}\!\Big(\mu + (z L^{\top}) \odot \sigma\Big), \quad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$$ $$\operatorname{margin\_gauss} = \frac{\overline{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\text{gauss}} - \overline{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\text{model}}}{\overline{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\text{clim15}}}$$

Enabling --gauss15 (AU-7) fits the Gaussian proxy on the recent 15-year window, cleanly separating Tempest-ARKNET's non-Gaussian distributional shape advantage (skewed wind speeds, bimodal inversions, bounded humidity) from background climate warming.

3.4.3

PIT Histograms, Order-Statistic Coverage & Spread-Skill Ratio (SSR)

A low CRPS alone does not guarantee that an ensemble's 99% outer safety bounds are calibrated—a sharp but slightly under-dispersed model can achieve competitive bulk CRPS while missing 3% of real launch-day extremes instead of 1%. Tempest-ARKNET therefore pairs proper scoring rules with three sharp calibration diagnostics: exact order-statistic coverage, randomized PIT histograms, and the finite-$m$-corrected Spread-Skill Ratio (SSR).

Exact Order-Statistic Coverage & Why M_CAL = 199

Interpolated sample quantiles systematically underestimate finite-ensemble coverage—for example, an idealized $m = 100$ ensemble achieves only $0.97$ on an interpolated 99% interval. coverage90(x, y, p) (score.py:81–88) evaluates exact order statistics in $O(m)$ time via np.partition:

Equation 3.4.6 — Exact Finite-Ensemble Order-Statistic Coverage $$k = \max\!\left(1,\; \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{1 - p}{2}(m + 1)\right)\right), \qquad \operatorname{Cov}_p(x, y) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[x_{(k)} \le y \le x_{(m + 1 - k)}\right]$$

Under an exchangeable calibrated ensemble, the truth's rank is uniform on $\{1, \dots, m + 1\}$, giving exact nominal coverage $\frac{m + 1 - 2k}{m + 1}$. Standardizing on M_CAL = 199 makes $m + 1 = 200$, so $\frac{1 - p}{2}(m + 1)$ is an exact integer for both $p = 0.90$ ($k = 10 \Rightarrow 180/200 = 0.900$) and $p = 0.99$ ($k = 1 \Rightarrow 198/200 = 0.990$, spanning minimum to maximum member). Companion diagnostics in diag.py:14–29 track one-sided misses (miss_lo, miss_hi, target $0.005$ each) and tail_support() (flagging quantile estimates with $< 10$ expected exceedances).

Randomized PIT Histograms & Total-Variation Distance (pit_tv)

The Probability Integral Transform (PIT) checks calibration across the entire cumulative distribution, not just a single central interval. For bounded variables like relative humidity (which exhibits point-mass atoms at 0% and 100%), naive rank histograms produce artificial spikes at the boundaries. metrics_ext.py computes a randomized PIT using uniform tie-breaking $u_n \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1)$ across equal members, then summarizes deviation from uniformity over $B$ histogram bins via Total Variation distance:

Equation 3.4.7 — Randomized PIT & Total-Variation Calibration Error $$\operatorname{PIT}_n = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{1}[x_{n,i} < y_n] + u_n \left(1 + \sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{1}[x_{n,i} = y_n]\right)}{m + 1}, \qquad \operatorname{PIT\_TV} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\left|\hat{p}_b - \frac{1}{B}\right|$$

A perfectly calibrated ensemble yields a flat $\operatorname{Uniform}(0, 1)$ PIT histogram ($\operatorname{PIT\_TV} \to 0$). A U-shaped histogram diagnoses under-dispersion (too many truths falling outside the ensemble envelope); a dome-shaped histogram diagnoses over-dispersion; and a monotonically tilted histogram diagnoses systematic mean bias.

Finite-$m$-Corrected Spread-Skill Ratio (ss_ratio) & Shape Diagnostics

In a calibrated ensemble, the root-mean-square ensemble spread should match the root-mean-square error of the ensemble mean. For finite $m$, the sample variance of $m$ draws underestimates the expected squared error of the ensemble mean by a factor of $\frac{m}{m + 1}$. diag.py therefore applies the exact Fortin et al. (2014) finite-ensemble correction:

Equation 3.4.8 — Finite-Ensemble Spread-Skill Ratio (SSR) $$\operatorname{SSR} = \sqrt{\frac{m + 1}{m}}\,\frac{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} s_n^2}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} (\bar{x}_n - y_n)^2}}, \qquad s_n^2 = \frac{1}{m - 1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(x_{n,i} - \bar{x}_n)^2$$

Ideal $\operatorname{SSR} = 1.0$: values $< 1$ indicate under-dispersion (overconfident ensemble) and values $> 1$ indicate over-dispersion. metrics_ext.py rounds out distributional diagnostics with robust standardized skewness/kurtosis, half-weight-tie tail_exceedance (eliminating RH 0%/100% atom artefacts), event_conditional_coverage (comparing against a pseudo-truth member), vertical_corr_error, KS/Wasserstein shear_distance, drift_proxy, and direction_error (evaluated where truth wind speed $> 3\text{ m/s}$).

3.4.4

Brier Skill Score for Launch Go/No-Go Gates & Whole-Column MC Audits

On launch day, range safety officers do not make decisions from raw continuous densities—they evaluate binary Go/No-Go threshold gates (e.g., 10 m pad wind exceeding 10 m/s, 0–6 km bulk shear exceeding vehicle structural limits, or rail-exit crosswind exceeding stability bounds). To measure whether Tempest-ARKNET's ensemble exceedance probabilities $p_n = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{1}[g(x_{n,i}) > \tau]$ are both sharp and honest relative to climatological base rates $\bar{o}$, metrics_ext.py computes the Brier Score (BS) and Brier Skill Score (BSS):

Equation 3.4.9 — Brier Score, Brier Skill Score & Murphy Decomposition $$\operatorname{BS} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(p_n - o_n)^2, \qquad \operatorname{BSS} = 1 - \frac{\operatorname{BS}_{\text{model}}}{\operatorname{BS}_{\text{clim}}}, \qquad o_n = \mathbf{1}[g(y_n) > \tau]$$ $$\operatorname{BS} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_k (p_k - \bar{o}_k)^2}_{\text{Reliability (REL, lower better)}} \;-\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_k (\bar{o}_k - \bar{o})^2}_{\text{Resolution (RES, higher better)}} \;+\; \underbrace{\bar{o}(1 - \bar{o})}_{\text{Uncertainty (UNC)}}$$

$\operatorname{BSS} = 1$ denotes a perfect deterministic oracle, $\operatorname{BSS} = 0$ equals climatological base-rate guessing, and $\operatorname{BSS} < 0$ indicates harmful probability calibration. Murphy's decomposition separates reliability (whether a predicted 30% scrub probability actually scrubs 30% of the time) from resolution (the model's ability to separate genuinely calm days from genuinely hazardous days).

A 6-DOF rocket trajectory simulator consumes an entire 44-level profile as a single continuous physical environment. mc.py:40–158 (--mc, GLOBAL_PLAN §9) audits every individual ensemble member as a whole column against ERA5 truth thresholds across six test families:

3.4.5

Radiosonde Verification Harness, Regime Stress Matrix & Bugs B4 / B14

ERA5 reanalysis is smooth by construction; the ultimate ground-truth audit is scoring against real weather balloons from the Integrated Global Radiosonde Archive v2 (IGRA2) at Santa Teresa, New Mexico (EPZ) and Midland, Texas (MAF). tempest/scoring/sonde.py evaluates 00Z launches (~17 h local solar time, default; optional --hour 12 for 12Z / ~05 h night) directly against same-station, same-month historical balloon climatology and Gaussian baselines.

Elevation Alignment, Partial Soundings (AU-6) & Sensor-Shift Offsets

  • Grid-to-Site Elevation Shift (sonde.py:206, 215): Queries the generator and applies grid-level calibration at the nearest ERA5 grid cell elevation z_grid, then shifts surface pressure, temperature (with $e^{-z/1500}$ decay), and liquid-water RH to the exact balloon launch pad elevation via to_site(draw(...), z_grid, ELEV[sid]).
  • Partial Soundings (--partial, AU-6, lines 35–57): Many real balloons burst before reaching 18 km AGL. Rather than discarding every incomplete ascent, low_complete(phys, top=5000.0) retains soundings whose $u, v, T$ up to <= 5 km AGL and all three surface fields are finite. Per-column validity masks (cm) exclude missing upper levels from CRPS and coverage averages, while fill_invalid() patches missing heights in climatology draws from other valid pool soundings. Gaussian baselines, variogram scores, and whole-profile 99% coverage remain restricted to complete soundings F (under --partial, sonde.py falls back to plain baseline and disallows --offset-from-year, --station-calib-year, --shear-table, and --extras).
  • Post-Shift Offset & Station Recalibration: Supports --offset-from-year / --offset-to-year (median residual shift per variable × band for the 2022–2023 radiosonde instrument upgrade) and --station-calib-year (balloon spread table), both strictly asserted to fit before held_from.

Confirmed Bugs B4 & B14 in tempest/scoring/sonde.py

  • Bug B4 — Monthly Shift Crash & Inverted Stack Order (sonde.py:134–158 vs. score.py:301–355): Line 140 calls shift_mod.apply(_sh, x) without passing launch month month=mo, immediately raising TypeError on any --by-month (12, D) table.   • Bug B14 — Shear Band-Edge Inequality Mismatch (sonde.py:265 vs. shear_map.py:27): When building the --shear-table quantile summary, sonde.py:265 bins layer upper endpoints mid = HEIGHTS[1:] with left-open, right-closed (mid > lo) & (mid <= hi), whereas shear_map.py:27, lapse_map, and shear_tail all use half-open (mid >= lo) & (mid < hi). Boundary layers sitting exactly on 1000 m, 5000 m, and 12000 m therefore fall into different height bands during calibration than during radiosonde shear verification.
3.4.6

Spatial Domain Guard (DI / AOA), Fail-Closed Ops & Promotion Criteria

1. Unit-Sphere Dissimilarity Index & Area of Applicability (di.py)

Neural weather generators can silently degrade when queried outside the geographic and topographic envelope they were trained on (such as evaluating a West Texas / New Mexico model on Denver's Front Range). Following Meyer & Pebesma (2021) (LOOP3 L3-28), tempest/scoring/di.py projects every grid point (lat, lon, elev_site, elev_grid) onto a 5-D unit-sphere + elevation coordinate $\psi(p)$ with a single pooled standard deviation $s_{xyz}$ across $(x, y, z)$ so the sphere remains isotropic without $\pm 180^\circ$ antimeridian seams or high-latitude longitude distortion:

Equation 3.4.10 — Unit-Sphere Dissimilarity Index & AOA Threshold (di.py) $$\psi(p) = \big(\cos\phi\cos\lambda,\; \cos\phi\sin\lambda,\; \sin\phi,\; z_{\text{site}},\; z_{\text{grid}}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^5, \qquad s_{xyz} = \sqrt{s_x^2 + s_y^2 + s_z^2}$$ $$\bar{d}_{\text{nn}} = \frac{1}{P}\sum_{i=1}^{P}\min_{j \neq i}\|z_i - z_j\|_2, \qquad \operatorname{DI}(p) = \frac{\min_{i}\|z(p) - z_i\|_2}{\bar{d}_{\text{nn}}}, \qquad \operatorname{AOA}_{\text{thr}} = \max_{i}\frac{\min_{j \neq i}\|z_i - z_j\|_2}{\bar{d}_{\text{nn}}}$$

Training grid points sit at $\operatorname{DI} \approx 1$ from their nearest neighbors (and $\operatorname{DI} = 0$ on themselves). Any query location with $\operatorname{DI}(p) > \operatorname{AOA}_{\text{thr}}$ is flagged as outside the Area of Applicability, warning operators that uncertainty calibration is extrapolating spatially or topographically. Time and climate drivers are intentionally excluded from LOC because future test years lie outside training years everywhere.

2. Fail-Closed 12 h & 24 h Forecast Archiving (ops/logger.py & ops/logger_p4.py)

Open-Meteo does not serve historical ensemble members for ecmwf_ifs025 (51 members) or google_weathernext2_ensemble (64 members), and retains ecmwf_ec46 (51 members × 46 daily surface days) only from September 2025 onward—any day not archived live is lost forever. Both loggers therefore run strictly fail-closed: requests use exponential backoff (honoring HTTP 429 Retry-After up to 600 s; failing immediately on HTTP 400/404), validate every payload in memory, compress to <out>.part, and commit via atomic os.replace(out + ".part", out) so a truncated transfer can never masquerade as a valid slot.

  • 12 h Ensemble Logger (logger.py): Rounds UTC time to 00z/12z slots (data/ops/raw/<site>/<model>/<YYYYMMDD>_<HH>z.json.gz), requiring >= 240 hourly steps (10 days), exact member counts (51 or 64), all first 72 hours present and within physical RANGES, and bad / members <= 0.10 (MAX_BAD_MEMBER_FRAC), while exempting WeatherNext 2's all-null OPTIONAL fields (relative_humidity_2m, wind_gusts_10m).
  • 24 h Sub-Seasonal Logger (logger_p4.py): Archives ecmwf_ec46 daily (<YYYYMMDD>.json.gz) across 8 daily surface fields, requiring >= 40 forecast days, 51 members, and <= 10% sparse members. Every attempt appends a UTC line (stored, already stored, skipped, or REJECTED) to data/ops/logger.log with a 2 s inter-request pause.

3. Archive Staleness, SHA-256 Parity (ops/health.py) & Promotion Criteria

python -m tempest.ops.health (health.py:23–102) serves as the automated watchdog for both live forecast archives and exported NumPy serving bundles, returning exit code 0 only when every check passes (with optional --json telemetry):

  • Staleness Thresholds: 12 h ensembles are nominal at <= 12 h, warned stale at 12–36 h, and hard-failed (do not use, exit 1) at > 36 h; 24 h ecmwf_ec46 archives fail at > 60 h.
  • 14-Day Slot & 3-Day Gap Scan: Audits every expected 12 h / 24 h slot over the trailing 14 days (allowing a 6 h arrival buffer). Any missing slot within the last 3 days sets recent_gap = True and fails health alongside 3-day stored / REJECTED log tallies.
  • SHA-256 manifest.json Parity (assets_check()): Streams every weight and calibration file listed in tempest_weather/assets/manifest.json through hashlib.file_digest(f, "sha256"), failing closed on any missing or mutated asset.
  • Candidate Promotion Criteria: No architecture, loss weight, or post-processor promotes to Shipped ON unless it passes (1) strict pre-2026 leakage hygiene, (2) 21-day block-bootstrapped skill15 and margin_gauss gains without regressing upper-tail twCRPS or vs_skill, (3) calibrated cov99 within tolerance across the 36-cell stress.py matrix and IGRA2 sonde.py balloons, and (4) cross-year parameter stability (the exact gate that killed tailcal.py).
