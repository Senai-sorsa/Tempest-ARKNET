2.1.1

Why single-step Engression over diffusion

At the core of Tempest-ARKNET sits a 2.8-million-parameter conditional Engression network (tempest/generator/model.py::Generator) that maps site coordinates, calendar phase, local solar time, and slowly varying global climate indices directly into a complete 44-height vertical atmospheric profile ($D = 179$ physical values) in a single forward pass. Before inspecting a single weight matrix, it helps to ask the most fundamental architectural question: why choose one-shot Engression instead of ordinary regression or a modern diffusion / flow-matching model?

Picture a concrete launch-planning scenario over Spaceport America in mid-June at 10 km AGL (roughly the cruising altitude of commercial airliners and the upper-tropospheric shear zone for high-altitude sounding rockets). Synoptic weather at that altitude is genuinely bimodal. Roughly half the time, a subtropical jet streak drives strong westerly flow at +20 m/s East; the other half, an upper-level monsoon ridge reverses the flow to −10 m/s West. A 6-DOF rocket Monte Carlo simulation needs crisp, physically valid atmospheric draws from both regimes—complete with their sharp vertical wind-shear sheets—never a fictional average sky.

Standard MSE Regression Predicts the conditional mean +5 m/s East—a blurred, unreal sky that almost never occurs in June and systematically underestimates aerodynamic fin loads. 1 forward pass (deterministic; cannot sample dispersion ensembles) Collapses multi-modal regimes into averages and erases thin jet-stream shear layers; useless for Monte Carlo dispersion.

Why Engression wins for launch-site physics

Unlike GANs (which suffer from adversarial mode collapse and fragile Nash equilibria) or multi-step diffusion models (which accumulate ODE integration drift and multiply CPU serving latency by 8–50×), Engression trains a single stochastic transport map $g_\theta(c, \varepsilon)$ by minimizing a strictly proper scoring rule across $m$ parallel draws per training row. Because the loss simultaneously rewards marginal accuracy against the observed atmosphere and pairwise diversity across independent draws, the generator cannot cheat by retreating to the +5 m/s mean—yet still executes in a single matrix-multiply pass at the launch pad.

2.1.2

Pre-additive noise injection theory

A natural first instinct when turning a deterministic neural network into a generative model is post-additive noise: predict a deterministic mean $\hat{\mu}(c)$ and add a Gaussian perturbation $\sigma(c)\,\varepsilon$ at the output layer. That approach fails immediately on atmospheric columns. Post-additive Gaussian noise can only produce symmetric, unimodal bell curves around a single mean profile; it can never bend a smooth mean into two distinct synoptic weather regimes, nor can it couple a nonlinear temperature inversion at 800 m with a sharp wind-direction reversal at 10 km.

Engression (Shen & Meinshausen, 2024) solves this through pre-additive noise injection. Instead of adding noise after the nonlinearity, an independent 32-dimensional standard normal vector $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{32})$ enters before the nonlinear layers—both concatenated into the input projection and broadcast as a learned modulation into every hidden block:

Equation 2.1.1 · Conditional Engression Transport Map $$\hat{y}^{(i)} = g_\theta\!\left(c,\, \varepsilon^{(i)}\right), \qquad \varepsilon^{(i)} \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, I_{32}), \qquad i = 1, \dots, m$$
Plain-English intuition: Think of $c$ as the stage setting (where on Earth you are, what season and solar hour it is, and what El Niño / QBO phase the planet is in) and $\varepsilon^{(i)}$ as a 32-number weather dice roll. Because $\varepsilon^{(i)}$ passes through four nonlinear SiLU layers, the network can warp a simple isotropic Gaussian ball into arbitrarily skewed, heavy-tailed, or bimodal atmospheric distributions—while preserving exact vertical physical correlations across all 179 outputs.

Cyclic calendar phase, local solar hour & the elev_site == elev_grid contract

Raw day-of-year jumps from 365 back to 1, and raw UTC hour misaligns afternoon solar heating across longitudes. In features.py:34–38, both time coordinates are projected smoothly onto the unit circle using local solar time:

Equation 2.1.2 · Cyclic Seasonal & Local Solar Phase $$a = \frac{2\pi \cdot \mathrm{doy}}{365.25}, \qquad \mathrm{solar\_hour} = \left(\mathrm{utc\_hour} + \frac{\mathrm{lon}}{15}\right) \bmod 24, \qquad h = \frac{2\pi \cdot \mathrm{solar\_hour}}{24.0}$$
Why these exact constants? Dividing day-of-year by 365.25 absorbs leap-year phase drift over a 35-year archive. Shifting UTC by lon / 15 (since Earth rotates $360^\circ / 24\text{ h} = 15^\circ\text{/h}$) guarantees that solar noon ($\cos h = -1$) aligns with peak convective boundary-layer heating everywhere across the 7°-wide West Texas / New Mexico domain and Denver corridor. For future climate queries when ENSO/QBO states are unknown, features.drivers() co-samples all active DRIVER_NAMES jointly from one historical row so cross-index physics (such as Warm Water Volume leading ONI) remain intact.

Equally subtle is the terrain-elevation contract. Every ERA5 training row lives on a 0.25° grid cell where elev_site == elev_grid. Because the neural network never saw a nonzero difference $\Delta z = z_{\text{site}} - z_{\text{grid}}$ during training, passing a launch pad’s exact elevation directly into elev_site at query time forces ungrounded out-of-distribution extrapolation. Instead, features.py enforces a strict rule: always pass elev_site = elev_grid into the network, then apply the analytical hydrostatic shift sample.py::to_site() (mirrored in tempest_weather/_core.py::site_shift()) after generation:

Equation 2.1.3 · Post-Generation Analytical Site Elevation Shift (to_site) $$p_s' = p_s \exp\!\left(\frac{-g_0\,\Delta z}{R_d(t_{2\mathrm{m}} - \Gamma\,\Delta z / 2)}\right), \qquad T'(z) = T(z) - \Gamma\,\Delta z\,e^{-z/1500}, \qquad \mathrm{RH}'(z) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\mathrm{RH}(z)\frac{e_{s,l}(T)}{e_{s,l}(T')},\, 0,\, 100\right)$$
Physical meaning: $\Gamma = 0.0065\text{ K/m}$ is the standard tropospheric lapse rate, and $e^{-z/1500}$ smoothly tapers the local terrain temperature offset back into the free-atmosphere column over a 1.5 km scale height. Multiplying relative humidity by the Tetens saturation vapor-pressure ratio $e_{s,l}(T)/e_{s,l}(T')$ conserves the air parcel’s true moisture mixing ratio as temperature adjusts.
2.1.3

AdaLN / FiLM conditioning mechanics

If you only concatenate the 32-D noise vector $\varepsilon$ at the input layer of a deep residual network, later layers can easily wash out or ignore the noise signal while memorizing the deterministic conditioning features. Tempest-ARKNET prevents this by adopting the clean, hardware-friendly residual MLP blueprint validated by ECMWF AIFS-CRPS and DeepMind FGN: a single 32-D draw $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{32})$ is passed once through a compact noise-embedding MLP to produce $z_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$, and that same $z_\varepsilon$ modulates every residual block via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN / FiLM).

Step-by-step trace of the four core modules

  1. Noise Embedding MLP (self.embed): Maps $\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times m \times 32}$ through Linear(32 → 128) → SiLU → Linear(128 → 128) → LayerNorm(128) to produce $z_\varepsilon$. Because every residual block reads the exact same $z_\varepsilon$, a single latent draw coordinates wind, temperature, and humidity fluctuations consistently from 10 m AGL all the way to 18 km AGL.
  2. Input Projection (self.inp): Concatenates the non-linearized trunk conditioning vector $c_{\text{keep}}$ (8-D on BASE_FEATURES) with the raw 32-D noise $\varepsilon$, projecting Linear(40 → 512) directly into the initial residual state $h_0$. This gives the trunk immediate joint access to location/season context and stochastic coordinates from layer 0.
  3. 4× Pre-Norm Residual Blocks (Block): Each block applies an affine-free LayerNorm(512, elementwise_affine=False), modulates the normalized hidden state with per-channel scale $s_\ell(z_\varepsilon)$ and shift $b_\ell(z_\varepsilon)$ produced by self.film: Linear(128 → 1024) (split into two 512-D halves), and runs the modulated vector through a two-layer feedforward block FF: Linear(512 → 512) → SiLU → Linear(512 → 512) before adding back to the residual skip connection.
  4. Output Head (self.head): Applies a final LayerNorm(512) → Linear(512 → 179) producing normalized vertical-difference targets: 44 entries each for $(u, v, T, \text{RH/lnd})$ plus 3 surface scalars $(t_{2\mathrm{m}}, \Delta p_s, \text{rh}_{2\mathrm{m}})$, where surface pressure is centered around standard_pressure(elev_grid).
Equation 2.1.4 · Pre-Norm AdaLN / FiLM Residual Block Update $$h_{\ell+1} = h_\ell + \mathrm{FF}_\ell\!\Big(\mathrm{LN}(h_\ell) \odot \big(1 + s_\ell(z_\varepsilon)\big) + b_\ell(z_\varepsilon)\Big), \qquad \big[s_\ell(z_\varepsilon),\, b_\ell(z_\varepsilon)\big] = W_{\text{film},\ell}\,z_\varepsilon + c_{\text{film},\ell}$$
Why affine-free LayerNorm + FiLM? Standard LayerNorm learns a single static gain $\gamma$ and shift $\beta$ for every input. Removing those static parameters (elementwise_affine=False) and replacing them with $(1 + s_\ell(z_\varepsilon))$ and $b_\ell(z_\varepsilon)$ turns each block into a noise-steered dimmer switch: different weather dice rolls amplify or suppress specific hidden atmospheric modes (such as jet-streak shear or nocturnal boundary-layer inversions) cleanly at every depth.
2.1.4

Zero-initialized residual blocks

Deep generative MLPs with multiplicative noise modulation are notoriously delicate at initialization: if random initial FiLM scales $s_\ell(z_\varepsilon)$ swing wildly between $-3$ and $+3$ at step 0, they scramble the normalized activations inside every block and inject massive gradient variance before the trunk has even learned the mean seasonal cycle. Tempest-ARKNET eliminates this failure mode with a strict architectural discipline: every stochastic modulation layer and every optional structural head is zero-initialized.

Why zero-initialize self.film in model.py:54–55?

At optimization step 0, both self.film.weight and self.film.bias are exact zeros. Consequently, $s_\ell(z_\varepsilon) = 0$ and $b_\ell(z_\varepsilon) = 0$, so the AdaLN modulation reduces identically to $\mathrm{LN}(h_\ell) \odot (1 + 0) + 0 = \mathrm{LN}(h_\ell)$—a completely calm, well-conditioned pre-norm residual network. During early steps, the trunk rapidly locks onto the climatological backbone, and proper-scoring-rule gradients smoothly dial in noise-conditioned spread only along the directions and altitudes where real atmospheric variance demands it.

The same zero-init principle governs every optional extension in tempest/generator/model.py::Generator. All extensions are gated off by default in the shipped configs/generator_c4w.toml, and because their output projections start at exact zero, any ablation begins at step 0 with the exact same loss trajectory as the production baseline:

2.1.5

Linear climate-trend bypass head

Across a 30-year ERA5 training archive (1991–2020), hourly sampling produces millions of rows—creating the illusion of massive statistical degrees of freedom. For slowly varying global climate drivers (year, oni, wwv, qbo30, qbo50), that illusion is a trap. Monthly ENSO and stratospheric QBO indices update only once per month and oscillate over multi-year cycles, yielding roughly ~35 effectively independent climate states across the entire 30-year archive.

If those five scalars enter the 512-wide nonlinear MLP trunk, the network immediately falls into a monthly-fingerprinting memorization trap: the 4-tuple (oni, wwv, qbo30, qbo50) uniquely fingerprints almost every individual calendar month from 1991 to 2020. Rather than learning generalizable atmospheric physics, the high-capacity trunk memorizes each specific month’s synoptic weather pattern. In Tempest-ARKNET’s ablations, allowing climate indices into the nonlinear trunk caused held-out validation error to degrade within a single training epoch.

To capture genuine multi-decadal warming and ENSO/QBO jet-stream teleconnections while making month-memorization mathematically impossible, Generator strips linear_cols out of keep_cols completely and routes them through an additive, strictly linear bypass whose sensitivity slope is predicted by a compact zero-initialized MLP (self.slope: n_slope → 128 → SiLU → 128 → n_out × K):

Equation 2.1.5 · Linear Climate-Trend Bypass Head $$\hat{y}(c, \varepsilon) = \mathrm{Head}(h_L) + \sum_{k \in \mathrm{linear\_cols}} x_k\,\mathrm{Slope}_k(c_{\mathrm{keep}})$$
Why this guarantees clean out-of-sample extrapolation: $\mathrm{Slope}_k$ sees only c_keep (latitude, longitude, elevation, seasonal phase, local solar hour)—never the climate drivers $x_k$ themselves. It can smoothly learn that a winter El Niño (oni > 0) strengthens the 12 km subtropical jet over New Mexico, or that rising year / gmst warms the troposphere while cooling the lower stratosphere, yet it is structurally incapable of carving out nonlinear lookup tables for specific historical years. Zero-initializing the final layer of self.slope ensures the bypass contributes zero at step 0 and adds only trends supported by held-out validation data.

Empirical confirmation: Why nonlinear driver interactions failed (Tier 4.1 · F-14 & IL-97)

Two experimental relaxations in model.py:132–133 tested whether adding driver-by-driver interactions would improve teleconnection skill: F-14 (interactions=True, letting self.slope see the driver columns themselves) and IL-97 (product_pairs, appending explicit bilinear products such as oni × qbo30 and oni × qbo50 to $x$). Both degraded out-of-sample generalization because ~35 independent climate states cannot constrain quadratic interaction surfaces without overfitting. The shipped configs/generator_c4w.toml keeps both strictly disabled (interactions=False, product_pairs=None).

Confirmed Code Bug B11 · Hardcoded FEATURES.index("year") breaks on P2 datasets

Both tempest/generator/build_wdata.py:52 and tempest/generator/s25_models.py hardcode FEATURES.index("year") to reconstruct timestamps or exclude same-year analogs. When a dataset is built with --features p2—which replaces "year" with 60-month trailing "gmst" in P2_FEATURES—both modules immediately crash with ValueError: 'year' is not in list. Any vertical-wind (w-head) or Section 25 analog pipeline running on P2 datasets must read the standalone d["year"] metadata array instead.

2.1.6

Bimodal jet-streak representation

Returning to our opening Spaceport America mid-June puzzle: how does a single deterministic weight matrix inside Generator represent a bimodal upper-tropospheric wind field—switching cleanly between a +20 m/s subtropical westerly jet streak and a −10 m/s monsoon ridge reversal—without blurring them into a fictional +5 m/s mean?

The answer lies in the partnership between deep pre-additive noise modulation and proper-scoring-rule training (detailed in Chapter 2.2):

  • Nonlinear regime partitioning in 32-D latent space: Because $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{32})$ steers the AdaLN scale and shift $(s_\ell, b_\ell)$ across all four 512-wide SiLU blocks, the network partitions the 32-D Gaussian sphere into distinct synoptic basins. Draws landing in one half-space of $\varepsilon$ route through activated channels that assemble a deep westerly jet streak with its matching thermal-wind temperature gradient; draws in the opposite basin assemble an easterly monsoon ridge with its own coherent moisture and shear structure.
  • Synchronized vertical coherence via shared $z_\varepsilon$: Because all four residual blocks read the exact same 128-D noise embedding $z_\varepsilon$ and output the entire 179-D column simultaneously in telescoping difference space, the generator never accidentally splices a +20 m/s jet at 10 km onto a calm monsoon temperature profile at 8 km. Every draw is a self-consistent vertical atmosphere.
  • Heavy-tail and shear-sheet sharpness: When an atmosphere exhibits rare, sharp jet boundaries or nocturnal low-level jets, the 4-member ensemble splits requested Monte Carlo draws evenly across all four independently seeded networks (so epistemic uncertainty across weight initializations complements aleatoric spread across $\varepsilon$). Optional zero-init heads (noise_t Student-$t$ channels and jump $\tanh$ vertical steps) further extend this mechanism when testing extreme shear regimes.

Operational takeaway for 6-DOF rocket dispersion

In a Monte Carlo launch campaign, bimodal jet representation means your simulated trajectories naturally split into realistic downrange drift clusters (eastward drift under the jet streak vs. westward drift under the monsoon ridge) rather than falsely concentrating inside a calm +5 m/s bullseye that never happens in June.

2.1.7

Training hyperparameters & stability

In tempest/generator/train.py (configured by the shipped configs/generator_c4w.toml), Tempest-ARKNET trains a 4-member deep ensemble side-by-side inside a single GPU loop. Uploading the normalized training dataset to VRAM once and stepping four independent seeds (seed + k, $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$) simultaneously amortizes memory bandwidth and completes all four networks in roughly 13 minutes on an RTX 5070 Laptop GPU (~125 ms per 4-member step).

  • Batching, draws & learning-rate schedule: Each optimization step samples batch = 4096 atmospheric rows and draws m = 4 parallel latent noise vectors per row (matching ECMWF AIFS-CRPS). Optimization runs for steps = 6000 using fused AdamW(lr = 1e-3, weight_decay = 1e-4) with cosine learning-rate decay smoothly annealing to zero.
  • Independent RNG & variance-locked validation: Each ensemble member owns a dedicated GPU torch.Generator so minibatch orderings decorrelate across seeds. Every eval_every = 500 steps, validation executes inside torch.random.fork_rng with fixed seed seed + 12345 so early stopping (patience = 10) compares genuine weight progress rather than chasing Monte Carlo noise luck.
  • Exponential Moving Average (ema_decay = 0.999): Adopted in generator_p2f.toml and retained in generator_c4w.toml, a shadow copy of every weight is updated each step via torch._foreach_lerp_, effectively averaging iterates across the trailing ~1,000 steps. The smoothed EMA weights—never the raw final iterate—are evaluated and exported; at serving time, Sampler divides requested draws evenly across all 4 exported members.

Mixed-precision hygiene: where bf16 stops and forced fp32 begins

Modern Tensor Cores accelerate 512-wide linear layers dramatically in bfloat16, yet careless low-precision casting silently corrupts atmospheric physics. Tempest-ARKNET draws a strict precision boundary between fast matrix multiplies and sensitive physical accumulations:

Pipeline Stage Precision (Dtype) Engineering Rationale
GPU Dataset Storage (Xc, Xt) float16 Normalized rows consume ~2 bytes × 192 columns/row in VRAM, letting 8M+ ERA5 rows reside permanently on GPU with zero PCIe bottleneck (store_dtype="float32" remains available as F-29 reference mode).
Training Forward Pass bfloat16 torch.autocast("cuda", torch.bfloat16) accelerates 512-wide GEMMs on Tensor Cores while keeping the full 8-bit exponent range of fp32 (preventing fp16 gradient underflow).
score(), Validation & _anom() float32 (forced) train.py:447 explicitly casts s.float() before rebuilding 44-level cumsum profiles and computing CRPS / Energy Score. Crucially, model.py:261 wraps _anom() in torch.autocast(..., enabled=False) because bf16 has only 7 mantissa bits and quantizes a ~290 K temperature climatology in ~1 K steps!

Why 7 mantissa bits break Kelvin temperature arithmetic

In IEEE bfloat16, numbers between $2^8 = 256$ and $2^9 = 512$ have a spacing of $2^{8-7} = 2.0$ K (half-step rounding error up to $\pm 1.0$ K). Evaluating harmonic temperature climatologies or vertical cumsum temperature differences inside bf16 autocast would inject artificial 1–2 K staircase quantization across the troposphere. Forcing fp32 at the output of the trunk completely eliminates this numerical trap at negligible runtime cost.
