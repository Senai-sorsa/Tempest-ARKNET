5.1.1

The Launch-Morning Information Gap

Before launch morning, Tempest-ARKNET's climate generator and ECMWF/GFS forecast ensembles describe what the atmosphere could do across a synoptic window. Once the rocket rolls onto the pad, however, real measurements begin arriving: an automated airport METAR from 45 km across the basin, a 3 km HRRR surface analysis, a NEXRAD Doppler radar wind profile overhead, a 200 m anemometer mast or tethered drone sounding, or a 6-hour multi-station trend history. Between the last global NWP initialization cycle and T-0, local boundary-layer winds, thermal inversions, and dryline surges evolve on timescales that coarse global grids cannot resolve.

Yet none of those live readings is a noiseless copy of the exact air column above the launch rail at ignition. A field observation is imperfect not merely because of anemometer electronics, but because it was measured earlier in time ($\Delta t$), somewhere else horizontally ($d$), or at a different terrain elevation ($\Delta z$). If a launch-day workflow naively pins the pad wind to a 2-hour-old airport METAR 45 km away, it discards real mesoscale uncertainty and collapses the Monte Carlo landing dispersion cone into dangerous false confidence. Conversely, ignoring live readings throws away genuine skill when a real wind ramp is already underway.

Core Mental Model — Blending Two Imprecise Measurements

Every assimilation call in tempest_weather_v2 treats launch-day conditioning as blending two imprecise measurements of the true pad atmosphere at T-0: the unconditioned ensemble prior $(\mu_{\text{prior}}, \sigma_{\text{prior}})$ and a noisy, aged, or displaced observation $y_{\text{obs}}$. Rather than hard-pinning the ensemble, obs.py computes a dimensionless effective regression gain $r \in [0.02, 1.0]$ representing the fraction of the observed anomaly that genuinely transfers to the pad at launch time, then converts $r$ into an equivalent observation standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{obs}}$ so the Gaussian copula in TempestFlightSimEngine.conditioned(..., soft=True) blends both sources strictly by inverse-variance precision.

The architecture diagram below traces all five live observation pipelines from raw external ingestion through physical bounds checking (OBS_LIMITS), empirical gain and noise conversion, soft Gaussian copula conditioning, non-Gaussian speed-space rescaling, optional Student-$t$ surprise widening, measured-wind gust re-evaluation, and HRRR post-anchoring into the final stratified Latin Hypercube launch pool.

5.1.2

Live METAR, ASOS, Pad Tower, HRRR & Nowcast Feeds

Tempest-ARKNET separates fetching and parsing from statistical weighting so that offline range tests, historical replay benchmarks, and live pad operations execute the exact same conditioning math. Four surface and boundary-layer pipelines feed launch-morning data into Launch (with upper-air NEXRAD VAD radar covered separately in Section 5.1.6).

1. METAR / SPECI Offline Parser & Live IEM Fetch (metar.py + obs.py)

tempest_weather_v2/metar.py is a zero-dependency standard-library parser that converts raw aviation METAR/SPECI strings (for example, KTCS 241553Z 19002KT 10SM CLR 23/12 A3012 RMK AO2) into immutable Metar records. Everything after the RMK token is discarded before group extraction so automated remarks (such as peak wind or sea-level pressure codes) never collide with primary wind or temperature groups.

  • Wind units & gusts: Parses knots (KT, multiplied by $0.514444\text{ m/s}$), meters per second (MPS), and kilometers per hour (KMH, divided by $3.6$), optional gusts (Ggg), and calm winds (00000KT). Variable-direction winds (VRB) are deliberately omitted from observation_kwargs() because a scalar speed without a bearing cannot condition Cartesian $(u, v)$; the dddVddd directional-spread token is preserved for display only.
  • Thermodynamics & altimetry: Handles M-prefixed negative temperatures and dewpoints, US altimeter settings (A#### in hundredths of inHg $\times 33.8639\text{ hPa}$), and international QNH (Q#### hPa).
  • Automated IEM retrieval & anti-leakage guard: Launch.observe_latest(max_km=150.0, hours=6, k=3) queries the keyless Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM_URL) through rate-limited _net.fetch(..., service="iem") across the $k = 3$ nearest ASOS stations, clamps the query window cutoff to min(t0, now) so future reports never leak during historical replays, converts sea-level altimeter readings back to pad station pressure via weather_calc.station_pressure_hpa(), and skips any station whose report violates OBS_LIMITS.

2. HRRR 3 km 10 m Wind Anchor (hrrr.py + Launch.observe_hrrr())

hrrr.fetch_wind() retrieves NOAA's 3 km CONUS High-Resolution Rapid Refresh 10 m wind (ncep_hrrr_conus) from Open-Meteo's keyless archive (for dates older than 2 days, stitching the near-analysis opening hours of each run) or live forecast API (up to +2 days ahead). Inside Launch._apply_hrrr(), every ensemble member's near-surface $(u, v)$ is shifted toward HRRR's 10 m value using HRRR_WEIGHT = 1.0 (fitted 0.98 $u$ / 1.00 $v$ on 2021–2023) and compressed in spread by SPREAD_SCALE = 0.8, with both adjustments tapering linearly to zero at TAPER_TOP_M = 300.0 m before re-evaluating 10 m gusts.

Critical Validation Caveat — HRRR Airport-Assimilation Bias (B16)

On 240 airport ASOS cases at TCS and MAF (2024–2025), HRRR appeared dramatically superior: CRPS 1.04 vs. 1.17 for past mode and 1.98 for climate mode (0.76 combined), with correlation 0.873 vs. 0.737 for ERA5 at TCS. However, HRRR directly assimilates airport ASOS stations. When evaluated independently against an unassimilated desert basin station (USCRN Las Cruces 20 N, scripts/hrrr_vs_uscrn.py, 2026-11-08), HRRR showed zero advantage over ERA5: correlation 0.839 vs. 0.841. At remote desert launch pads lacking a co-located ASOS sensor, expect no near-analysis gain over ERA5—and multi-hour forecast lead skill remains unmeasured (explicitly flagged in Launch.trust).

3. Pad Tower, Drone & Pibal Mast Soundings (Launch.observe_tower())

The lowest 200 m above the launch rail governs rocket weathercocking, rail-exit angle of attack, and apogee scatter. Launch.observe_tower(heights_agl_m, u, v, sigma_ms=0.5) accepts a local anemometer mast, tethered balloon, pibal, or multirotor drone wind profile, converts the AGL heights to ASL using the pad elevation, and delegates directly to observe_radar() across all canonical model heights $\le 200\text{ m}$ (10, 50, 100, 150, 200 m AGL) with tight local sensor uncertainty sigma_ms = 0.5 m/s. Validated on the Texas Tech SWiFT 200 m meteorological tower (kit b67 PASS), layering observe_tower() on top of the same tower's 10 m observe() reduced 50–150 m wind CRPS by −49% at 30-minute lead.

4. NOW2 Multi-Station Ridge Nowcast (nowcast.py + Launch.observe_nowcast())

A single latest METAR snapshot cannot tell whether a synoptic wind ramp is accelerating or subsiding. nowcast.py predicts 10 m $(u, v)$ at exact leads LEADS = (1, 2, 3, 6) hours (explicitly refusing unmeasured intermediate leads) for any of the 9 regional ASOS stations in obs.STATIONS using a 31-feature ridge regression on month × UTC-hour standardized anomalies: 6 own hourly $(u, v)$ lags (12 features) plus 3 lags from each of the 3 nearest neighbor stations (18 features) plus an intercept (1 feature), with separate day (0) and night (1) weight matrices B of shape (31, 2) and predictive residual standard deviations S stored in data/nowcast.npz.

To route the ridge prediction seamlessly through Launch.observe(), nowcast.predict() converts the mean normalized residual variance $\bar{s}_{\text{pred}}^2$ into an equivalent soft-observation gain $g = \text{clip}(1 - \bar{s}_{\text{pred}}^2,\, 0.02,\, 1.0)$. When the target station sits within 5 km of the pad, observe_nowcast() back-solves the virtual reading and equivalent report age $\Delta t_{\text{eq}} = -6.3 \ln(g)$ so the standard Gaussian update lands on the exact nowcast median and variance. Tested on 2015–2017 across all 9 stations, NOW2 beat single-reading observe() at every station by +8.3% (+1 h), +8.5% (+2 h), +8.2% (+3 h), and +7.0% (+6 h) CRPS with 0.908 90% coverage.

5. Two-Tier Physical Safety Guards (OBS_LIMITS & $> 4\sigma$ Outlier Policy)

Before any reading enters the Gaussian copula, Launch._check_observation() (launch.py:618–640) enforces a strict Tier 1 hard physical gate, raising ValueError on non-finite values, negative ages or distances, or readings outside Earth-surface physical bounds (catching unit mix-ups such as knots entered as m/s, Fahrenheit entered as Celsius, or broken sensor sentinels):

Observation Variable Allowed Physical Range (OBS_LIMITS) Failure Mode Caught
wind_10m 0.0 to 100.0 m/s Negative speeds, sentinel codes (999), unit blowups
wind_from_deg 0.0 to 360.0 deg Unwrapped angles or invalid bearing flags
temperature −90.0 to 60.0 °C Kelvin (298) or Fahrenheit (95°F) accidentally passed as °C
station_pressure 300.0 to 1100.0 hPa Pascals (101325) or inHg (29.92) passed instead of hPa
altimeter 850.0 to 1100.0 hPa Unconverted US altimeter (30.12 inHg) or corrupted pressure

Tier 2 — $> 4\sigma$ Outlier Flagging (Keep as Real Weather, Never Silently Drop)

Inside _conditioned_pool() (launch.py:474–482), any measurement that clears OBS_LIMITS yet deviates from the prior median by more than $4\sigma$ ($|y - \text{med}| / \sigma_{\text{prior}} > 4.0$) is kept as real weather—because real cold fronts, drylines, and convective outflows genuinely live past $4\sigma$—while appending an explicit audit warning to Launch.trust: 

observation-far-outside-the-prior-<vars>-kept-as-real-check-for-a-typo

This prevents silent suppression of hazardous launch-day extremes while alerting the range safety officer if a plausible-looking decimal typo (such as typing 35 m/s instead of 3.5 m/s) slipped inside OBS_LIMITS.

5.1.3

Exponential Lead-Time Decay (TAU_H = 6.3 h)

When an anemometer sits directly on or beside the launch rail (horizontal distance $d < 5.0\text{ km}$, governed by OWN_STATION_KM = 5.0), spatial representativeness error vanishes. The sole reason the reading is an imperfect predictor of T-0 winds is temporal decorrelation: turbulent eddies advect downstream, daytime mixing deepens, and mesoscale pressure gradients shift between the measurement timestamp and ignition.

In tempest_weather_v2/obs.py, own-pad skill decays exponentially with report age $\Delta t \ge 0$ (hours) using the empirically fitted e-folding timescale TAU_H = 6.3 hours and a hard numerical floor of 0.02:

Equation 5.1.1 — Own-Pad Persistence Gain Law (obs.persistence_gain, $d < 5.0\text{ km}$) $$r_{\text{own}}(\Delta t) = \max\!\left(\exp\!\left(-\frac{\max(\Delta t,\, 0)}{6.3}\right),\; 0.02\right), \qquad \texttt{TAU\_H} = 6.3\text{ h}$$

Empirical Calibration (Benchmark GW-1): Across same-station ASOS persistence pairs, benchmark GW-1 measured an anomaly regression gain of 0.80 ($u$) / 0.88 ($v$) at $\Delta t = 1\text{ h}$ and 0.63 ($u$) / 0.66 ($v$) at $\Delta t = 3\text{ h}$. The single-parameter exponential with $\tau_h = 6.3\text{ h}$ reproduces this decay tightly, giving $e^{-1/6.3} = \mathbf{0.853}$ at 1 h and $e^{-3/6.3} = \mathbf{0.621}$ at 3 h, while reaching the 0.02 floor at $\Delta t = -6.3 \ln(0.02) \approx \mathbf{24.6\text{ h}}$.

Because the Gaussian copula converts gain $r$ into equivalent observation noise $\sigma_{\text{obs}}/\sigma_{\text{prior}} = \sqrt{1/r - 1}$ and shrinks posterior standard deviation to $\sigma_{\text{post}}/\sigma_{\text{prior}} = \sqrt{1 - r}$ (derived in Section 5.1.5), the 6.3-hour decay constant maps directly onto intuitive launch-countdown behavior:

Upper-Air Persistence Reference (persistence.py & the NASA Shuttle Upper Bound)

Surface persistence at 10 m decays far faster than synoptic winds aloft. For height-by-height persistence diagnostics—modeled on NASA's Space Shuttle “wind in-persistence” operational concept—tempest_weather_v2/persistence.py reads a precomputed ERA5 lag-correlation table (models/coherence_lagcorr.npz, shaped 4 seasons × 13 lags (0–12 h) × 176 columns) and computes the expected RMS wind-change spread over lag $\Delta t$ as:

Equation 5.1.2 — Lag-Correlation Wind-Change Standard Deviation (persistence.py) $$\operatorname{sd}_{\text{change}}(z, \Delta t) = \sqrt{2\,\big(1 - \rho_{\text{ERA5}}(z, \Delta t)\big)}$$

Why persistence.py Is Strictly an Upper Bound on Persistence

In ERA5, 3-hour lag correlation remains remarkably high aloft—0.90–0.94 at 2 km and 0.96–0.97 at 10 km compared to 0.76–0.87 at 10 m. However, $0.25^\circ$ (~28 km) ERA5 reanalysis smooths out mesoscale gravity waves and thin wind-shear laminae shorter than ~1.3 km vertically. Historical NASA Shuttle paired Jimsphere balloon releases demonstrated that those fine-scale features decorrelate within 90 minutes. Consequently, persistence.py documents an optimistic large-scale upper bound on upper-air persistence, not a guarantee against sub-grid wind-shear drift between pre-launch soundings.

5.1.4

Own-Station Radius (5.0 km) & Cross-Station Representativeness

Most amateur, collegiate (IREC), and commercial desert launch sites do not sit directly on an FAA ASOS runway. Spaceport America, for instance, sits roughly 45 km southeast of Truth or Consequences Municipal Airport (TCS) across desert basin terrain. Once a weather station lies at or beyond OWN_STATION_KM = 5.0 km from the pad, horizontal separation $d$, terrain elevation difference $|\Delta z|$, and atmospheric boundary-layer stability dominate the error budget far more than a 30-minute timestamp lag.

To quantify how much signal survives across space, elevation, and time, Tempest-ARKNET fits a leave-one-station-out (LOSO) regression (scripts/obs_loso.py, 2015–2022) on month × UTC-hour wind anomalies across 9 southern New Mexico and West Texas ASOS stations: TCS (Truth or Consequences, 1479 m), ONM (Socorro, 1486 m), LRU (Las Cruces, 1357 m), ELP (El Paso, 1206 m), ALM (Alamogordo, 1304 m), MAF (Midland, 872 m), FST (Fort Stockton, 920 m), PEQ (Pecos, 790 m), and ABQ (Albuquerque, 1619 m).

Day “Communicating Room” vs. Night Cold-Pool Inversion

The LOSO study uncovered a sharp physical bifurcation between convective daytime mixing and stable nocturnal inversions, encoded via the piecewise-linear solar weight obs.night_weight(solar_hour):

  • Daytime well-mixed “communicating room” ($w_n = 0$, solar 08–17 h): Solar heating drives deep convective thermals that couple the entire basin vertically and horizontally. Even a station 50 km away retains a base gain of 0.62, vertical decoupling decays slowly with elevation scale REP_DZ["day"] = 1317.0 m, and cross-station reports remain flat for 2 hours before decaying slowly beyond 6 hours (TAU_X["day"] = 27.0 h).
  • Nighttime isolated cold-pool inversion ($w_n = 1$, solar 20–05 h): Radiative cooling traps shallow, decoupled drainage pools inside local arroyos and valley floors. Base gain drops to 0.48 at 50 km and 0.18 at 500 km, elevation coupling tightens to REP_DZ["night"] = 919.0 m, and reports stale more than twice as fast beyond 6 hours (TAU_X["night"] = 12.0 h).
  • Dawn & dusk linear transitions: Between solar 05–08 h, $w_n = (8 - h)/3$ ramps smoothly from $1 \to 0$; between solar 17–20 h, $w_n = (h - 17)/3$ ramps smoothly from $0 \to 1$.

Worked Example — Spaceport America Assimilating a 1-Hour-Old TCS METAR

Consider Spaceport America assimilating a 1-hour-old METAR from TCS ($d = 45\text{ km} \ge 5.0\text{ km}$, $|\Delta z| = 70\text{ m}$):

  • Late morning (11:00 solar, $w_n = 0$): Linear interpolation on REP_TABLE["day"] gives $r_{\text{dist}}(45) = 0.624$; elevation attenuation gives $e^{-70/1317} = 0.948$; 1-hour age factor is $1.00$. Total gain drops to $r = \mathbf{0.440}$, increasing equivalent observation noise to $\sigma_{\text{obs}} = 1.13\,\sigma_{\text{prior}}$ so the decoupled cold-pool airport reading cannot falsely hijack the pad ensemble.
5.1.5

Kalman / Bayesian Posterior Ensemble Update

Once obs.effective_gain() determines how much weight $r$ an observation deserves, Tempest-ARKNET executes a four-stage Bayesian conditioning stack inside Launch._conditioned_pool(): (1) gain-to-sigma conversion and multivariate Gaussian copula Kalman update, (2) non-Gaussian 10 m speed-space rescaling, (3) optional Student-$t$ surprise widening, and (4) measured-wind gust re-evaluation.

1. Converting Gain $r$ to Sensor Noise $\sigma_{\text{obs}}$ & Multivariate Schur Complement

In a 1D conjugate Gaussian update, blending a prior $\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\text{prior}}, \sigma_{\text{prior}}^2)$ with a noisy measurement $\mathcal{N}(y_{\text{obs}}, \sigma_{\text{obs}}^2)$ shifts the posterior mean by the scalar Kalman gain $r = \sigma_{\text{prior}}^2 / (\sigma_{\text{prior}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{obs}}^2)$. Inverting this identity for $\sigma_{\text{obs}}$ yields obs.observation_sigma(prior_sd, gain) (clamped to $r \in [0.02, 1.0]$ and floored at $10^{-3}\max(\sigma_{\text{prior}}, 10^{-6})$ for numerical stability):

Equation 5.1.4 — Equivalent Observation Noise & Posterior Moments (obs.observation_sigma) $$\sigma_{\text{obs}} = \sigma_{\text{prior}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{r} - 1}, \qquad \mu_{\text{post}} = (1 - r)\,\mu_{\text{prior}} + r\,y_{\text{obs}}, \qquad \sigma_{\text{post}} = \sigma_{\text{prior}}\sqrt{1 - r}$$

Three Calibration Regimes: (i) Noiseless lock ($r \to 1 \implies \sigma_{\text{obs}} \to 10^{-3}\sigma_{\text{prior}}$): a fresh pad reading pins the observed level and propagates the update vertically through the prior correlation matrix; (ii) Midpoint calibration ($r = 0.5 \implies \sigma_{\text{obs}} = \sigma_{\text{prior}}$): both sources carry equal precision, placing $\mu_{\text{post}}$ halfway between prior and observation and shrinking spread to $0.707\,\sigma_{\text{prior}}$; (iii) Prior untouched ($r \to 0.02 \implies \sigma_{\text{obs}} = 7.0\,\sigma_{\text{prior}}$): stale or remote nighttime readings contribute negligible precision, leaving the unconditioned ensemble virtually unchanged.

In the full $P$-dimensional vertical profile state, Launch._conditioned_pool() passes these per-variable sigmas into base.engine().conditioned(eng_obs, sensor_sigma=sig, soft=sig is not None, inflate_decay_m=INFLATE_DECAY_M), which computes the multivariate Kalman gain matrix $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{C}_{\text{all}, o}(\mathbf{C}_{oo} + \mathbf{R})^{-1}$ and Schur-complement posterior covariance $\mathbf{S}_{\text{post}} = \mathbf{C} - \mathbf{K}\mathbf{C}_{\text{all}, o}^\top$ in normal-score copula space. Posterior ensemble members are drawn via 16-dimensional stratified Latin Hypercube Sampling (latin_hypercube(n, 16)) for low-discrepancy variance reduction, followed immediately by a mandatory gust-law refresh (_regust(..., measured=MEASURED_GUST_LAW)), which switches from the model-wind gust parameterization to the gust law fitted on measured anemometer winds—cutting validation gust CRPS from 0.93 → 0.51 overall and from 12.95 → 1.40 above 16 m/s.

2. Speed-Space Normal-Score Update (OBS_SPEED_SPACE = True)

Conditioning on Cartesian wind components $(u_{10}, v_{10})$ inside a Gaussian copula accurately updates wind direction and upper-air shear, but it systematically distorts scalar wind speed $s = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}$ near the ground. Because surface wind speed is strictly non-negative and Weibull-skewed, independent Gaussian shrinkage on $u$ and $v$ compresses speed tails too aggressively—leaving 90% speed prediction intervals under-covered (only 74% at TCS and 85% at MAF in component-only ablation tests).

For every soft observation (whenever age_hours, distance_km, or elevation_diff_m is supplied), _speed_space_update() runs right after the Gaussian copula step and rescales the conditioned wind vectors inside the prior pool's own empirical normal-score space:

Equation 5.1.5 — Normal-Score Speed Update & Direction-Preserving Vector Taper (launch.py::_speed_space_update) $$z_{\text{obs}} = \Phi^{-1}\!\big(F_{\text{prior}}(s_{\text{obs}})\big), \qquad z_i \sim \mathcal{N}\!\big(\rho\, z_{\text{obs}},\; 1 - \rho^2\big), \qquad s_{\text{target}, i} = F_{\text{prior}}^{-1}\!\big(\Phi(z_i)\big)$$ $$\rho = \begin{cases} \min\!\left(0.72^{\Delta t},\; 0.97\right) & \text{if } d < 5.0\text{ km (own pad)} \\ \min\!\left(r_{\text{report}},\; 0.72^{\Delta t},\; 0.97\right) & \text{if } d \ge 5.0\text{ km (off-site, } \texttt{NEIGHBOUR\_RHO\_FROM\_GAIN = True}\text{)} \end{cases}$$ $$k_i = \frac{s_{\text{target}, i}}{\max(s_{0, i},\; 0.05)}, \qquad \operatorname{fac}_i(z) = 1 + (k_i - 1)\,\operatorname{clip}\!\left(1 - \frac{z - 10}{300 - 10},\; 0,\; 1\right), \qquad (u_i', v_i') = \operatorname{fac}_i(z)\,(u_i, v_i)$$

Bit-for-Bit Direction Preservation: The $n$ stratified target speeds $s_{\text{target}}$ are rank-matched to the conditioned samples' 10 m speeds $s_0 = \sqrt{u_0^2 + v_0^2}$, and the positive ratio $k_i$ tapers linearly to $1.0$ from $10\text{ m}$ up to SPEED_TOP_M = 300.0 m. Because both $u_i$ and $v_i$ are multiplied by the exact same positive scalar $\operatorname{fac}_i(z)$, wind bearing $\operatorname{atan2}(-u_i, -v_i)$ is preserved bit-for-bit at every altitude while temperature, pressure, humidity, and upper-air winds above 300 m remain untouched. Exact pinned observations (no age/distance/elevation metadata) bypass this step.

Empirical Verification on ASOS 2023–2025 Holdout (w6_observe_eval.py)

  • Own-station persistence (300 h/station): Using SPEED_RHO_PER_HOUR = 0.72 (fitted 0.67 at TCS and 0.76 at MAF on 2015–2022) improved CRPS from 1.148 → 1.140 (TCS) and 1.016 → 0.987 (MAF), lifting 90% coverage from 0.74 → 0.81 (TCS) and 0.85 → 0.91 (MAF).
  • Off-site neighbor cap (NEIGHBOUR_RHO_FROM_GAIN = True): Capping $\rho = \min(r_{\text{report}}, \rho_{\text{age}})$ for stations $\ge 5\text{ km}$ away lifted 90% coverage by +0.07 to +0.15 and lowered CRPS in 13 of 14 cross-station benchmark runs.

3. Student-$t$ Surprise Widening (_t_widen_factor, Opt-In t_update = False)

A well-known limitation of Gaussian Bayesian updating is that posterior variance depends only on sensor noise and prior covariance—never on how surprising the observation is. Whether a pad thermometer reads $1\sigma$ or $4\sigma$ away from the prior median, a Gaussian update shrinks posterior spread by the exact same factor. In reality, a $4\sigma$ anomaly signals an abnormal synoptic regime (a dryline surge, frontal passage, or thunderstorm outflow) where unmeasured upper-air winds are substantially less predictable.

When a user opts in with observe(..., t_update=True), _t_widen_factor() evaluates the standardized Mahalanobis surprise $D^2$ across up to $k \le 10$ observed scalars using heavy-tailed Student-$t$ degrees of freedom T_NU = 8.0:

Equation 5.1.6 — Student-$t$ Mahalanobis Surprise Multiplier (launch.py::_t_widen_factor) $$D^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \frac{\big(y_j - \mu_{\text{prior}, j}\big)^2}{\sigma_{\text{prior}, j}^2 + \sigma_{\text{obs}, j}^2}, \qquad f_t = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\nu + D^2}{\nu + k}},\; 1.0,\; 2.0\right) \qquad (\nu = 8.0)$$

Safety-Margin Behavior: For an ordinary $1\sigma$ reading ($D^2 \approx k$), $f_t \approx 1.0$, and clipping to $[1.0, 2.0]$ guarantees it never narrows dispersion margins. For a single $4\sigma$ reading ($k = 1, D^2 = 16$), $f_t = \sqrt{(8 + 16)/(8 + 1)} = \sqrt{24/9} \approx \mathbf{1.63\times}$, expanding dispersion by 63% and emitting the audit flag t-widened-x1.63. _conditioned_pool() scales departures around the posterior mean (m + t_fac * (s[k] - m)) on u, v, and t2m only—leaving $T$, $p$, and $\text{RH}$ columns unwidened so hydrostatic density $\rho$ and sound speed $a$ remain consistent without re-integration. It stays opt-in (off by default) because the 2026-11-20 port rerun did not reproduce the earlier validation gain.

5.1.6

NEXRAD VAD Radar Wind Profile Assimilation

Surface anemometers and airport METARs constrain only the lowest tens of meters of the boundary layer; they carry minimal correlation with upper-air jet streaks at 5–15 km where high-power rockets reach apogee and deploy drogue chutes. Waiting for a twice-daily 00Z/12Z NWS radiosonde leaves up to a 12-hour blind spot on launch morning.

tempest_weather_v2/radar.py closes this upper-air gap by assimilating public NEXRAD Level III Velocity Azimuth Display (NVW) wind profiles—each WSR-88D Doppler radar's own volume-scan wind retrieval updated every 5–10 minutes up to ~10 km. Each NVW file is ~9 KB and fetched keyless from the Unidata AWS S3 archive (unidata-nexrad-level3) within a strict recency window of max_age_min = 45 minutes prior to launch time.

Decoding Pipeline & Regional WSR-88D Network

radar.parse_nvw() decodes the binary Level III product using metpy.io.Level3File (raising a clear ImportError if MetPy is not installed), extracts numeric rows from the 'VAD Algorithm Output' tabular pages, converts altitude from hundreds of feet ($\times 30.48\text{ m}$) ASL to pad AGL, and sorts the profile monotonically in height. Launch.observe_radar() then linearly interpolates $(u, v)$ onto four canonical conditioning levels: 500, 1000, 1500, and 2000 m AGL with uniform sensor noise VAD_SIGMA_MS = 3.0 m/s—matching the empirically measured 2.9–3.2 m/s radar-minus-radiosonde vector wind error (scripts/p3_vad_vs_balloon.py).

Upward Covariance Propagation & Why Conditioning Stops at 2000 m

Because the Gaussian copula stores the full cross-altitude vertical correlation matrix $\mathbf{C}$ across all 44 height levels, conditioning only the four lower-tropospheric levels (500–2000 m AGL) propagates information well above 2 km into completely unobserved upper-air altitudes. Evaluated on 120 days of El Paso (EPZ) radar vs. co-located NWS radiosondes (2021–2023, 00Z, scripts/p3_vad_condition.py):

Unobserved Altitude Band CRPS Improvement over Climate Prior 90% Interval Coverage Physical Mechanism
2.5 – 5.0 km AGL +31.3% (~+32%) 0.93 – 0.94 Tight synoptic coupling between 2 km top-of-PBL flow and lower mid-troposphere
5.0 – 12.0 km AGL +16% 0.93 – 0.94 Thermal-wind and deep baroclinic trough correlation up to jet core
12.0 – 18.0 km AGL +7% 0.93 – 0.94 Residual upper-tropospheric / lower-stratospheric correlation tail

Why Four Levels (≤ 2000 m) Is Mandatory & the Distant-Radar Caveat

1. Over-tightening beyond 2000 m: Feeding every available radar altitude up to 5 km into the diagonal sensor-noise matrix $\mathbf{R} = (3.0)^2 \mathbf{I}$ treats vertically correlated Doppler beam errors as independent measurements, collapsing 90% coverage from 0.93–0.94 down to 0.49. Limiting ingestion to the four 500 m-spaced levels up to 2000 m preserves calibrated spread.
2. Distant-radar displacement: The EPZ benchmark tested a radar co-located with the balloon release site. A radar ~100 km from the launch pad—such as Holloman (HDX) for Spaceport America—samples a different air mass and warrants a larger (currently unmeasured and flagged) sigma_ms. Also recall Confirmed Bugs B9 (radar._get() bypassing offline/retry controls) and B10 (offline None elevation crash) from Section 5.1.5.
