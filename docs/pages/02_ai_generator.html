2. Generative AI Core

Single-step Engression with AdaLN/FiLM noise conditioning, linear climate trend bypass, proper scoring rules, and vertical-wind / gust extensions.

2.1

Why Engression beats mean forecasts

Standard MSE regression predicts the average of all possible skies. When a jet stream can sit high or low, averaging two sharp regimes produces a blurred, low-shear profile that never occurs in nature.

  • The MSE blur trap: If Regime A has a sharp 45 m/s jet streak at 9 km and Regime B has one at 13 km, an MSE model outputs a smeared 28 m/s lump across 9–13 km, hiding the real wind-shear hazard from 6-DOF flight simulations.
  • Single-step generative regression (Engression): Rather than running 50+ slow diffusion denoising steps, Engression feeds a random noise draw directly into a deterministic network and trains it with proper scoring rules (CRPS + Energy Score).
  • Sharp, diverse ensemble fans: Each noise vector produces one physically sharp vertical profile in a single forward pass, letting Tempest-ARKNET generate hundreds of realistic Monte Carlo weather worlds in milliseconds.

Bimodal Jet-Streak Intuition: MSE Blur vs. Engression Fan

Physical realism matters for flight safety: A blurred average underestimates peak shear and structural bending loads. Engression preserves sharp vertical gradients across every individual ensemble member.

2.2

Noise conditioning & architecture

Tempest-ARKNET uses a compact 4-block residual MLP (width 512) modeled after modern ML weather ensembles (ECMWF AIFS-CRPS and DeepMind FGN). Two structural choices keep the generator fast, stable, and immune to climate-index memorization.

  • Pre-additive noise ($z_{\varepsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$): One 32-dimensional standard normal vector is embedded once and injected into every residual block so every altitude layer shares the same coherent weather draw.
  • AdaLN / FiLM dimmer switches: Inside each block, the noise embedding outputs per-channel scale ($s_\ell$) and shift ($b_\ell$) parameters that modulate the normalized hidden state — acting like a bank of dimmer switches that steer the network toward a specific weather realization.
  • Zero-initialized residual blocks: Every FiLM projection starts at exact zero weights and biases ($s_\ell = 0, b_\ell = 0$), so training begins as a clean, stable LayerNorm MLP and smoothly learns stochastic spread without early divergence.
  • Linear climate trend bypass: Slow monthly indices (gmst, oni, wwv, qbo) bypass the nonlinear trunk so the network cannot fingerprint and memorize individual historical months, forcing clean extrapolatable trends.

Visual Profile Modulation: Noise Bend vs. Linear Climate Shift

Eq. 2.1 — AdaLN / FiLM Residual Block & Linear Bypass $$h_{\ell+1} = h_\ell + \mathrm{FFN}\!\left(\mathrm{LN}(h_\ell) \odot (1 + s_\ell(z_{\varepsilon})) + b_\ell(z_{\varepsilon})\right), \qquad z_{\varepsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$$ $$\hat{y} = f_{\text{core}}(x_{\text{local}}, z_{\varepsilon}) + \sum_{k \in \mathcal{L}} \beta_k(x_{\text{local}})\, x_k$$

Keeping $\mathcal{L} = \{\text{year/gmst}, \text{oni}, \text{wwv}, \text{qbo30}, \text{qbo50}\}$ in the linear bypass lets the model extrapolate future warming smoothly while blocking month-level memorization.

2.3

Training losses, gusts & vertical wind (W)

Because the network outputs an ensemble of $M$ draws per training step (default $M = 4$), Tempest-ARKNET trains with strictly proper scoring rules that reward both accuracy and honest spread, paired with targeted rocket-safety heads for gusts and vertical air motion.

  • Almost-Fair CRPS (crps_alpha = 0.95): Scores every marginal coordinate using the unbiased spread denominator $M(M-1)$. Pure fair CRPS ($\alpha = 1$) has a degenerate optimum at small $M$; setting $\alpha = 0.95$ eliminates that instability while preserving honest spread.
  • Multivariate Energy Score (es_weight = 0.1): Scores the full 179-D Euclidean vector jointly, penalizing ensembles whose altitude levels move independently instead of as coherent atmospheric columns.
  • Shear-tail & low-wind rail-exit losses: Optional targeted proper scores emphasize extreme vertical shear layers above the 90th percentile (shear_tail_q = 0.9) and near-pad vector winds at or below 300 m AGL (low_wind_loss) where rail-exit weathercocking is sharpest.
  • Gumbel peak gust excess (10 m): Airport ASOS observations fit a conditional Gumbel distribution for $G = \max(\text{gust}, \text{wind})$ with location linear in wind speed, harmonics, and station flag, and log-linear scale $\beta = \exp(\phi^\top x)$.
  • Vertical wind $w$ & convective scale $w^*$: Resolved synoptic vertical velocity is converted from ERA5 pressure velocity via $w = -\omega / (\rho g_0)$, while sub-grid daytime thermals scale with the Deardorff convective velocity $w^*$ inside the mixed boundary layer ($0 < z < z_i$).
Eq. 2.2 — Almost-Fair CRPS & Multivariate Energy Score $$\mathrm{afCRPS}_\alpha(X, y) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} |X_i - y| - \frac{1 - \varepsilon}{2 M (M - 1)} \sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M} |X_i - X_j|, \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{1 - \alpha}{M}$$ $$\mathrm{ES}(X, y) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} \|X_i - y\|_2 - \frac{1}{2 M (M - 1)} \sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M} \|X_i - X_j\|_2$$

The first term pulls ensemble members toward truth; the second term rewards pairwise diversity up to the true uncertainty level, preventing ensemble collapse.

Eq. 2.3 — Synoptic Vertical Wind & Convective Thermal Scale $$w = -\frac{\omega}{\rho\, g_0}, \qquad w^* = \left(\frac{g_0}{T}\,\frac{H}{\rho\, c_p}\, z_i\right)^{1/3}$$ $$\sigma_w(z) = \sqrt{1.8}\, w^* \left(\frac{z}{z_i}\right)^{1/3}\left(1 - 0.8\,\frac{z}{z_i}\right) \quad (0 < z < z_i)$$

When upward surface heat flux $H \le 0$ (stable night air), $w^* = 0$ and convective thermals shut off automatically; during desert afternoons, $\sigma_w(z)$ peaks near $z \approx 0.35\, z_i$.
