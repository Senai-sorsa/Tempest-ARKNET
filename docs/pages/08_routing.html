3.3 Lead-Time Routing & Forecast Modes

Routing every launch timestamp smoothly across six lead-time regimes with raised-cosine Bernoulli whole-profile mixing, past-date ERA5 balloon-residual replay (Mode P), live 50:50 IFS neural-and-Ridge forecasts (Mode F), multi-model near-term ensembles, and the UR5 climate wind-sequence post-processor.

Modules: router.py, modes.py, modeP.py, modeF.py, sigma.py, tailcal.py, scenarios.py, nearterm/ensemble.py, ur5.pyShipped ON: Mode P, Mode F (50:50 Net+Ridge), Mode C & OPTIONS['ur5_sequence']Companion: Colab Lab 3
3.3.1

Three Operational Modes: Climate, Forecast, Launch

A single atmospheric model cannot serve every point on a launch campaign's timeline equally well. A flight reconstructed two months after launch has a completed global ERA5 reanalysis archive sitting on disk; a launch scheduled three days from now has a sharp live ECMWF IFS numerical weather forecast; a launch two hours away has live pad anemometers and pibal soundings; and a launch planned nine months ahead lives entirely in climate-conditioned generative space. Tempest-ARKNET resolves this span by organizing inference around three primary operational pillars—Climate (Mode C / "ai"), Forecast & Historical Replay (Mode F / "forecast", Mode P / "past", and medium-range F2/M), and Launch-Day Assimilation (pad observations fused into the near-term ensemble in tempest_weather_v2)—all coordinated by a single deterministic lead-time router.

Every query enters the router with a target location and timestamp (lat, lon, valid_time) and computes the signed lead time $\text{lead} = t_{\text{valid}} - t_{\text{now}}$ in days. Regardless of which backend serves the query, every path emits the exact same canonical 44-level AGL state tensor ($u, v, T, \mathrm{RH}$ across 44 heights plus surface $t_{2\text{m}}, p_{\text{sfc}}, \mathrm{rh}_{2\text{m}}$, totaling $D = 179$ physical dimensions) alongside explicit mode, source, and quality metadata flags. Downstream 6-DOF rocket simulators therefore never need mode-specific parsing code.

Whole-Profile Scenario Selection (tempest/forecast/scenarios.py)

Once the router produces an $m$-member calibrated ensemble pool, aerospace teams frequently ask for a compact set of deterministic mission cases—such as the 1st, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 99th percentile wind or drift atmospheres—for rapid trajectory design checks. Taking component-wise percentiles across altitude levels would synthesize an unphysical composite column that never occurs in nature. scenarios(samples, outcome_fn, pcts=(1, 25, 50, 75, 99), weights=None) enforces a strict whole-profile rule: it evaluates any scalar flight metric outcome_fn(samples) (such as 0–3 km mean wind, parachute drift, or landing distance), computes exact or weighted target percentiles $t_p$, and selects the actual individual ensemble member closest to each target via $\arg\min_i |o_i - t_p|$. Every returned scenario is therefore a complete, hydrostatically and vertically coherent atmosphere.

Core Architectural Invariant — Never Average Independent Columns

Both router.mix() at regime boundaries and scenarios() at mission export obey the same physical law: never linearly average two distinct atmospheric profiles. Averaging a column with a 10 km jet streak and a column with a 13 km jet streak creates a phantom double-humped jet and collapses ensemble variance by up to 50%. Every transition and scenario selection operates strictly on whole, intact columns.

3.3.2

Lead-Time Horizon Regimes (Past, 0 h to 16 d+, and Climate)

tempest/forecast/router.py partitions the continuous timeline from deep historical dates through multi-year future planning into six contiguous regimes defined in router.DEFAULTS. Before any dispatch occurs, router.check(s) validates strict monotonicity of all regime boundaries (p_days < p0_days < f_days < f2_days < m_days) and strictly positive hand-over widths (end > start). In production serving (tempest_weather/modes.py), any regime whose external data feed is unavailable gracefully falls back to the always-on climate generator ("ai") and appends an explicit fallback-mode quality flag.

Mode P — Past-Date ERA5 Replay & Balloon-Residual Network (modeP.py)

When reconstructing a historical launch ($\text{lead} \le -6\text{ d}$), ERA5 already provides an hourly 3D reanalysis of the atmosphere—yet a $0.25^\circ$ grid cell is systematically smoother than the actual air column a rocket or radiosonde traverses. tempest/forecast/modeP.py treats ERA5 as the deterministic first guess and trains a compact CRPS-minimizing Engression residual network on ~40,000 paired IGRA2 00Z/12Z soundings at Santa Teresa (EPZ) and Midland (MAF) to generate the full $D = 4 \times 44 = 176$ balloon-minus-ERA5 residual vector:

Equation 3.3.1 — Mode P Balloon-Residual Target & Stochastic Replay $$y_{\text{res}} = x_{\text{obs}} - x_{\text{ERA5}} \in \mathbb{R}^{176}, \qquad \hat{x}^{(i)} = \mathrm{clip}_{\text{RH}\in[0,100]}\!\left(x_{\text{ERA5}} + f_\theta(c,\; z_\varepsilon^{(i)})\right)$$

cond_matrix() packs 180 conditioning features: [station, sin(doy), cos(doy), hour12, era_0..era_175]. Two optional extensions live in modeP.py: (1) 1-hour ERA5 tendency (COND_NAMES_TEND, +176 dims adding ERA5(t + 1 h) - ERA5(t) to capture atmospheric drift during a balloon's ~90-minute ascent), and (2) the post2022 sensor indicator (COND_NAMES_POST, evaluated in F-11 Part 2 and discarded once the F-11a station hybrid cleanly resolved the upper-tropospheric humidity offset). After sampling in blocks of M_CAL = 199, fit_calibration() applies a pooled 2021–2023 spread-calibration table across 16 groups (4 variables × 4 height bands: 0–1, 1–5, 5–12, 12–18 km).

Three Shipped Mode P Refinements (_past_phys & _anchor_to_era5)

Production past-date serving in tempest_weather/api.py augments the base Mode P residual network with three verified physical refinements, all shipped enabled by default:

  • F-12 Joint Empirical Surface Residual Pool (modep_sfc): Originally _past_phys left surface pressure sp with zero spread (np.full) and shifted t2m/rh2m using the 10 m profile residual. F-12 draws a complete empirical (t2m, sp, rh2m) balloon-minus-ERA5 residual row matched by station × season × launch hour. On 2024–2025 00Z soundings (1,416 sondes), this cut t2m CRPS from 1.16 → 0.68 K, sp CRPS from 48.8 → 25.9 Pa, and rh2m CRPS from 3.75 → 2.92%, achieving 99% coverages of 0.991 / 0.994 / 0.999.
  • F-11a Upper-Air RH Station-Correction Override (A["sta"]): Because Mode P trained on 1991–2017 inherited pre-2022 radiosonde dry biases in the upper troposphere, all 13 columns of RH spanning 12–18 km (where linear station correction beat the neural net on 2021–2023) are overridden with $a_g + b_g\,\text{ERA5}$ plus a moisture-tercile residual draw keyed by 5–13 km mean RH. This lowers 12–18 km RH CRPS from 4.76 → 3.70 and restores mean upper-tropospheric RH from 4.9% → 10.9% at modern sensor levels.
  • GW-F Near-Ground Wind Anchor (_anchor_to_era5): Re-centers the median of near-ground $(u, v)$ onto the ERA5 10 m wind with weight $w \in [0, 1]$ while keeping ANCHOR_SPREAD = 1.0 (narrowing spread hurt 99% coverage), tapering linearly to zero at ANCHOR_TOP_M = 300 m:
Equation 3.3.2 — Linear 300 m Near-Ground Wind Anchor Taper (GW-F) $$\tau(z) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(1 - \frac{z - 10}{300 - 10},\; 0,\; 1\right)$$

Enabled by default in Launch(past_era5_anchor=True), reducing 10 m wind-speed CRPS by −5% at Truth or Consequences (TCS) and −11% at Midland (MAF) without introducing a boundary-layer shear cliff.

3.3.3

Smooth Crossfading, Mode F (IFS + Ridge) & SIGMA Scaling

Think of lead-time regime hand-overs like a DJ crossfading two tracks at different tempos. A hard cut from a sharp 7-day numerical weather forecast directly to a broad seasonal climate ensemble at midnight on Day 15 produces an unphysical step jump in predicted wind median and 90% dispersion between adjacent hours. tempest/forecast/router.py eliminates seam jumps using a $C^1$-smooth raised cosine ramp with zero derivative at both endpoints across both transition windows (ens_blend = (10.0, 15.0) and m_blend = (35.0, 46.0)):

Equation 3.3.3 — Raised Cosine Hand-Over Weights (router.py) $$r(x;\, a, b) = 0.5 - 0.5\cos\!\left(\pi \cdot \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{x - a}{b - a},\; 0,\; 1\right)\right)$$ $$w_{\text{ens}}(\text{lead}) = 1 - r(\text{lead};\; 10.0,\; 15.0), \qquad w_{\text{clim}}(\text{lead}) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{lead} \le 15.0 \\ r(\text{lead};\; 35.0,\; 46.0) & \text{lead} > 15.0 \end{cases}$$

At $\text{lead} \le 10\text{ d}$, $w_{\text{ens}} = 1$ (100% live ensemble); at $12.5\text{ d}$, $w_{\text{ens}} = 0.5$; at $\ge 15\text{ d}$, $w_{\text{ens}} = 0$. Similarly, $w_{\text{clim}}$ stays $0$ through Day 35 and climbs smoothly to $1.0$ at Day 46.

Rather than averaging profiles, router.mix(rng, forecast_samples, other_samples, w_other) (router.py:65–76) draws an independent Bernoulli coin flip per ensemble member—pick = rng.random(m) < w_other—and copies entire rows out[pick] = other_samples[pick]. Mathematically, this realizes the exact mixture distribution $(1 - w)P_{\text{fcst}} + w P_{\text{other}}$: every individual trajectory preserves its intact tropopause break, boundary-layer inversion, and jet shear, and total variance never collapses at $w = 0.5$ (whereas linear averaging cuts variance in half: $\mathrm{Var}(0.5 X + 0.5 Y) = 0.5 \sigma^2$).

Mode F — Live ECMWF IFS 0.25° Forecasts & Boundary-Layer Reconstruction

For upcoming launches between 0.75 and 7.5 days ahead and local solar hours 02–20 h (FORECAST_LEAD = (0.75, 7.5), FORECAST_SOLAR = (2.0, 20.0)), tempest/forecast/modeF.py fetches live ECMWF IFS 0.25° pressure-level and surface fields via Open-Meteo (with offline training against WeatherBench2 archived HRES runs wb2_hres at 00Z and wb2_hres_v2 at 00Z+12Z). lead_bin() maps continuous leads into four tested buckets: < 2 d → 1, 2–4 d → 3, 4–6 d → 5, and 6–7.5 d → 7 (with > 7.5 d → 0 unused).

Because archived HRES pressure-level files omit both 100 m wind (u100, v100) and 2 m dewpoint (d2m) needed by profiles_from_levels(), boundary_inputs() and fit_boundary_layer() reconstruct both anchors from the lowest pressure level strictly above ground (agl > 0):

  • 100 m Wind Ratio by Stability Regime: Computes the near-surface lapse rate $\Gamma_{\text{low}} = (T_{2\text{m}} - T_{\text{low}}) / \max(z_{\text{low,km}},\, 0.05)$ and bins it across LAPSE_BINS = [0.0, 4.0, 7.0, 9.0] K/km into five stability classes: inversion (<0), stable (0–4), neutral (4–7), unstable (7–9), and super-adiabatic (>9 K/km). Each bin applies its ERA5 training-year median ratio $|u_{100}|/|u_{10}|$ (for $|u_{10}| > 1\text{ m/s}$; fallback 1.4, matching a neutral log-law with $z_0 \approx 0.1\text{ m}$).
  • 2 m Dewpoint Depression Regression: Converts specific humidity $q$ to relative humidity via IFS mixed-phase rh_from_q(), computes the lowest-level dewpoint depression $\text{DPD}_{\text{low}} = T_{\text{low}} - T_{d,\text{low}}$, and maps it linearly to 2 m:
Equation 3.3.4 — Boundary-Layer 2 m Dewpoint Anchor Regression $$T_{d,2\text{m}} = T_{2\text{m}} - \mathrm{clip}\!\left(a_{\text{dpd}} + b_{\text{dpd}}\,\text{DPD}_{\text{low}},\; 0,\; 60\right)$$

Default fallback (DEFAULT_BL): $a_{\text{dpd}} = 0.0$, $b_{\text{dpd}} = 1.0$.

50:50 Schaake-Shuffled Error Network & Per-Lead Ridge Mixture ($\lambda = 200$)

At runtime (api._forecast_phys), Mode F combines two complementary forecast-error models via a 50:50 Bernoulli whole-profile mixture (use = rng.random(n) < 0.5, tagged forecast-mixture:network-and-ridge-50-50):

  1. Neural Residual Path + Schaake Shuffle (modef_tpl): A regularized Engression network (coarse=4, sampling every 4th altitude level of the forecast input to prevent overfitting) generates residual profiles, applies the spread range map, and Schaake-shuffles each column against 1,500 real historical forecast-error templates per lead (modef_tpl/L{1,3,5,7}, stored in float16) to restore realistic vertical and cross-variable error correlation.
  2. Standardized Per-Lead Ridge + Empirical Residual Pool (modef_ridge): For each lead $L \in \{1, 3, 5, 7\}$, export_tool.py solves $W_L = (Z^\top Z + 200\,I)^{-1} Z^\top O$ on standardized HRES inputs ($\lambda = 200$) and pairs predictions with a 3,000-row empirical residual pool. Any missing underground input level is imputed to $\mu$ so no Ridge row ever propagates NaN.

Gotcha — Western-Hemisphere lon - 360 Alignment & Opt-In IL-14 Ensemble Features

In modeF.py:78 and modeF.py:129, grid alignment computes h.longitude.values[None, :] - 360.0 - pn[:, None] to match WeatherBench2's $[0, 360)^\circ\text{ E}$ longitudes against the ERA5 store's $[-180, 180)^\circ$ convention. This works cleanly across West Texas, New Mexico, and Denver ($-108^\circ$ to $-101^\circ$), but will trip the grid-match assertion in the Eastern Hemisphere unless wrapped modulo 360 before differencing. Separately, passing ens_features into atmosphere_set() activates the opt-in modefens network (IL-14), appending 24 IFS-ensemble (mean, sd) summary features (u10, v10, t2m plus u, v, T at 500 / 700 / 850 hPa) for a 1.9–3.1% CRPS reduction across 1–7 day leads on test year 2022 (flagged forecast-ifs-ensemble-features).

SIGMA Lead-as-Noise Scaling (sigma.py) & Killed Far-Tail Stretch (tailcal.py)

In standard Mode F, the raw residual target obs - hres expands dramatically from Day 1 to Day 9, forcing one network to span wildly different error scales. The opt-in research candidate tempest/forecast/sigma.py (--sigma) reframes lead time as a known signal-decay and noise-scale schedule so the neural generator $F$ always predicts a standardized $O(1)$ target (obs - base) / s across every lead day $d \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ and column $c \in \{1, \dots, 176\}$:

Equation 3.3.5 — SIGMA Lead-as-Noise Decomposition & NEST Width Allocation $$\text{member}_{d,c} = \mu_{\text{clim}} + \beta_{d,c}\!\left(\text{hres} - \mu_{\text{clim}}\right) + s_{d,c}\,F, \qquad s_{d,c} = s_{\text{rel},d,c}\,\sigma_{\text{clim},m,c}\,\gamma_{\text{ens}}$$ $$w_d = \frac{\text{width}}{4}\,\mathrm{rint}\!\left(4 - 3\,\frac{s_d - s_{\min}}{s_{\max} - s_{\min}}\right)$$

Climatology $\mu_{\text{clim}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{clim}}$ are leak-free (year <= 2020); $\beta_{d,c} \in [0, 1.2]$ and $s_{\text{rel},d,c} \ge 0.05$ (S_FLOOR = 0.05) are tabled directly per lead day because Stage 1 showed 2-parameter exponential decay curves had the wrong shape. Under UR2 Stage-5 spread scaling (ens_scale=True), $\gamma_{\text{ens}}$ scales $s_{d,c}$ by each row's IFS ensemble spread relative to its fit-row median, clipped strictly to ENS_CLIP = (0.8, 1.5) (mapping < 500 m → surface, 500 m–2.5 km → 700 hPa, > 2.5 km → 500 hPa, skipping underground 850 hPa; RH keeps factor 1.0). On the UR15 third-period (2023–2024) test, SIGMA held within +0.5% at Days 1–7 and beat binned Mode F by 6.9–8.2% at Day 9 in both pilot boxes (remaining an opt-in candidate), whereas NEST width quantization failed 99% coverage.

Unwired / Killed Experiment — Far-Tail Stretch (tempest/forecast/tailcal.py, UR1 & X2)

tailcal.py was engineered to repair under-covering 99% ranges without touching the inner 90% core or Schaake rank order. Using order statistics $q_{05}, q_{95}$ ($k = 10$ for $m = 199$), it applied a strictly increasing outer-10% multiplier $x' = q_{05} - f_{\text{lo}}(q_{05} - x)$ for $x < q_{05}$ and $x' = q_{95} + f_{\text{hi}}(x - q_{95})$ for $x > q_{95}$ ($f \in [0.60, 3.00]$, step $0.02$, 1-2-1 lead-smoothed to hit TARGET = 0.005 per side). Despite clean monotone math, UR1 (b25) and X2 (b85) killed it: cross-year optimal stretch factors drifted by 0.38–0.995 across single validation years and still drifted by 0.26–0.77 when pooling two full years (2021+2022 tested on 2023–2024)—failing the pre-set stability gate of $\le 0.15$. Fitting extreme 0.5% tail multipliers on 1–2 years of weather overfits rare synoptic storms rather than generalizing.

3.3.4

Near-Term Multi-Model NWP Ensemble Ingestion

To feed medium-range regime F2 (Days 7–15) and operational forecast verification, tempest/nearterm/ensemble.py ingests daily-logged gzipped Open-Meteo snapshots (read_logged()) from two independent global ensemble systems: Google WeatherNext 2 (64 members) and ECMWF IFS 0.25° (51 members). Together they form a 115-member raw multi-model pool projected onto the exact canonical 44-height AGL grid—arrays of shape (M, T, 44) for u, v, T, RH plus (M, T) surface fields t2m, sp, rh2m—using pure physics interpolation with zero statistical post-processing.

Vertical Projection, Underground Masking & Log-Wind Anchoring

Every snapshot parses 13 standard pressure levels (LEVELS = [1000, 925, 850, 700, 600, 500, 400, 300, 250, 200, 150, 100, 50] hPa), converts temperatures to Kelvin and pressure to Pascals, decomposes wind speed/direction via meteorological vectorization $(u, v) = (-s\sin\theta,\, -s\cos\theta)$, and converts mixed-phase relative humidity to liquid-water RH via liquid_rh(). Member keys are sorted by _members() with key (k != var, k) so the unperturbed control member is always index 0, followed by *_member01..N (returning None if a variable is absent or all-null).

Equation 3.3.6 — Underground Pressure-Level Mask (ensemble.py:65) $$\text{above} = \bigl(p < p_{\text{sfc}} - 500\text{ Pa}\bigr) \;\wedge\; \bigl(z_{\text{AGL}} > 0\bigr), \qquad z_{\text{AGL}} = \mathrm{geometric\_height}(\Phi_{\text{gh}} \cdot g_0) - z_{\text{elev}}$$

Every level at or below terrain elevation—or within 5 hPa of surface pressure—is masked out before vertical interpolation. Because Open-Meteo ensemble feeds omit a 100 m wind anchor, LOG_TO_FIRST_LEVEL = True (AU-15 fix) interpolates wind in $\ln(z)$ all the way from the 10 m surface anchor to the first valid above-ground pressure level rather than switching to linear above 100 m, preventing an artificial boundary-layer kink. Temperature and RH prepend the 2 m surface anchor before calling interp_columns().

Confirmed Bug B5 — Underground 1000 hPa RH Fallback for WeatherNext 2 (tempest/nearterm/ensemble.py:70)

Open-Meteo does not serve relative_humidity_2m for Google WeatherNext 2, so rh2m evaluates to None. Line 70 falls back to: 

rh_anchor = rh2m if rh2m is not None else np.where(np.isfinite(rh[..., :1].squeeze(-1)), np.clip(rh[..., 0], 0, 100), np.nan)

Because LEVELS[0] = 1000 hPa, indexing rh[..., 0] reads the 1000 hPa field—which lies hundreds of metres underground at high-elevation launch sites such as Spaceport America (~1,400 m MSL, $p_{\text{sfc}} \approx 860\text{ hPa}$) and Denver (~1,600 m MSL, $p_{\text{sfc}} \approx 840\text{ hPa}$). Even though line 65 masks 1000 hPa out of agl, line 70 reads the unmasked rh array and injects subterranean humidity into both rh2m and the lowest boundary-layer anchor.

Clean fix: When rh2m is None, locate the lowest real atmospheric level via first_valid = np.argmax(above, axis=-1) and gather np.take_along_axis(rh, first_valid[..., None], axis=-1).squeeze(-1) (masked by above.any(-1)).

3.3.5

UR5 Climate Wind-Sequence Post-Processor (ur5.py & OPTIONS["ur5_sequence"])

Single-hour climate ensembles are calibrated to match marginal wind distributions, yet launch-window planning requires multi-hour coherent sequences (e.g., 3-to-6-hour holds across a launch morning via api.atmosphere_sequence()). In multi-hour climate sequences, two subtle artifacts appeared in earlier evaluations: (1) the tool's 10 m wind speed distribution drifted slightly from ERA5 across specific diurnal hours, and (2) standard Gaussian temporal lag coupling underestimated the strong persistence difference between calm nocturnal inversions and windy mixed-layer afternoons.

The UR5 / WD1 post-processor (tempest_weather/ur5.py, passed in b29 UR5 + b40 spread refit on 2026-11-15 and ported to serving in tempest_weather/api.py:28, 60–61, 650–659) fixes both issues cleanly. It is governed by OPTIONS["ur5_sequence"] = True (api.py:28, Shipped ON by default) and activates automatically whenever atmosphere_sequence() runs in pure climate mode (all(i["mode"] == "ai" for _, i in sets)) and the asset bundle includes post/ur5_* (exported via export_tool.py --post-ur5). Crucially, it runs only inside atmosphere_sequence()—never inside single-timestamp _post_process().

Exact Mathematics of the Four ur5.py Stages & the anchor=True Shear Discovery

At the nearest table grid point $p = \arg\min_k \big(( \text{lat}_k - \text{lat})^2 + (\text{lon}_k - \text{lon})^2\big)$ and each hour's (month, UTC hour) cell where T["ok"][p, m, h] is true, ur5.apply() executes three core operations before api.py fades the delta vertically:

  1. Direction-Preserving 100-Knot Speed Quantile Map (fit_map / apply_map): Maps the tool's 10 m speed $s = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}$ onto ERA5 1991–2005 speed quantiles tabulated at 100 knots QS = np.linspace(0.005, 0.995, 100) (monotonized via np.maximum.accumulate(xt + np.arange(100) * 1e-9), linear interpolation between knots, flat beyond). Scaling both components by $k = \operatorname{interp}(s, x_t, x_e) / \max(s, 10^{-6})$ preserves every member's exact wind direction.
  2. Component-Wise Spread Rescaling (scale, b40 refit): Rescales $u$ and $v$ around their hourly ensemble means using the fitted global pair $(s_u, s_v)$: $u' = \bar{u} + s_u(u - \bar{u})$ and $v' = \bar{v} + s_v(v - \bar{v})$.
  3. Two-Regime Gaussian Rank Chain (regime_rho & apply): Converts hourly 10 m speeds $w_t$ into normal scores $z_t = \Phi^{-1}\!\big((\operatorname{rank}(w_t) + 0.5)/n\big)$. Between consecutive valid hours separated by $0 < \Delta t \le 6\text{ h}$, the persistence correlation depends on whether the previous hour was calmer or windier than the ERA5 median $m_{p,m,h}$, decayed exponentially across multi-hour gaps:
Equation 3.3.7 — Two-Regime Rank Chain, Anchored Noise & 300 m Vertical Taper $$\rho_i = \begin{cases} \rho_{\text{calm}}^{\Delta t} & w_{t-1, i} < \mathrm{med}_{t-1} \\ \rho_{\text{windy}}^{\Delta t} & w_{t-1, i} \ge \mathrm{med}_{t-1} \end{cases}, \qquad z_t = \rho \odot z_{t-1} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \odot e_t$$ $$e_t^{\text{anchor}} = \frac{c_t - r(c_t, z_{t-1})\,z_{t-1}}{\max\!\big(\operatorname{std}(c_t - r z_{t-1}),\; 10^{-9}\big)}, \qquad \Delta u(z) = (u_{10}^{\text{ur5}} - u_{10})\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(1 - \frac{z - z_0}{300 - z_0},\; 0,\; 1\right)$$

Here $c_t$ is each member's own current-hour 10 m speed normal score and $r = \operatorname{corr}(c_t, z_{t-1})$. In production (anchor=True, api.py:653), using the orthogonalized residual $e_t^{\text{anchor}}$ instead of fresh Gaussian noise preserves the exact target lag correlation $\rho$ while minimizing member movement—and updating speed only keeps each member's original 10 m wind direction aligned with the column above it. Finally, api.py:654–657 tapers the 10 m adjustment linearly to zero at 300 m AGL, refreshes hydrostatic pressure/density/sound speed via _core.thermo_column(), and appends "sequence-10m-wind:ur5-wd1" to info["quality"].

Why anchor=True & the 300 m Taper Were Essential (Experiment b89)

In early integration testing (b89), blindly permuting only the 10 m wind vector with random chain noise (anchor=False) detached each member's surface wind direction from its 50 m and 100 m winds above—catastrophically spiking median 10–50 m wind shear from 18 m/s/km to 115 m/s/km. Switching to anchor=True (orthogonalized member rank noise + direction-preserving speed scaling) and fading the 10 m delta linearly out to 300 m completely eliminated the shear explosion while preserving exact hourly marginal speed sets and two-regime temporal persistence.

3.3.6

Graceful Degradation, Feature Flags & Offline Fallbacks

Launch pads in West Texas and New Mexico routinely experience spotty cellular backhaul, upstream Open-Meteo rate limits, or missing pressure levels over high terrain. Tempest-ARKNET is engineered so that no transient network outage or edge-case elevation can ever crash a flight-dispersion run or silently emit unflagged degraded data. Every fallback follows a strict degrade-and-tag contract: the engine steps down to the highest-fidelity locally verifiable backend and records an explicit machine-readable token in info["quality"].
