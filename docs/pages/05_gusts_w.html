Wind Gusts, Thermals & Vertical W

How Tempest-ARKNET couples finer-scale vertical and surface turbulence to the 179-D Engression column: uncensored 1-minute ASOS heteroscedastic Gumbel gusts, Deardorff/Lenschow convective thermals, PBLH & Bulk Richardson shear instability, and the conditional vertical-wind head (whead + wcalib).

Modules: gust/model.py, build_wdata.py, whead.py, thermals.pyGUST_PHYSICAL = Truewhead_a24 + wcalib
2.3.1

Why rockets care about gusts & updrafts

The primary 179-output Engression generator produces vertically coherent profiles of horizontal wind ($u, v$), temperature ($T$), and humidity across 44 heights above ground. Yet three finer-scale atmospheric motions outside that 179-D hourly mean vector directly govern launch-day safety and recovery dispersion:

  1. Peak 10 m surface wind gusts: A 5-second wind spike as the rocket clears the launch rail drives sudden weathercocking and can exceed pad Go/No-Go wind limits even when the 2-minute sustained average looks calm.
  2. Grid-resolved vertical wind ($w$): Synoptic ascent, mountain waves, and frontal lifting alter ascent airspeed and significantly extend or shorten main-parachute descent time and downrange drift.
  3. Sub-grid convective thermals: Narrow desert buoyant plumes (100 m–2 km wide, averaged out by ERA5’s 25 km grid) can hold a descending parachute aloft for minutes or jolt a slow-moving vehicle near apogee.

Tempest-ARKNET isolates each of these motions inside a dedicated, physically grounded side module so the validated 179-output backbone remains completely untouched while preserving sample-by-sample physical coupling:

The METAR censoring trap & why 1-minute ASOS data transformed gust accuracy

A naive gust model fitted on standard hourly airport METAR reports falls straight into a severe reporting bias. Under aviation reporting rules, an ASOS station only appends a gust group (GxxKT) when the peak wind exceeds the lull by at least 10 knots ($\ge 5.14\text{ m/s}$)—and never reports gusts below 14 knots. Training a statistical gust law on METAR strings is like measuring ocean waves only during hurricanes: every calm or moderate launch morning has its real 5-second wind spikes silently censored out of the record.

Why legacy METAR-fitted laws understated launch-morning gusts

Because an hour without a METAR gust group is encoded as gust = sustained wind, earlier METAR-convention laws learned a median gust factor of 1.00 at light and moderate winds—falsely telling the rocket simulator that a 4 m/s morning breeze is completely laminar. In reality, high-frequency anemometers measure a physical gust factor of 1.44 at 2–5 m/s, 1.40 at 5–8 m/s, 1.38 at 8–12 m/s, and 1.33–1.35 above 12 m/s. An even earlier two-stage prototype (“is a gust reported?” + excess) also broke whenever ERA5 ran slightly faster than the airport anemometer, leaving its 99% interval covering only 59% of truth (tempest/gust/model.py:4–6).

To eliminate censoring permanently, Tempest-ARKNET refitted both gust laws (W8d, scripts/gust_1min.py) directly on ASOS 1-minute records (2016–2020 at Truth or Consequences TCS and Midland MAF), defining the target as the physical gust: the highest 5-second peak recorded during the 10 minutes preceding each report, regardless of whether METAR printed a gust group. Setting GUST_PHYSICAL = False in tempest_weather/api.py restores the legacy METAR laws, but GUST_PHYSICAL = True ships on by default:

2.3.2

Gust factor & gust excess parameterization

Because a 5-second peak over a 10-minute window is the maximum of many turbulent eddies, extreme-value theory points directly to a Gumbel (Type I) distribution. Rather than assuming constant gust variance across calm mornings and stormy afternoons, tempest/gust/model.py fits a heteroscedastic Gumbel model whose location $\mu$ and log-scale $\ln\beta$ vary linearly with wind speed, seasonality, diurnal phase, and local terrain.

Design matrix & the critical UTC-hour gotcha

Given 10 m wind speed $w_s$ (m/s), day of year $d$, and UTC hour $h_{\text{UTC}}$, design() builds a 7-column core feature vector (plus optional station, past-date ERA5 gust, and F-26 boundary-layer stability columns):

Equation 2.3.1 · Heteroscedastic Gumbel Design Matrix (tempest/gust/model.py) $$X = \left[\, 1,\;\; w_s,\;\; \frac{w_s^2}{10},\;\; \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi d}{365.25}\right),\;\; \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi d}{365.25}\right),\;\; \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi h_{\text{UTC}}}{24}\right),\;\; \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi h_{\text{UTC}}}{24}\right) \,\right]$$
Optional columns: station appends a binary terrain proxy from station_flag(lat, lon) (1.0 nearer Spaceport America / Truth or Consequences TCS at 33.2369°N, 107.2672°W; 0.0 nearer Midland MAF at 31.9425°N, 102.2019°W). Past-date fg appends [fg, fg - ws] using ERA5’s fg10 field. Opt-in stab (F-26, default off) appends 500 m wind speed and scaled lapse rate (t2m - T_500m) / 5.0.

Critical Gotcha · UTC hour vs. local solar hour

Nearly every other module in tempest_weather operates in local solar time ((utc_hour + lon / 15) % 24), and tempest_weather_v2.Window uses IANA wall-clock timezones. In contrast, gust/model.py was fitted directly on the UTC hour of ASOS airport reports (utc_hour(t) = t.hour + t.minute / 60.0). Passing solar hour into design() or _gust_params() silently shifts the afternoon convective gust peak by roughly 7 hours in West Texas and New Mexico!

Regularized Gumbel NLL, inverse-CDF sampling & quadratic-runaway caps

Location $\mu$ and strictly positive scale $\beta$ are parameterized by linear predictors $\theta, \phi \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and fitted via scipy.optimize.minimize(..., method="L-BFGS-B") from method-of-moments intercepts ($\hat{\beta}_0 = s_g\sqrt{6}/\pi$, $\hat{\mu}_0 = \bar{g} - \gamma_E\hat{\beta}_0$ with Euler’s constant $\gamma_E = 0.5772156649$), applying L2 regularization (l2 = 1.0) strictly to the non-intercept weights $\theta_{1:}, \phi_{1:}$:

Equation 2.3.2 · Heteroscedastic Gumbel Parameters, NLL & Inverse-CDF Draw $$\mu = X\theta, \qquad \beta = \exp\!\big(\mathrm{clip}(X\phi,\, -5,\, 5)\big) \quad \text{(runtime physical law clips to } [-6,\, 4]\text{)}$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\theta, \phi) = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\ln\beta_i + z_i + e^{-z_i}\right] + \lambda_2\left(\|\theta_{1:}\|_2^2 + \|\phi_{1:}\|_2^2\right), \qquad z_i = \frac{g_i - \mu_i}{\beta_i}$$ $$G = \max\!\big(\mu - \beta\ln(-\ln u),\, 0\big) \;\;(\texttt{model.py}), \qquad G_{\text{rt}} = \max\!\big(\mu - \beta\ln(-\ln u),\, w_{10}\big) \;\;(\texttt{registry.py} / \texttt{ranking.py})$$
Physical invariant & numerical guard: Uniform draws $u$ are clipped to $[10^{-12},\, 1 - 10^{-12}]$ so $\ln(-\ln u)$ stays finite, and runtime clipping against the sample’s own sustained 10 m wind $w_{10}$ enforces $G_{\text{rt}} \ge w_{10}$ (a peak gust can never be slower than the sustained wind).

Because the design matrix contains a quadratic speed term $w_s^2 / 10$, extrapolating above the high-wind support of airport reports causes polynomial runaway—uncapped, the legacy METAR model-wind law mapped a 20 m/s storm wind to an impossible 59 m/s median gust, and the physical model-wind law climbed to a 2.3× gust factor at 20 m/s. Inside tempest_weather/api.py::_gust_physical, speeds above a cap are clamped to $w = \min(w_0, w_{\text{cap}})$ and both $\mu$ and $\beta$ are multiplied by $k = w_0 / w$, holding the gust factor constant into extreme storms:

Runtime Constant / Guard Value Physical Role in tempest_weather/api.py
GUST_SWITCH_WS 5.0 m/s AV13 speed switch (OPTIONS["gust_speed_switch"]): uses a region’s exported physical gust law when $w_s \ge 5.0\text{ m/s}$ and falls back to built-in GUST_PHYS_MODEL below 5 m/s (+1.09% cross-station gain).
GUST_PHYS_MODEL_CAP 9.0 m/s Upper cap on model 10 m wind for the physical 1-min law; above 9 m/s, $\mu$ and $\beta$ scale linearly with $w_0 / 9.0$ to prevent the positive $w_s^2$ weight (+0.9378) from blowing up.

In live tempest_weather runtime, atm["gust_mu"] and atm["gust_beta"] are 1-D (n,) 10 m arrays (also reused by flight/dryden.py to draw discrete 1-cos gust peaks).

2.3.3

Convective velocity scale $w_*$ & CAPE

ERA5’s 0.25° (~25 km) grid resolves large-scale synoptic ascent and mountain waves, but completely averages out 100 m–2 km desert convective thermals. On a hot afternoon at Spaceport America, buoyant plumes driven by intense solar heating can produce localized 2–4 m/s updrafts even when the 25 km grid-mean vertical velocity $w$ is nearly zero. While CAPE (Convective Available Potential Energy, J/kg) measures the deep, column-integrated buoyant fuel available to thunderstorms reaching the upper troposphere, dry boundary-layer thermals on clear launch days are governed by the Deardorff convective velocity scale $w_*$.

tempest/output/thermals.py implements a self-contained boundary-layer similarity model for sub-grid vertical turbulence (w_sigma, w_turb), driven by mixed-layer depth $z_i$ (ERA5 blh) and upward surface sensible heat flux $H$ (converted from ERA5’s downward-positive accumulated sshf via $H = -\mathrm{sshf} / (3600 \cdot \Delta t_{\text{hr}})$). Whenever solar heating produces upward heat flux ($H > 0$) inside a mixed layer ($z_i > 0$), $w_*$ sets the characteristic buoyant updraft speed (clamped to $0$ in stable air where $H \le 0$ or $z_i \le 0$):

Equation 2.3.3 · Deardorff Convective Velocity Scale & Lenschow (1980) Profile $$w_* = \left(\frac{g_0}{T}\,\frac{H}{\rho c_p}\,z_i\right)^{1/3} \qquad \big(g_0 = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2,\;\; c_p = 1004.0\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}\big)$$ $$\sigma_w^2(z) = 1.8\,w_*^2\!\left(\frac{z}{z_i}\right)^{2/3}\!\left(1 - 0.8\frac{z}{z_i}\right)^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \sigma_w(z) = \sqrt{1.8}\,w_*\!\left(\frac{z}{z_i}\right)^{1/3}\!\left(1 - 0.8\frac{z}{z_i}\right)$$
Physical intuition: Just as deep-convection updrafts scale with $\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{CAPE}}$, dry thermals scale with $(B_0 z_i)^{1/3}$, where $B_0 = \frac{g_0}{T}\frac{H}{\rho c_p}$ is the surface buoyancy flux. Inside the mixed layer ($0 < z < z_i$), $\sigma_w(z)$ starts at zero at the ground (impermeable surface), peaks in the lower-middle mixed layer, and decays toward the capping inversion at $z = z_i$ (dropping to $0$ or floor above $z_i$).

Spec typo caught & fixed in code (34% turbulence inflation avoided)

The written design spec (docs/specs/atmosphere-outputs.md) mistakenly wrote the standard deviation as $\sigma_w = 1.8\,w_*(z/z_i)^{1/3}(1 - 0.8\,z/z_i)$, forgetting the square root on 1.8. Because $1.8 / \sqrt{1.8} = \sqrt{1.8} \approx 1.3416$, implementing the spec verbatim would have inflated thermal turbulence by 34%. tempest/output/thermals.py:39 implements the exact variance-derived $\sqrt{1.8}$ factor.

Skewed updraft/downdraft sampling & why thermals.py stays unwired

Real desert thermals are strongly asymmetric—narrow, vigorous updrafts surrounded by broad, gentle downdrafts—and vertically coherent from the ground to $z_i$. In thermals.draw(sigma, rng, updraft_area=0.35, between_share=0.8, z_corr=0.8), each atmospheric sample receives a single column-wide regime ($a = 0.35$ updraft probability, $1 - a = 0.65$ downdraft probability) plus a vertically correlated normal fluctuation:

Equation 2.3.4 · Moment-Exact Skewed Thermal Mixture (thermals.py) $$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{f}{a(1 - a)}}, \qquad \mu_{\text{reg}} = \begin{cases} +(1 - a)\delta & \text{updraft (prob. } a = 0.35\text{)} \\ -a\delta & \text{downdraft (prob. } 1 - a = 0.65\text{)} \end{cases}$$ $$\varepsilon_h = \rho_z Z_{\text{col}} + \sqrt{1 - \rho_z^2}\,Z_h, \qquad w_{\text{therm}}(z_h) = \sigma_w(z_h)\!\left(\mu_{\text{reg}} + \sqrt{1 - f}\,\varepsilon_h\right)$$
Exact moment identities: By construction, $a\mu_{\text{up}} + (1-a)\mu_{\text{down}} = 0$ (exact zero mean) and $f + (1 - f) = 1$ (exact unit standardized variance, so $\mathrm{Std}(w_{\text{therm}}) = \sigma_w(z)$ at every level), with positive skewness and strong vertical correlation (z_corr = 0.8). thermals.py remains unwired in production (leaving w_sigma and w_turb as NaN placeholders) for two explicit reasons: (1) ERA5 sshf is absent from the Earthmover ARCO store and requires a separate Copernicus CDS download, and (2) the empirical parameters (0.35, 0.8, 0.8) still await calibration against high-resolution weather-balloon ascent rates (docs/agent/ASK_LATER.md).
2.3.4

Boundary-layer mixing height (PBLH)

The planetary boundary-layer height (PBLH), denoted $z_i$ (ERA5 variable blh), is the invisible thermodynamic ceiling separating turbulent surface-driven air from the smoother free troposphere aloft. For launch operations in high desert basins like Spaceport America, $z_i$ undergoes one of the most dramatic diurnal swings on Earth:

  • Pre-dawn nocturnal inversion ($z_i \approx 100\text{–}300\text{ m}$ AGL): Radiative cooling chills the desert floor overnight, creating a shallow, strongly stratified pool of cold air near the pad. Surface winds decouple from winds aloft, gust factors drop, and vertical mixing shuts off ($H \le 0 \Rightarrow w_* = 0$). This is why launch teams favor early-morning windows—while staying alert for sharp low-level wind shear right across the top of the shallow inversion.
  • Mid-afternoon convective mixed layer ($z_i \approx 2.5\text{–}4.5\text{ km}$ AGL): Intense desert insolation breaks the morning cap and pumps sensible heat flux $H$ upward, deepening $z_i$ to several kilometers. Differentiating the Lenschow profile $\sigma_w(z) \propto (z/z_i)^{1/3}(1 - 0.8\,z/z_i)$ shows that convective vertical turbulence peaks at $z / z_i = \frac{1}{4 \times 0.8} = 0.3125$—roughly one-third of PBLH—meaning an afternoon 3.2 km mixed layer places maximum thermal turbulence right near 1.0 km AGL, squarely inside the rocket’s powered ascent and main-chute deployment zone.
  • Interaction with terrain adjustment & gust stability: Because boundary-layer mixing erodes surface terrain temperature anomalies with altitude, to_site() tapers its surface elevation offset over a comparable 1.5 km scale height ($e^{-z/1500}$). Likewise, the opt-in F-26 stability extension in gust/model.py uses (t2m - T_500m) / 5.0 and 500 m wind speed as a direct proxy for whether afternoon boundary-layer mixing is actively coupling stronger momentum aloft down to the 10 m pad anemometer.
2.3.5

Bulk Richardson number & shear turbulence

How does the atmosphere decide whether a layer of wind shear stays smooth and laminar or breaks down into chaotic gusts and Kelvin–Helmholtz turbulence? The governing dimensionless ratio is the Bulk Richardson number ($Ri_b$), which pits buoyant stratification (static stability resisting vertical motion) against mechanical wind shear (kinetic energy trying to overturn the layer):

Equation 2.3.5 · Bulk Richardson Number & Critical Turbulence Threshold $$Ri_b = \frac{g_0}{\bar{\theta}_v}\,\frac{\Delta \theta_v\,\Delta z}{(\Delta u)^2 + (\Delta v)^2}, \qquad Ri_b < Ri_c \approx 0.25 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{dynamic shear instability}$$
Physical intuition: When the numerator is negative ($\Delta\theta_v < 0$, superadiabatic heating near the hot desert floor), buoyancy itself drives convective overturning ($Ri_b < 0$). When the air is stably stratified ($\Delta\theta_v > 0$), turbulence can still erupt whenever strong vertical wind shear $(\Delta u)^2 + (\Delta v)^2$ drives $Ri_b$ below the critical threshold $0.25$—transferring high-momentum air downward into sharp surface gusts and triggering localized vertical velocity fluctuations.

Converting ERA5 pressure velocity $\omega$ to geometric vertical wind $w$ (build_wdata.py)

While $Ri_b$ governs sub-grid shear breakdown, grid-scale vertical motion comes directly from ERA5’s hydrostatic primitive-equation archive. Global weather models solve in isobaric (pressure) coordinates rather than geometric height, so ERA5 archives pressure velocity $\omega = dp/dt$ (Pa/s) on 13 pressure levels rather than $w = dz/dt$. Because atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude, the sign is inverted: $\omega < 0$ means rising air (updraft) and $\omega > 0$ means sinking air (subsidence).

Using the hydrostatic balance $dp = -\rho g_0\,dz$, tempest/generator/build_wdata.py::omega_to_w converts interpolated $\omega$ into geometric vertical velocity $w$ (m/s, upward positive) using each row’s own hypsometrically consistent pressure $p$ and moist density $\rho$ from thermo.column:

Equation 2.3.6 · Hydrostatic Pressure-Velocity Conversion (omega_to_w) $$\omega = \frac{dp}{dt} \approx -\rho g_0 \frac{dz}{dt} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad w = -\frac{\omega}{\rho g_0} \qquad \big(g_0 = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2\big)$$
Ground-layer collapse over high terrain: At Spaceport America (~1,400 m MSL) or Denver (~1,600 m MSL), lower ERA5 pressure levels (1000 hPa, 925 hPa) lie underground. Any pressure level within 10 hPa of the surface or below it ($p_{\text{lev}} \ge p_s - 1000\text{ Pa}$) is collapsed onto the ground with $\omega = 0$ (flat-ground kinematic boundary condition), nudged by a tiny + np.arange(L) * 1e-3 tie-breaker to preserve strict monotonicity, and capped with an explicit surface anchor at $(p_s + 1\text{ Pa},\; \omega = 0)$. Linear interpolation in pressure then yields $\omega(z)$ on all 44 AGL heights. Rows dated before the ERA5 w archive starts (1990-10-29) are left as NaN.
2.3.6

Coupling W-models to the 179-D profile

Appending 44 vertical-wind targets directly onto the main Engression generator (expanding 179 → 223 outputs) would force a complete retraining of the primary backbone and risk degrading carefully tuned horizontal-wind and shear scores. Instead, tempest/output/whead.py implements a compact second conditional Engression network whose input is the concatenation [cond, phys] (F + 179 float32 features)—both the climate/time conditioning vector and the complete sampled physical column. Because $w$ is drawn conditionally on the sampled atmosphere, a calm winter dawn and a strongly sheared stormy afternoon automatically receive physically consistent vertical velocity without altering a single weight of the primary generator.

Calibration, sequence re-pairing & operational edge cases

  • Dedicated spread calibration (wcalib, F-27): Because whead trains on true ERA5 profiles but runs at inference on generated, range-calibrated profiles, its raw ensemble spread is slightly under-dispersed. scripts/whead_calib.py fits an M_CAL = 199 quantile range map on 2021–2023 generated inputs; applying wcalib in climate (ai) mode (by cycling rows into full blocks of 199) lifts 99% coverage (cov99) from 0.984 → 0.991 on held-out 2024–2025 test years.
  • Sequence & downrange re-pairing (_refresh_w): When atmosphere_sequence() (multi-hour trajectory) or atmosphere_downrange() (spatial corridor) reorders horizontal profiles across hours or kilometers via a Gaussian copula, _refresh_w() re-indexes atm["w"] to each reordered row’s nearest original atmosphere in standardized (u, v, T, RH) space so vertical motion never decouples from horizontal wind.
  • All-hours head & Bug B11 reminder: Legacy day-only heads (whead_clean.pt, whead.pt) emit a quality flag outside solar hours 06–21 h and have been superseded by the all-hours whead_a24 checkpoint. Note again Bug B11: build_wdata.py::row_times() calls FEATURES.index("year"), which raises ValueError on --features p2 datasets that replace "year" with "gmst".
