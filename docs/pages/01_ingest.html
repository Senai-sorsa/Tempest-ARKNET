1.1 Data Ingest & Spatial Alignment

How Tempest-ARKNET aligns five heterogeneous atmospheric streams—Earthmover ERA5, ARCO boundary-layer backfills, IGRAv2 radiosondes, live NWP feeds, and causal climate teleconnections—onto one canonical geographic lattice without temporal leakage.

1.1.1

Why multi-source alignment matters

Why this exists: No single meteorological archive gives a rocket 6-DOF flight simulator everything it needs. Global reanalyses like ERA5 deliver 35 continuous years of hourly 3-D fields on a regular grid, but their ~25 km cells smooth out razor-sharp nocturnal thermal inversions and convective pad gusts. Weather balloons (IGRAv2 radiosondes) capture true vertical wind shear down to tens of metres, but launch only twice per day at sparse airport and desert stations. Airport ASOS sensors record turbulent 10 m gusts every few minutes, but tell you nothing about the 12 km jet stream. Live Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) feeds narrow uncertainty in the final 1–16 days before launch, while ocean–atmosphere climate indices bias the seasonal dice months ahead.

Before Tempest-ARKNET can project a single vertical column or train a probabilistic neural generator, it must align all of these heterogeneous streams onto one shared geographic blueprint without leaking future information into historical training splits. Earlier prototypes scattered hard-coded West Texas / New Mexico bounds ($31^\circ\text{–}34^\circ\text{ N}$, $108^\circ\text{–}101^\circ\text{ W}$), grid counts ($13 \times 29 = 377$), row-major ordering, and balloon station IDs across dozens of scripts. Centralizing those coordinates into a single immutable Region dataclass in tempest/region.py (loaded from configs/region.toml) guarantees that data downloaders, nearest-neighbor lookups, balloon calibration, and out-of-domain checks agree on the exact same spatial lattice—and lets operators retarget a new launch corridor (such as the Denver box at $38^\circ\text{–}40^\circ\text{ N}$, $106^\circ\text{–}104^\circ\text{ W}$) by swapping one TOML file or setting the TEMPEST_REGION environment variable.

Canonical Lattice Dimensions & Index Mapping

A Region defines a uniform latitude–longitude lattice at ERA5’s native spacing $\Delta = 0.25^\circ$. For the default West Texas / New Mexico pilot box ($\phi_N = 34.0^\circ$, $\phi_S = 31.0^\circ$, $\lambda_W = -108.0^\circ$, $\lambda_E = -101.0^\circ$), the inclusive lattice dimensions are:

tempest/region.py — Inclusive Lattice Size $$n_{\text{lat}} = \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{\phi_N - \phi_S}{\Delta}\right) + 1 = 13, \qquad n_{\text{lon}} = \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_E - \lambda_W}{\Delta}\right) + 1 = 29, \qquad N_{\text{pts}} = n_{\text{lat}} \cdot n_{\text{lon}} = 377$$

Why this equation works: Both endpoints are included (hence $+1$), and round() eliminates IEEE-754 floating-point drift (such as $7.0 / 0.25 = 27.999999999999996$) that would otherwise silently drop the easternmost or southernmost boundary row when constructing Zarr slices.

Every local profile store lays out these $377$ grid points in row-major order starting from the north-west corner $(\phi_N, \lambda_W)$ with longitude varying fastest (np.repeat(la, n_lon), np.tile(lo, n_lat)), matching ERA5’s north-to-south latitude convention. Mapping any arbitrary launch pad coordinate $(\phi, \lambda)$ to its nearest stored grid point (Region.point_index) clips both axes into valid bounds before flattening:

tempest/region.py::Region.point_index — Nearest-Point Flattening $$i = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{\phi_N - \phi}{\Delta}\right),\, 0,\, n_{\text{lat}} - 1\right), \quad j = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{\lambda - \lambda_W}{\Delta}\right),\, 0,\, n_{\text{lon}} - 1\right), \quad \text{idx}(\phi, \lambda) = i \cdot n_{\text{lon}} + j$$

Why this equation works: Subtracting $\phi$ from $\phi_N$ preserves ERA5’s descending-latitude storage order ($i = 0$ at $34^\circ\text{ N}$, $i = 12$ at $31^\circ\text{ N}$), while clamping prevents an out-of-bounds pad coordinate from raising an index error or wrapping onto the opposite edge of the box.

Because human-authored TOML files store signed longitudes (degrees East, negative in the Western Hemisphere) whereas ERA5 Zarr archives index longitude on $[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$, Region.era5_lon360 wraps both endpoints modulo $360^\circ$:

tempest/region.py::Region.era5_lon360 — Longitude Convention Bridge $$\lambda_{360} = \lambda \bmod 360^\circ \implies (-108.0^\circ,\; -101.0^\circ) \longmapsto (252.0^\circ,\; 259.0^\circ)$$

Why this equation works: Performing the modulo conversion once inside the Region object guarantees that Earthmover Icechunk, ARCO-ERA5, and static terrain slices never suffer a silent sign mismatch that would download the Eastern Hemisphere ($101^\circ\text{–}108^\circ\text{ E}$, central China) instead of New Mexico and West Texas.

Operational Gotchas — Antimeridian & ASOS Dictionary Ordering

  • No Antimeridian Crossing: region.load() hard-asserts lon_west < lon_east. Rectangular boxes crossing $\pm 180^\circ$ are unsupported; the global model bypasses rectangular boxes entirely in favor of discrete station/point lists and spherical harmonics.
  • ASOS Dictionary Order Is Semantic: Reordering entries under [asos] in configs/region.toml silently changes the integer station flag encoded into the gust training dataset.
1.1.2

ERA5 reanalysis archive ingestion

Why this exists: Training a generative neural network to draw realistic 44-level atmospheres across every season, time of day, and ENSO phase requires hundreds of thousands of complete 3-D profiles. ERA5 (ECMWF Reanalysis v5) is the gold-standard 35-year hourly reconstruction of Earth’s atmosphere at $0.25^\circ$ spacing, but downloading it through the traditional Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) queues can take weeks. Tempest-ARKNET splits ERA5 ingestion across three fast, cloud-native pipelines—each chosen for a specific physical resolution and network bottleneck—and commits every file atomically via temporary .part paths so interrupted runs resume cleanly.

1. Earthmover Icechunk ERA5 — 13 Standard Pressure Levels & Surface (fetch_earthmover.py)

fetch_earthmover.py streams hourly ERA5 directly from Earthmover’s anonymous public S3 Icechunk store (earthmover-icechunk-era5, prefix icechunkV2, region us-east-1, Zarr v3, max_concurrent_requests=16). It retrieves 5 pressure-level variables (u, v, t, r, z across 13 standard levels from $1000\text{–}50\text{ hPa}$ in group pressure/temporal) and 7 single-level surface variables (u10, v10, u100, v100, t2m, d2m, sp in single/temporal), plus opt-in Phase 3 extras (fg10, blh, ssrd, slhf, ssr and pressure-level w via --only).

To avoid downloading any remote time chunk twice across chunk boundaries, subset() snaps the start index backward to the store’s native time-chunk boundary $t_c$ (landing in late 1990 for the default 1991-01-01T00 to 2025-12-31T23 span), drops static sdor/slor if bundled, and re-chunks pressure fields to pressure_level: 1 before writing <var>.zarr:

tempest/ingest/fetch_earthmover.py::subset — Chunk-Aligned Time Slicing $$i_0 = \left\lfloor \frac{\operatorname{searchsorted}(t,\, t_{\text{start}})}{t_c} \right\rfloor \cdot t_c, \qquad i_1 = \operatorname{searchsorted}_{\text{right}}(t,\, t_{\text{end}})$$

Why this equation works: Zarr/Icechunk object stores fetch whole compressed chunks from S3. If a slice starts mid-chunk, the client still pays full bandwidth to decompress the leading chunk and throws away the prefix; snapping $i_0$ down to a multiple of $t_c$ ensures 100% chunk alignment and zero wasted egress.

2. ARCO-ERA5 Boundary-Layer Backfill — 8 Extra Levels → 21 Total (arco_fill.py)

The 13 standard Earthmover levels ($1000, 925, 850, 700, 600, 500, 400, 300, 250, 200, 150, 100, 50\text{ hPa}$) leave wide vertical gaps inside the planetary boundary layer (PBL) where rockets experience their sharpest low-altitude wind shear and nocturnal jets. arco_fill.py backfills 8 intermediate boundary-layer pressure levels—[975, 950, 900, 875, 825, 800, 775, 750] hPa—from Google Cloud’s gcp-public-data-arco-era5 (ar/full_37-1h-0p25deg-chunk-1.zarr-v3), lifting total vertical resolution to $13 + 8 = 21$ pressure levels and writing monthly float32 NetCDF files (<yyyymm>.nc) shaped (time, isobaricInhPa, latitude, longitude).

Because ARCO stores specific humidity $q$ (kg/kg) rather than relative humidity (%), rh_from_q(q, t, p_pa) reproduces ECMWF’s exact IFS mixed-phase saturation formula (IFS Doc Part IV §7.2.1) with $\epsilon = R_d / R_v = 287.0597 / 461.5250 \approx 0.62198$, $T_0 = 273.16\text{ K}$, and $T_{\text{ice}} = 250.16\text{ K}$ so the 8 backfilled levels seamlessly match the 13 Earthmover levels before unified liquid-water conversion in Chapter 1.2:

tempest/ingest/arco_fill.py::rh_from_q — Exact IFS Mixed-Phase RH Reconstruction $$e = \frac{p_{\text{Pa}}\,q}{\epsilon + (1 - \epsilon)q}, \qquad e_s(T; a_3, a_4) = 611.21 \exp\!\left(\frac{a_3(T - T_0)}{T - a_4}\right)$$ $$\alpha(T) = \begin{cases} 1, & T \ge T_0 \\ 0, & T \le T_{\text{ice}} \\ \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\left(\dfrac{T - T_{\text{ice}}}{T_0 - T_{\text{ice}}}\right)^2,\, 0,\, 1\right), & T_{\text{ice}} < T < T_0 \end{cases}$$ $$e_{s,\text{mix}}(T) = \alpha(T)\,e_s(T; 17.502, 32.19) + \big(1 - \alpha(T)\big)\,e_s(T; 22.587, -0.7), \qquad \mathrm{RH} = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(100\frac{e}{e_{s,\text{mix}}(T)},\, 0,\, 100\right)$$

Why this equation works: Inverting $q = \epsilon e / (p - (1-\epsilon)e)$ recovers exact partial vapor pressure $e$, while the quadratic phase blend $\alpha(T)$ matches ECMWF’s native 13-level RH convention across the $-23^\circ\text{C}$ to $0^\circ\text{C}$ mixed-phase window. That prevents artificial humidity jumps between an Earthmover level (e.g., 850 hPa) and an adjacent ARCO level (e.g., 825 hPa).

Two Critical ARCO-ERA5 Engineering Gotchas

  • Network Locality (~370 GB/month raw read): ARCO chunks one global hour across all 37 levels (~85–120 MB/variable, ~0.5 GB/hour read). Always run arco_fill.py inside Google’s network (e.g., Colab with --source gs) so only the ~25 MB/month regional NetCDF slice ever leaves GCP.
  • Dask Memory Bomb (chunks=None): open_arco() intentionally passes chunks=None to xr.open_zarr. Passing chunks={"time": 1} upfront builds Dask task graphs for all 273 archive variables and consumes ~5.9 GB of RAM before reading a single byte.

3. Six Global Static Terrain Invariants (fetch_statics.py) & All-Hours Surface Store

Latitude and longitude alone cannot tell a neural generator whether a launch pad sits on flat plains or leeward of a steep mountain ridge—sub-grid terrain physics can. fetch_statics.py downloads six raw Blosc-compressed Zarr v2 chunks directly from ARCO-ERA5 via Python stdlib urllib + numcodecs.Blosc (zero fsspec/aiohttp overhead) at timestamp 2020-01-01T00 (indexed as integer hours since 1900-01-01T00), saving six (721, 1440) float32 global grids (lat: 90 → -90, lon: 0 → 359.75, <100 MB compressed) into era5_invariants.npz. In parallel, tempest/profiles/surface_hourly.py builds a point-aligned 24-hour surface Zarr (t2m, u10, v10, fg10, blh, ssrd, ssr, slhf) for thermal soak and gust modeling, converting ERA5 hourly accumulated energy fluxes (J/m²) into mean power (W/m²) by dividing by 3600.0.

1.1.3

IGRAv2 radiosonde ground truth

Why this exists: ERA5 is a model-data blend, not direct ground truth at a launch pad. Its ~25 km grid cells smooth out sharp morning inversions, under-represent peak jet-stream wind shear, and carry systematic cold/dry biases over arid desert basins. Twice every day at 00Z and 12Z, NOAA launches real helium weather balloons (radiosondes) that measure temperature, humidity, pressure, and GPS wind drift all the way from the surface to ~30–35 km burst altitude. Tempest-ARKNET uses NOAA NCEI’s Integrated Global Radiosonde Archive v2 (IGRAv2) as the physical answer key for both station bias calibration (Stage 3.1) and strict out-of-sample verification (Stage 3.4), paired with airport ASOS/METAR observations for turbulent 10 m gusts.

Vectorized 52-Byte Parsing & 81-Level Extended Altitude Grid

fetch_igra.py downloads IGRA v2.2 derived-POR archives (*-drvd.txt.zip) for pilot stations USM00072364 (Santa Teresa NM) and USM00072265 (Midland TX). For multi-station global ingestion, igra_to_heights.py parses plain *-data.txt.zip archives in vectorized 52-byte fixed-width ASCII blocks (S52 → uint8 NumPy view), maps any sentinel value $\le -8888$ to NaN, falls back to release hour rel // 100 whenever a sounding header reports hour 99, and projects every ascent onto the 81-level extended altitude ladder EXT_HEIGHTS:

  • Levels 0–43 (44 model heights): 10 m to 18,000 m AGL (exact match to canonical HEIGHTS).
  • Levels 44–55 (12 upper-stratosphere levels): 19,000 m to 30,000 m every 1 km.
  • Levels 56–80 (25 mesosphere levels): 32,000 m to 80,000 m every 2 km (remain NaN above balloon burst at ~30–35 km; filled later by ERA5/ISA when needed).

Hypsometric Gap-Filling for Significant Pressure Levels

Plain IGRAv2 data records omit geopotential height on many “significant” thermodynamic pressure levels (where temperature or humidity bends sharply between mandatory levels). Dropping those levels would erase the exact inversions we need balloons to capture. Instead, heights_gph() interpolates temperature $T$ linearly in $-\ln p$, computes virtual temperature $T_v$ with Magnus vapor pressure ($R_d = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, $\epsilon = 0.622$, $T_c = T - 273.15$), integrates the hypsometric thickness upward, and pins the resulting curve to every reported height via linear offset interpolation in $-\ln p$:

tempest/ingest/igra_to_heights.py::heights_gph — Hypsometric Gap-Fill & Anchoring $$e = \frac{\mathrm{RH}}{100} \cdot 611.2 \exp\!\left(\frac{17.67\,T_c}{T_c + 243.5}\right), \qquad T_v = T\!\left(1 + 0.61\,\epsilon\,\frac{e}{p}\right)$$ $$\Delta z_k = \frac{R_d}{g_0}\cdot\frac{T_{v,k} + T_{v,k-1}}{2}\cdot(\ln p_{k-1} - \ln p_k), \qquad z_{\text{fill}} = z_h + \operatorname{interp}_{-\ln p}\!\big(z_{\text{rep}} - z_h\big)$$

Why this equation works: Integrating $T_v$ in $-\ln p$ recovers the physically curved altitude profile between mandatory levels, while adding the interpolated residual $(z_{\text{rep}} - z_h)$ guarantees exact zero-error passage through every reported balloon altitude without accumulating drift over 30 km of ascent.

Heights are then converted to geometric metres above the launch surface ($h = z_{\text{geom}}(z \cdot g_0) - z_{\text{geom}}(z_{i_0} \cdot g_0)$ using the first surface level lt2 == 1), meteorological wind speed $s$ and FROM-direction $\theta$ are decomposed into Cartesian velocity components $u = -s\sin\theta$ and $v = -s\cos\theta$, and missing RH is reconstructed via magnus_rh(tk, tk - dpd/10).

Compact int16 Quantization & ASOS Gust Complement

To store decades of global balloon soundings in compact .npz archives with negligible physical loss ($\pm 0.05\text{ m/s}$, $\pm 0.05\text{ K}$, $\pm 0.05\%\text{ RH}$), enc() quantizes every profile to signed 16-bit integers with sentinel MISSING = np.int16(-32768):

tempest/ingest/igra_to_heights.py::enc — Signed 16-Bit Quantization $$q_x = \operatorname{round}(10 \cdot x) \in \text{int16} \;\;(x \in \{u, v, T, \mathrm{RH}\}), \qquad q_{\ln p} = \operatorname{round}\!\big(2000 \cdot \ln p_{\text{Pa}}\big) \in \text{int16}, \qquad \text{NaN} \leftrightarrow -32768$$

Why this equation works: Storing $2000 \ln p_{\text{Pa}}$ instead of raw pressure gives uniform $0.05\%$ relative pressure precision from 101,325 Pa at sea level all the way up to 10 Pa in the mesosphere, fitting comfortably inside the $[-32767, +32767]$ int16 dynamic range while cutting storage footprint by 50% vs. float32.

On read, load() restores float32 arrays via q / 10.0 and p = exp(q_lnp / 2000.0). Alongside IGRAv2 upper-air truth, fetch_asos.py downloads airport ASOS/METAR observations (sknt, gust, drct, tmpf, report_type=3, 4, tz=Etc/UTC) from Iowa Environmental Mesonet (mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/asos.py) for MAF (Midland International) and TCS (Truth or Consequences, ~30 km from Spaceport America) across 2000–2025, sleeping 8 seconds between files with up to 6 linear-backoff retries ($30(k+1)\text{ s}$).

Critical ASOS Censoring Semantics — Empty Gust Is NOT Missing Data

ASOS stations only report a gust when the peak 5-second wind exceeds the lull by 10 knots or more (with peak ≥ 18 kt in standard US METAR rules). An empty gust column therefore means “no reportable gust occurred” (left-censored below threshold), not a broken sensor. Treating blanks as NaN and dropping them would throw away calm days and severely over-predict launch-pad wind gusts; instead, the heteroscedastic Gumbel gust head (tempest/gust/model.py) explicitly models censored likelihoods.

Live NWP feeds (HRRR, GFS, GEFS, IFS)

Why this exists: Historical ERA5 + climate drivers tell you the honest statistical distribution of a launch date 6 months away, but once a launch enters the 16-day forecast window, live physics-based Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models actually know where individual troughs, cold fronts, and jet streaks are right now. Uniform Projection of Live NWP Ensembles onto 44 AGL Levels

When tempest/nearterm/ensemble.py::to_profiles() parses an Open-Meteo ensemble JSON response, it converts temperatures from Celsius to Kelvin ($+273.15$), surface pressure from hPa to Pa ($\times 100$), and meteorological wind speed/direction into $(u, v)$. Crucially, it applies liquid_rh() to IFS pressure-level humidity so upper-air RH matches balloon ground truth, drops any isobaric level underground or within $500\text{ Pa}$ ($5\text{ hPa}$) of the surface pressure, and interpolates winds between 10 m and the lowest valid pressure level in $\ln z$ (LOG_TO_FIRST_LEVEL = True, AU-15):

tempest/nearterm/ensemble.py:64–65 — NWP Above-Ground Level Mask $$z_{\text{AGL}} = z_{\text{geom}}(Z_{\text{gh}} \cdot g_0) - z_{\text{elev}}, \qquad \text{keep}(p, z_{\text{AGL}}) \iff \big(100\,p_{\text{hPa}} < p_s - 500\text{ Pa}\big) \;\land\; \big(z_{\text{AGL}} > 0\big)$$

Why this equation works: A pressure level sitting just 1–4 hPa above the surface pressure can land only 10–30 m above ground with noisy boundary-layer extrapolation, creating a violent artificial wind-shear spike right above the 10 m anchor. Requiring at least a 5 hPa clearance above $p_s$ keeps the transition from 10 m surface wind to free-atmosphere pressure levels smooth.

HRRR Near-Analysis Taper & Independent Station Reality Check

When a user invokes Launch.observe_hrrr(), tempest_weather_v2/launch.py::_apply_hrrr shifts the ensemble median toward HRRR’s 10 m wind and narrows spread by $0.8\times$, tapering both effects linearly to zero between $z_0 = 10\text{ m}$ and $z_{\text{top}} = 300\text{ m}$:

tempest_weather_v2/launch.py::_apply_hrrr — Linear Vertical Taper $$\tau(z) = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(1 - \frac{z - z_0}{300 - z_0},\; 0,\; 1\right), \qquad u'(z) = \tilde{u} + (u - \tilde{u})\big(1 + (0.8 - 1)\tau(z)\big) + 1.0\cdot(u_{\text{HRRR}} - \tilde{u})\tau(z)$$

Why this equation works: HRRR’s 3 km terrain grid was only validated at 10 m AGL. Tapering linearly to zero at 300 m ensures the surface wind adjustment blends seamlessly into the upper-air profile without disturbing mid-level jet structure.

Scientific Honesty Check (B16 / USCRN Independent Verification)

On 240 airport ASOS cases (2024–25 at TCS and MAF), HRRR 10 m wind scored CRPS 1.04 vs. 1.17 for past-mode ERA5 (and 0.76 blended). However, an independent 2026-11-08 verification at USCRN Las Cruces 20 N—a desert basin station that HRRR does not assimilate—showed no advantage over ERA5 (correlation 0.839 vs. 0.841, compared to 0.873 vs. 0.737 at assimilated airport TCS). That is why observe_hrrr() is strictly opt-in and appends an explicit trust caveat reminding operators not to expect near-analysis gains at remote unassimilated launch pads.

Resilient Caching & Stale-Cache Fallback: Every openmeteo.get() call hashes its canonical URL via SHA-256 (32-char hex filename in data/cache/openmeteo/) and writes atomically via .part + os.replace(). If the network fails or Open-Meteo returns an API error when a cached file exists, Tempest gracefully returns the last good cached JSON tagged with doc["_stale"] = True instead of crashing a launch-morning script. In addition, passing elevation=z_site (AU-3) prevents Open-Meteo from silently downscaling t2m and sp to its internal 90 m DEM elevation.

1.1.5

Climate teleconnection drivers (ONI, QBO, WWV)

Why this exists: Deterministic NWP skill evaporates beyond ~10–14 days, yet sounding-rocket and IREC campaigns are planned 3 to 12 months in advance. Slow-moving ocean–atmosphere teleconnections act like tilted boundary conditions on the regional wind distribution: El Niño / Southern Oscillation (oni, wwv) shifts the subtropical jet stream southward across the US Southwest and strengthens winter–spring upper-level wind shear; the stratospheric Quasi-Biennial Oscillation (qbo30, qbo50) reverses equatorial stratospheric winds every ~28 months and modulates mid-latitude wave guides; and Global Mean Surface Temperature (gmst) tracks secular warming—replacing a naive linear year counter so the generator extrapolates physically into future decades.

fetch_indices.py refreshes all five monthly series on every run (refresh=True): NOAA CPC oni.ascii.txt (3-month seasons DJF..NDJ assigned to their center month), CPC qbo.u30.index and qbo.u50.index (fixed-width 7-char columns in the "ORIGINAL DATA" block, -999.9 missing), NOAA PMEL wwv.dat (Warm Water Volume anomaly in $\text{m}^3$), and NASA GISS GLB.Ts+dSST.csv ("***" missing).

No-Leakage 60-Month Trailing GMST & 3-Month Lagged ONI

A centered moving average of global temperature would leak future warming into past training years. Instead, indices.gmst_table() computes a strictly causal 60-month trailing mean shifted forward by exactly one month so every month $t$ sees only observations strictly prior to month $t$:

tempest/ingest/indices.py::gmst_table & oni_lag — Causal Climate State $$\mathrm{GMST}_t = \frac{1}{60}\sum_{k=1}^{60} g_{t - k}, \qquad \mathrm{oni\_lag3}(t) = \mathrm{ONI}(t - 3\text{ months})$$

Why this equation works: In pandas, s.rolling(60, min_periods=60).mean().shift(1) eliminates both short-term ENSO surface-temperature spikes and any lookahead bias. Meanwhile, lagging ONI by 3 months captures the physical Rossby-wave propagation delay from equatorial Pacific sea-surface anomalies to mid-latitude jet-stream shear over New Mexico and West Texas. The month immediately after the last observed GISTEMP row is appended explicitly, and future query dates carry forward the last known value via searchsorted(months, side="right") - 1.

Joint 6-Lag Ridge Climate Index Forecaster (index_forecast.py)

When simulating a launch 6 months from today, future ONI, WWV, and QBO values are unknown. Drawing each driver independently destroys physical dynamics: equatorial Pacific warm-water recharge (wwv) leads El Niño (oni) by 6–9 months, and stratospheric QBO winds propagate downward from 30 hPa to 50 hPa. index_forecast.py models all four drivers simultaneously as a regularized linear dynamical system conditioned on the last LAGS = 6 months plus the seasonal cycle ($4 \times 6 + 2 = 26$ predictors), restricting training origins strictly to org = np.arange(6, pos - h + 1) so every target $Y_{t+h}$ is observed on or before origin:

tempest/ingest/index_forecast.py::_design & fit — Standardized Joint Ridge $$X_t = \Big[\, c_{t-\ell} \;\text{for } c \in \{\text{oni}, \text{qbo30}, \text{qbo50}, \text{wwv}\},\; \ell \in \{0..5\},\;\; \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi m_t}{12}\right),\;\; \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi m_t}{12}\right) \,\Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{26}$$ $$Z = \frac{X - \mu_X}{\sigma_X + 10^{-9}}, \qquad Y_s = \frac{Y - \mu_Y}{\sigma_Y + 10^{-12}}, \qquad \hat{\beta} = \left(Z^\top Z + \frac{\lambda_{\text{ridge}} \cdot N}{100}\, I_k\right)^{-1} Z^\top Y_s, \qquad R = Y_s - Z\hat{\beta}$$

Why this equation works: Standardizing both $X$ and $Y$ eliminates a fatal $10^{13}$ scale singularity—Warm Water Volume (wwv) is in cubic metres ($\mathcal{O}(10^{13})$) while oni and qbo are $\mathcal{O}(1\text{–}10)$. Scaling the Ridge penalty ($\lambda_{\text{ridge}} = 3.0$) by $N/100$ keeps regularization strength invariant as the archive grows each year.

To sample correlated multi-driver forecast shocks without triggering floating-point non-PSD errors, _psd_cov() eigendecomposes the residual correlation matrix $C = V\operatorname{diag}(w)V^\top$, floors eigenvalues at $10^{-6}$, re-normalizes the diagonal to $1$, and rescales by residual standard deviations $\sigma_R$. For P2_FEATURES, which needs both $\mathrm{ONI}_h$ and $\mathrm{ONI}_{h-L}$ ($L = 3$ months earlier) for the same Monte Carlo trajectory, draw_lag() (AU-16) returns the exact observed constant when $h - L \le 0$ and otherwise samples conditionally on the already-drawn $\mathrm{ONI}_h$ using the aligned hindcast residual correlation $\rho$:

tempest/ingest/index_forecast.py::_psd_cov, draw & draw_lag (AU-16) $$\tilde{C} = V \operatorname{diag}\!\big(\max(w,\, 10^{-6})\big) V^\top, \qquad \hat{C}_{ij} = \frac{\tilde{C}_{ij}}{\sqrt{\tilde{C}_{ii}\tilde{C}_{jj}}}, \qquad \Sigma_R = \hat{C} \odot \big(\sigma_R \sigma_R^\top\big)$$ $$Y^{(i)} = \mu_Y + \sigma_Y \odot \mathcal{N}\!\left(z_{\text{org}}\hat{\beta},\; \Sigma_R\right), \qquad \mathrm{ONI}_{h-L} \mid \mathrm{ONI}_h = \mu_g + \sigma_g\!\left(\rho\,\frac{\mathrm{ONI}_h - \mu_f}{\sigma_f} + \sqrt{\max(1 - \rho^2,\, 0)}\,\varepsilon\right)$$

Why this equation works: On 2012–2025 hindcasts (scripts/index_bakeoff.py), this joint Ridge forecaster beat both the $k = 8$ historical analog baseline (indices.analog) and climatology at every horizon from 1 to 12 months—achieving ONI mean CRPS 0.342 vs. 0.424 (analog) vs. 0.457 (climatology)—and naturally widens back to climatology beyond 12 months as predictability decays.

Ablation Note — Why qbo_phase=False Stays Off (AU-8)

An experimental flag qbo_phase=True appends polar QBO coordinates [sin(theta), cos(theta), amp] from causal _qbo_sd(tab, pos). On hindcast testing it worsened ONI CRPS by +2–8% and QBO CRPS by +1–4% due to extra predictor degrees of freedom, so it remains disabled by default.

1.1.6

Base 13 vs. P2 16 feature schemas

Why this exists: Every atmospheric sample generated by Tempest-ARKNET is conditioned on a compact numeric feature vector describing where the pad is, when in the solar year and day the launch occurs, and what state the large-scale climate system is in. Using a single shared function (tempest/generator/features.py::features()) for both training dataset assembly and live inference guarantees zero train/serve feature drift. Over development, Tempest evolved from its original 13-feature baseline (BASE_FEATURES) to an upgraded 16-feature climate-aware schema (P2_FEATURES) that fixes linear time extrapolation and asymmetric El Niño / La Niña jet responses, plus a $4\pi$-normalized spherical harmonic basis (tempest/geo/sh.py) for global scaling.

Idx BASE_FEATURES (13) P2_FEATURES (16) Physical Meaning & Transform
0–3 lat, lon, elev_site, elev_grid lat, lon, elev_site, elev_grid Pad coordinates (deg) and site vs. ERA5 cell elevation (m). Note: ERA5 training rows have elev_site == elev_grid.
4–7 doy_sin, doy_cos, hour_sin, hour_cos doy_sin, doy_cos, hour_sin, hour_cos Cyclic seasonal ($\frac{2\pi\cdot\text{doy}}{365.25}$) and solar-hour ($\frac{2\pi\cdot\text{hr}}{24}$) harmonics—no Dec 31 / midnight jump.

tempest/generator/features.py:34–38 — Cyclic Time & Asymmetric ENSO Hinges $$a = \frac{2\pi\,\text{doy}}{365.25}, \quad h = \frac{2\pi\,\text{solar\_hour}}{24.0}, \qquad \text{oni\_pos} = \max(\text{ONI},\, 0), \quad \text{oni\_neg} = \min(\text{ONI},\, 0)$$

Why this equation works: El Niño ($\text{ONI} > 0$) and La Niña ($\text{ONI} < 0$) do not push the Southwestern jet stream symmetrically—strong El Niño winters dramatically amplify upper-level westerlies over New Mexico, while La Niña shifts storm tracks north. Splitting ONI into two ReLU hinges lets even the linear climate-trend bypass head learn distinct positive and negative slopes immediately.

Global Geodesy: $4\pi$-Normalized Real Spherical Harmonics (tempest/geo/sh.py)

Raw (lat, lon) coordinates work inside a regional pilot box, but fail globally due to two topological defects: an antimeridian seam discontinuity (jumping $+180^\circ \to -180^\circ$) and polar metric pinching ($1^\circ$ of longitude shrinks as $\cos\phi \to 0$ near $\pm 90^\circ$). Real spherical harmonics $Y_\ell^m(\phi, \lambda)$ are the exact smooth Fourier basis on the sphere $S^2$. Given $c = \sin\phi$ and $s = \cos\phi$, sh(lat, lon, L) evaluates the geodesy $4\pi$-normalized associated Legendre functions $\bar{P}_{\ell,m}(c)$ (satisfying $\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2} Y_i Y_j\,d\Omega = \delta_{ij}$, with $\sqrt{2}$ for $m > 0$ folded into $\bar{P}_{m,m}$):

tempest/geo/sh.py::sh — 4π-Normalized Associated Legendre Recurrence $$\bar{P}_{0,0} = 1, \qquad \bar{P}_{m,m} = \begin{cases} \sqrt{3}\,s\,\bar{P}_{0,0}, & m = 1 \\ \sqrt{\dfrac{2m + 1}{2m}}\,s\,\bar{P}_{m-1,m-1}, & 2 \le m \le L \end{cases}, \qquad \bar{P}_{m+1,m} = \sqrt{2m + 3}\,c\,\bar{P}_{m,m}$$ $$a_{\ell,m} = \sqrt{\frac{4\ell^2 - 1}{\ell^2 - m^2}}, \qquad b_{\ell,m} = \sqrt{\frac{(\ell - 1)^2 - m^2}{4(\ell - 1)^2 - 1}}, \qquad \bar{P}_{\ell,m} = a_{\ell,m}\!\left(c\,\bar{P}_{\ell-1,m} - b_{\ell,m}\,\bar{P}_{\ell-2,m}\right)$$ $$\operatorname{sh}(\phi, \lambda, L) = \Big[\, \bar{P}_{0,0},\;\; \bar{P}_{1,0},\; \bar{P}_{1,1}\cos\lambda,\; \bar{P}_{1,1}\sin\lambda,\;\; \dots,\;\; \bar{P}_{L,L}\sin(L\lambda) \,\Big] \in \mathbb{R}^{(L+1)^2}$$

Why this equation works: Every feature has unit variance over the sphere (ideal for neural network initialization), zero seam cliffs, and zero polar singularities. Initializing P[(0, 0)] = c * 0 + 1 inherits input shape, dtype, and PyTorch/NumPy backend automatically via _ns(lat) so GPU training and pure-NumPy serving execute bit-identical geodesy math.

Positional Normalization Guardrail (sample.py:145–147)

Because conditioning normalization is positional, passing columns in an unexpected order produces plausible-looking but silently wrong weather! features.set_active(cond_names) mutates FEATURES[:] in place from the dataset metadata, and Sampler explicitly asserts that self.cond_names matches either BASE_FEATURES or P2_FEATURES verbatim.

Quality control, sentinels & leakage guards

Why this exists: In atmospheric machine learning, a single leaked future timestamp, unmasked sentinel value (like -9999 interpreted as a $-999.9^\circ\text{C}$ temperature), or half-written file can silently inflate validation metrics while wrecking real launch-day forecasts. Tempest-ARKNET embeds hard runtime assertions, sentinel filters, and atomic filesystem commits directly into every ingest script rather than relying on manual operator discipline.
